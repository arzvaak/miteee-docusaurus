# Extra Practice

These are newly generated exam-style questions for self-assessment. They cover the full syllabus and are designed to test understanding beyond what has appeared in past papers.

## CO1 — Energy Fundamentals

1. A country has proven coal reserves of 100 billion tonnes and annual coal consumption of 2 billion tonnes. New reserves of 20 billion tonnes are discovered, and consumption grows at 3% per year. Estimate the reserve life before and after the discovery, assuming constant consumption. Then discuss why the R/P ratio is a dynamic metric.

2. Explain the difference between primary, secondary, commercial, and non-commercial energy with two examples of each. Why might solar energy be classified differently depending on whether it is grid-connected or used in a standalone home system?

3. A developing nation has per capita energy consumption of 0.5 tonnes of coal equivalent while a developed nation has 5 tonnes. If the developing nation's population is 1.4 billion and the developed nation's is 300 million, calculate total energy consumption for each and the ratio of their aggregate consumption.

## CO1 — Advanced Conversion Technologies

4. An MHD generator has a duct of height 0.5 m, width 0.2 m, and the plasma velocity is 800 m/s in a magnetic field of 4 T. Calculate the open-circuit voltage. If the internal resistance is 0.01 Ω and the external load is 0.09 Ω, find the power delivered to the load.

5. Compare FBC, MHD, thermionic, and fuel cell technologies on the basis of: (a) whether they have moving parts, (b) typical efficiency range, (c) current commercial readiness, and (d) primary fuel/input type.

## CO2 — Solar Energy

6. At latitude 20° N on March 21 (equinox), calculate: (a) the declination angle, (b) the sunrise hour angle, (c) the day length, and (d) the angle of incidence on a surface tilted at 30° facing south at solar noon.

7. A flat-plate collector has area 2 m², $F_R tau alpha = 0.80$, $F_R U_L = 4.0$ W/m²°C, solar irradiance 800 W/m², inlet temperature 30°C, and ambient temperature 25°C. Calculate the useful heat gain and the collector efficiency.

8. A PV module consists of 36 cells in series. Each cell has $I_"SC" = 3.5$ A, $V_"OC" = 0.6$ V, and the module fill factor is 0.72. Calculate the module's maximum power point voltage, current, and power. If the module area is 0.5 m² and irradiance is 1000 W/m², what is the module efficiency?

9. A stand-alone PV system must power a load of 5 kWh/day. Peak sun hours are 5 h/day. System efficiency (charge controller × battery × inverter) is 0.78, and the performance ratio is 0.72. How many 250 Wp modules are needed?

## CO3 — Wind Energy

10. Two anemometers are placed at 30 m and 80 m height at the same site in open terrain ($alpha = 0.14$). If the wind speed at 30 m is 7 m/s, calculate the speed at 80 m. Then calculate the ratio of available wind power at 80 m versus 30 m for the same rotor swept area.

11. A wind turbine has a rotor diameter of 100 m. At a wind speed of 12 m/s and air density 1.2 kg/m³, calculate: (a) the total wind power, (b) the mechanical power extracted if $C_p = 0.42$, (c) the electrical output if drivetrain efficiency is 94% and generator efficiency is 96%, and (d) the required rotor RPM if optimum TSR is 7.

12. Explain why variable-speed wind turbines with DFIG or PMSG generators produce more energy than fixed-speed turbines, even when both have the same rated power. Include the concept of the $C_p$-lambda curve in your answer.

13. A wind farm has 50 turbines, each rated at 2 MW. The average wind speed gives a capacity factor of 0.32. Calculate the annual energy production. If the farm must supply a continuous load of 25 MW, what fraction of the load can it meet, and what storage or backup capacity is needed?

## CO4 — Biomass

14. A community biogas plant processes cattle dung from 200 cows. Each cow produces 8 kg dung/day (18% dry matter). The biogas yield is 0.3 m³/kg dry mass. Retention time is 40 days. Slurry density is 1050 kg/m³. Calculate: (a) daily biogas production, (b) digester volume, (c) daily energy available if methane fraction is 0.65, burner efficiency is 60%, and methane CV is 28 MJ/m³.

15. Compare updraft and downdraft gasifiers in terms of: tar content, gas exit temperature, fuel flexibility, typical application, and thermal efficiency. For each, explain why the tar content has the value it does, relating it to the flow path of pyrolysis gases.

16. A biomass gasification plant processes 500 kg/h of wood chips (CV 15 MJ/kg). The gasifier has a cold gas efficiency of 70%. The syngas fuels an engine with 38% efficiency. Calculate: (a) the syngas energy output, (b) the engine electrical output in kW, and (c) the overall system efficiency from biomass to electricity.

## CO5 — Geothermal and Ocean Energy

17. Compare dry steam, flash steam, and binary cycle geothermal plants in terms of: resource temperature requirement, working fluid, emissions, corrosion risk, and geographic applicability. For which type is binary cycle preferred and why?

18. A closed-cycle OTEC plant operates between surface water at 27°C and deep water at 5°C. The ammonia turbine generates 5 MW gross. Pumping power is 30% of gross. The heat exchanger effectiveness requires a minimum temperature difference of 3°C at each exchanger. Calculate the Carnot efficiency, the actual net power output, and discuss whether this plant is economically viable.

19. A tidal power plant has a basin area of 2 km² and a mean tidal range of 8 m. Calculate the energy available per tide and the average power if generation occurs over 5 hours with 65% efficiency. How does this change if a minimum head of 2 m is required (i.e., the turbine stops when head drops below 2 m)?

20. Explain how the hybrid OTEC cycle combines the advantages of closed and open cycles. Why does it produce fresh water, and what is the trade-off in terms of system complexity?
