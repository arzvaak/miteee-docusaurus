# Biogas Production and Plant Design

> [!note] ◇ AFTER MIDSEM
> This chapter is outside the immediate midsem boundary and is retained for the complete course.


## Biogas Composition and Sources

Biogas is a combustible gas mixture produced by anaerobic digestion of organic matter. Typical composition: 55–65% CH₄ (primary energy carrier), 30–40% CO₂, trace H₂, H₂S, N₂. Heating value of raw biogas: approximately 18–23 MJ/m³.

**Sources:** cattle dung (primary), poultry droppings, pig manure, sewage, crop residues, vegetable waste, industrial waste (sugar, paper, food processing), aquatic biomass (water hyacinth, algae).

## The Anaerobic Digestion Process

Biogas is produced by anaerobic bacteria at 35–75°C. The efficiency depends on several critical factors:

| Factor | Optimal Range / Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| Temperature | 35–38°C (mesophilic) gives highest digestion rate |
| pH | 6.5–7.5 for microbial activity; > 8.2 for raw animal/plant waste |
| Retention period | 30–50 days; maximum gas in first 4 weeks |
| Loading rate | Volatile solids fed per day |
| Solid concentration | 4 parts dung : 5 parts water |
| Nutrients | Bacteria need C, H, O, P, S; P and N₂ rich materials boost production |
| Toxic substances | Ammonia, pesticides, detergents, heavy metals inhibit fermentation |

## Plant Components and Classification

### Main Components
1. **Digester (fermentation tank):** Underground brick/cement cylindrical tank
2. **Gas holder:** Fixed dome or floating drum
3. **Inlet:** Sloped pipe/tank for dung-water slurry
4. **Outlet:** For spent slurry (digestate) removal
5. **Slurry mixing tank:** Where biomass is mixed with water
6. **Gas outlet pipe:** Carries gas to point of use
7. **Stirrer:** Optional for large plants to ensure uniform fermentation

### Plant Types
1. **Continuous plants:** Single-stage (simple) or two-stage (two digesters in series — first for acid production, second for methanation; more efficient, shorter retention time)
2. **Batch-type:** Filled, sealed 35–45 days, emptied. Uneven gas production; multiple digesters needed for steady supply
3. **Floating drum (KVIC model):** Inverted steel drum as gas holder floating on slurry
4. **Fixed dome (Janta/Chinese model):** Gas collects under fixed dome, displacing slurry to outlet tank

## Fixed-Dome vs. Floating-Drum Design

### Fixed-Dome Plant

**Working:** Slurry fed via inlet; anaerobic fermentation produces gas under fixed dome; gas pressure builds, forcing spent slurry into outlet chamber and overflow tank; gas valve opened to supply user.

**Advantages:** Inexpensive (local materials), no moving parts, low maintenance, long lifespan (20+ years).

**Disadvantages:** Variable gas pressure, complex construction (skilled masons), gas leakage if dome cracks.

### Floating-Drum Plant

**Working:** Slurry fed into inlet chamber; biogas collects in floating drum, causing it to rise; drum weight provides constant gas pressure; spent slurry displaced to outlet.

**Advantages:** Constant gas pressure, simple construction, drum height indicates gas volume.

**Disadvantages:** Expensive (steel drum), drum rusts and requires regular painting, shorter lifespan.

### Comparison

| Feature | Fixed-Dome | Floating-Drum |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gas pressure | Variable | **Constant** |
| Cost | **Low** | High |
| Maintenance | **Very low** | High (rust prevention) |
| Lifespan | **20+ years** | Shorter (drum replacement) |
| Construction | Complex, needs skill | Simpler |
| Gas indication | None | Drum height indicates |

## Site Selection

- Optimum distance from source: 10 m for a 2 m³ plant
- Open space with temperature range 15–30°C
- Water availability for slurry preparation
- Consider water table to avoid seepage
- Minimum 15 m from well to check seepage
- Avoid areas prone to monsoon flooding

## Community Biogas Plants

Serve multiple families (50+), offering economies of scale: lower capital and maintenance cost per m³ of gas, reduced land requirement (land needed is inversely proportional to the square of capacity), compost as by-product, reduced smoke and pollution.

**Challenges in India:** Variability in dung availability (seasonal), labour-intensive collection, inconsistent feeding, lack of awareness, inadequate maintenance.
