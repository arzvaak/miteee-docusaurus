# Renewable Energy: Characteristics and Applications

> [!important] ◆ MIDSEM SYLLABUS
> This chapter is inside the immediate midsem boundary.


Renewable energy is derived from natural processes replenished at a rate equal to or faster than consumption. Unlike finite fossil fuels, renewable sources are virtually inexhaustible on a human timescale. The transition to renewables is driven by the need for energy security, environmental protection, and sustainable economic development.

## Advantages of Renewable Energy

**Environmental:** Low or zero greenhouse gas emissions during operation; directly reduces carbon footprint; improves air and water quality.

**Economic:** Zero or negligible fuel cost after installation, insulating against volatile fossil fuel prices; creates jobs in manufacturing, installation, and maintenance; reduces reliance on imported fuels, enhancing national security.

**Operational:** Distributed generation (e.g., rooftop solar) enhances grid stability; systems scale from household to utility-scale; many systems (solar PV) have few moving parts and low maintenance.

## Disadvantages and Barriers

**Technical:** Solar and wind are intermittent and weather-dependent, requiring storage or backup; high renewable penetration can cause voltage/frequency instability without smart grid controls; lower power density than fossil fuels requires more land.

**Economic:** High initial capital cost; battery and other storage systems add to project cost; often reliant on government subsidies and policy incentives for competitiveness.

**Environmental and social:** Large-scale solar/wind farms require significant land; visual and noise impact from wind turbines; mining for rare earth metals has environmental consequences.

## Geothermal Energy: A Case Study

Geothermal energy is the thermal energy generated and stored in the Earth's crust. It is a reliable, baseload renewable source. The Earth's interior heat creates a temperature gradient of approximately 0.03°C per metre of depth.

### Resource Classification

| Type | Description | Typical Temperature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hydrothermal** | Natural hot water or steam reservoirs; most common and economically viable | > 150°C |
| **Geo-pressured** | Hot, pressurized brine with dissolved methane at ~3500 m depth | 150–200°C |
| **Hot Dry Rock** | Hot, impermeable rock requiring artificial fracturing and fluid injection | > 150°C |
| **Magma** | Direct heat from molten rock; largely experimental | > 600°C |

### Power Plant Configurations

**Dry steam:** Steam from the reservoir drives a turbine directly. Operating at approximately 165°C and 7 atm, with plant efficiency around 14%.

**Flash steam:** High-pressure hot water (>180°C) is flashed into steam in a flash tank. The steam drives the turbine; brine is reinjected. Limitations include mineral scaling and ground subsidence.

**Binary cycle:** Hot geothermal water (<150°C) heats a secondary working fluid (isobutane, isopentane) via a heat exchanger. The vaporized fluid drives a turbine in a closed loop. Enables power generation from lower-temperature resources with minimal emissions.

### Applications Beyond Electricity
- Space heating and cooling (district heating)
- Industrial process heat (food dehydration, pasteurization)
- Aquaculture, spa and tourism, agricultural uses

### Geothermal Energy in India
- **Potential:** Estimated 10,600 MW across 350 hot spring sites in 11 states
- **Key locations:** Puga Valley (Ladakh), Sohana (Rajasthan), West Coast (Maharashtra-Goa), Cambay (Gujarat), SONATA belt, Godavari, and Mahanadi belts
- **Status:** No large-scale operational power plants yet; Puga Valley (5 MW) under development

### Environmental and Economic Considerations
- **Pros:** Minimal GHG emissions, small land footprint, high capacity factor (>90%), long plant life (30–50 years)
- **Cons:** Potential H₂S release, induced seismicity, brine disposal, land subsidence, high exploration and drilling costs

## Comparative Summary

| Feature | Renewable Energy | Conventional (Fossil Fuel) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fuel source | Inexhaustible | Finite |
| Emissions | Low to zero | High CO₂, SO₂, NOₓ |
| Fuel cost | Zero or negligible | Volatile |
| Capital cost | Generally high | Moderate to high |
| Reliability | Intermittent or baseload | Dispatchable |
| Land use | Often high | Moderate (plus mining) |
| Technology maturity | Rapidly evolving, costs falling | Mature |
