## 7.1 Renewable Energy Sources

### Solar Energy

| Technology | Application | Efficiency Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Solar PV** | Electricity generation | 15–22% (commercial modules) |
| **Solar thermal** | Water heating, process heat | 40–70% (thermal collection) |
| **Concentrated Solar Power (CSP)** | Grid-scale electricity | 15–25% |

- India's target: 500 GW non-fossil-fuel capacity by 2030.
- Solar PV cost has dropped >90% in the last decade.

### Wind Energy

- India's installed wind capacity: >45 GW (one of the largest globally).
- Onshore wind turbines: 1–3 MW per turbine.
- Capacity factor: 15–30% depending on site.
- India has a strong wind resource along the western and southern coasts.

### Biomass Energy

- Agricultural residues, wood waste, energy crops.
- **Direct combustion** for heat; **gasification** for electricity.
- **Biogas** from anaerobic digestion of organic waste.
- Carbon neutral if sourced sustainably.

### Small Hydropower

- Plants with capacity ≤ 25 MW.
- Run-of-river designs minimize environmental impact.
- India has significant small hydro potential in Himalayan states.

### Other Sources

- **Geothermal** — heat from the earth's interior.
- **Tidal and wave** — ocean energy (early stage in India).
- **Hydrogen** — produced by electrolysis using renewable electricity.

## 7.2 Energy Conservation in Buildings

### Building Energy Consumption

Buildings account for approximately **40% of global energy consumption** and **33% of greenhouse gas emissions**.

In commercial buildings:
- **Lighting:** ≈ 40–60% of electricity
- **HVAC:** ≈ 30–40% of electricity
- **Plug loads and other:** ≈ 10–20%

### Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC)

- Introduced by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency under the EC Act 2001.
- Sets minimum energy performance standards for commercial buildings (>500 m² or connected load >100 kW).
- Covers building envelope, HVAC, lighting, electrical systems, and water heating.

### Passive Design Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Building orientation** | Maximize north-south alignment to reduce solar heat gain |
| **Insulation** | Reduce heat transfer through walls, roof, and glazing |
| **Glazing** | Double/triple glazing, low-E coatings, appropriate SHGC |
| **Shading** | Overhangs, louvers, trees, fins to block direct sun |
| **Natural ventilation** | Cross-ventilation, stack ventilation to reduce cooling load |
| **Thermal mass** | Materials that absorb and release heat slowly (concrete, brick) |
| **Roof treatment** | Cool roofs (high solar reflectance), green roofs |

### Energy Performance Index (EPI)

$$   "EPI" = frac("Total annual energy consumption (kWh)", "Total conditioned area (m"^2")")   $$

- Lower EPI = more energy-efficient building.
- ECBC benchmark EPI values vary by building type and climate zone.

### Building Energy Audit Checklist

1. Building envelope — insulation, glazing, air leakage.
2. Lighting — lamp types, controls, daylighting.
3. HVAC — chiller efficiency, duct sealing, controls, maintenance.
4. Electrical — power factor, motor efficiency, VSDs.
5. Water heating — solar vs electric vs gas.
6. Plug loads — equipment scheduling, power management.

## 7.3 Accelerating Global Adoption of Energy-Efficient Products

### Barriers to Adoption

1. **High initial cost** — efficient equipment often costs more upfront.
2. **Lack of awareness** — consumers don't know the savings potential.
3. **Split incentive** — building owner pays for equipment, tenant pays for energy.
4. **Information asymmetry** — consumers can't compare efficiency.
5. **Access to finance** — difficulty funding efficient equipment.

### Policy Instruments

| Instrument | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Standards and Labeling** | Minimum efficiency standards + consumer information labels |
| **Incentives** | Tax rebates, subsidies, low-interest loans |
| **ESCOs** | Performance contracting removes upfront cost barrier |
| **Awareness campaigns** | UJALA, BEE star rating, PAT scheme |
| **Government procurement** | Leading by example in public buildings |

### The PAT (Perform, Achieve and Trade) Scheme

- Market-based mechanism for energy-intensive industries.
- **Designated consumers** in aluminum, cement, iron & steel, pulp & paper, textiles, fertilizers, railways, thermal power.
- **Energy Saving Certificates (ESCerts)** — tradeable certificates for exceeding targets.
- Targets specific energy consumption (SEC) reduction.

### International Frameworks

- **UNFCCC** — aims to reduce GHG emissions; COP is the deciding body.
- **Kyoto Protocol** (effective 2005) — binding emission targets for developed nations.
- **Paris Agreement** (2015) — 196 countries; limit warming to well below 2 °C, ideally 1.5 °C.
- **IRENA** — International Renewable Energy Agency; projects USD 27 trillion investment needed for energy transformation (2016–2050).

### Investment Context

- Historical energy-transition investment (2018): USD 309 billion/yr.
- Current plans projection (2016–2050): USD 343 billion/yr.
- Energy transformation projection: USD 662 billion/yr (>2× current plans).
- REmap Case cumulative investment (2016–2050): 110 USD trillion, with energy efficiency (35%) as the largest segment.

## 7.4 Role of Energy Managers and Auditors

### Duties of an Energy Manager (EC Act 2001)

1. Establish and improve the energy accounting system.
2. Conduct internal energy audits.
3. Recommend energy-efficient technologies.
4. Prepare annual energy conservation action plans and reports.

### Responsibilities

1. Ensure compliance with EC Act rules.
2. Train staff and build awareness.
3. Monitor consumption against norms.
4. Report to designated agency via the designated consumer.
