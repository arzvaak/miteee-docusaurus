## 1.1 What Is an Energy Audit?

An **energy audit** is a depth study to determine how and where energy is being utilized and converted from one form to another. It encompasses:

- Systematic study of energy production and its total cost.
- Quantification of where and what percentage of energy is utilized by each piece of equipment.
- Estimation of how much energy gets wasted.
- Strategies to increase efficient use of energy by reducing the cost of production.

The **Energy Conservation Act, 2001** provides the statutory definition:

> "A **verification, monitoring and analysis of use of energy** including submission of a **technical report containing recommendations for improving energy efficiency with cost benefit analysis**."

> **Exam note:** The statutory definition has four examinable elements — verification, monitoring, analysis, and a technical report with recommendations **plus cost–benefit analysis**.

### Need for Energy Audit

1. Minimise the cost of energy production.
2. Minimise operational cost.
3. Minimise the cost of maintenance.
4. Quality control.
5. Increase the efficiency of the plant by reducing overall cost and increasing profit.

## 1.2 Global Energy Scenario

- The global need for energy increases on average by about **2.4% every year**.
- Over **85% of total primary energy** comes from fossil fuels; current oil consumption is not sustainable long-term.
- Energy consumption has a significant impact on the natural environment — there is clear evidence that climate change is caused by human activity, mostly related to energy use.

### Sources of Global Electricity

| Source | Share | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 33% | Most carbon-intensive fossil fuel |
| Natural gas | >20% | — |
| Nuclear | ≈10% | Low-emission source |
| Hydropower | — | Generates more than all other renewables combined |
| Wind | 8% | Leading non-hydro renewable |
| Solar PV | 5% | — |

## 1.3 Energy Classification

Three independent classification axes:

1. **Primary** (unconverted fuels: coal, crude oil, gas) vs **Secondary** (stored or converted: electricity, steam, petrol). Electricity is secondary energy.
2. **Commercial** (available for a price) vs **Non-commercial** (firewood, agricultural waste, solar, wind). Note: solar and wind are non-commercial in this scheme even though they are also renewable.
3. **Renewable** (inexhaustible) vs **Non-renewable** (deplete with time).

## 1.4 The Tonne of Oil Equivalent (toe)

Primary energy content of fuels is expressed in toe:

$$   1 " toe" = 1 times 10^(7) " kcal" = 11{,}630 " kWh" = 41{,}868 " MJ"   $$

### Key Conversions

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| 1 barrel of oil equivalent (boe) | ≈ 0.136 toe |
| 1 tonne diesel | 1.01 toe |
| 1 tonne petrol | 1.05 toe |
| Thermal-to-electrical efficiency (thermal power plants) | 39% |

> **Common trap:** A boe is much smaller than a toe (≈ 0.136). Diesel and petrol exceed 1 toe per tonne.

### Worked Example

How much electricity (in kWh) can be generated from 1 toe in a thermal power plant at 39% efficiency?

$$   E = 0.39 times 11{,}630 = 4{,}535.7 " kWh" approx 4.54 " MWh"   $$

## 1.5 India's Energy Landscape

- India is a developing country with ever-increasing energy needs requiring huge investments.
- 70% of power generating capacity in India is from coal-based thermal power plants.
- Coal is pulverized to the consistency of talcum powder and burnt at 1300 °C inside the boiler.
- Commercial buildings: **lighting (60%)** and **air conditioning (30%)** together ≈ 90% of electricity consumption.
- Electricity consumption from commercial buildings grows at **9–10%** annually.
- Built-up area currently ≈ 1,396 million m²; estimated to reach 2,211 million m² in 20 years.
- Electricity Act 2003: the act of Parliament enacted to transform the power sector.
- National Action Plan on Climate Change has **8 missions**.

### Energy Pricing and Availability Based Tariffs (ABT)

- ABT applies incentives and disincentives to grid participants against deviations from their commitment.
- It brings enhanced grid discipline, promoting competition and efficiency.
- Non-payment of prescribed charges is liable for appropriate action.

## 1.6 Energy Security

**Energy security** is the continuous availability of energy in varied forms in sufficient quantities at reasonable prices.

**Strategies:**

1. Building energy supplies.
2. Diversification of energy supply sources (energy mix), including renewable energy.
3. Increased capacity of fuel switching.
4. Controlling and managing demand.
5. Energy efficiency via minimum energy performance standards.
6. Sustainable development.
7. Efficient generation of energy resources.
8. Privatization with reduced cross subsidies on oil products and electricity tariffs.

## 1.7 Energy Efficiency and Climate Change

### Global Environmental Issues

- **Svante Arrhenius** (1896), Swedish scientist, predicted that human activities will affect the way the sun interacts with earth — global warming and climate change.
- Three issues of global significance: ozone layer depletion, global warming, loss of biodiversity.

### Ozone Layer Depletion

- Ozone layer blocks UV radiation from the Sun.
- Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS): chemicals containing chlorine or bromine.
- One chlorine molecule can break down thousands of ozone molecules.
- Key ODS: CFCs (refrigeration, aerosols, foam-blowing), Halons (fire extinguishers), HCFCs (substitutes for CFCs), methyl bromide (pesticide), carbon tetrachloride (industrial processes), methyl chloroform (solvent).

**Countermeasures:** Montreal Protocol (1987) — international treaty to phase out ODS production and consumption.

### Global Warming

- Increase in average atmospheric temperature from increased greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide).
- Global Warming Potentials depend on **radiative efficiency** (ability to absorb energy) and **atmospheric lifetimes**.
- Implications: rise in global temperature, sea-level rise, food shortages. India could face a 2.3 to 4.8 °C increase, with North India more affected.

### Carbon Credits and Trading

- **Prototype Carbon Fund (PCF):** public-private partnership by World Bank (2000) to pilot market-based mechanisms for reducing GHG emissions.
- **Carbon credit:** emission of CO₂ or other GHGs equal to one tonne of CO₂. Equivalent to approximately 2,400-mile drive. Credits can be traded; companies get a declining set number over time.

### Sustainable Development

> "Meeting the needs of the present, without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

Three pillars: economic security and prosperity, social development and advancement, environmental sustainability.

- **Input:** Use renewables at a rate at which they can be renewed; use non-renewables till a substitute is found.
- **Output:** Do not overstrain the assimilation capacity of the ecosystem.
