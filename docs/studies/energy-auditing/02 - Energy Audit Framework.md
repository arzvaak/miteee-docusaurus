## 2.1 The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

The EC Act 2001 is the governing legislation for energy auditing in India. Its overarching goal is **to promote energy efficiency in the country**.

### Five Objectives

1. Provide a **policy framework** and direction to national energy conservation activities.
2. Coordinate policies and programs on **efficient use of energy** with stakeholders.
3. Establish systems to **verify, measure, and monitor** energy efficiency improvements.
4. Leverage multilateral, bilateral, and private sector support.
5. Demonstrate energy efficiency delivery systems through **public-private partnerships**.

### Institutional Thrust Areas

- **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE):** implements policies and programs at the national level.
- **State Designated Agencies (SDA):** coordinate, regulate, and enforce the Act within the state.
- Identification of **Designated Consumers**.
- Implementation of the **Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC)**.

### Programmatic Thrust Areas

- Energy efficiency in government buildings.
- **Demand Side Management (DSM)** in the power and agriculture sectors.
- **Standards & Labeling** program.
- Certification of **Energy Managers & Auditors**.
- Establishment of **Energy Service Companies (ESCOs)**.

> **Common trap:** SDAs act at the state level; BEE at the national level. DSM is named for two sectors: power and agriculture.

## 2.2 Designated Consumers

Designated Consumers must:

1. Appoint **certified energy managers**.
2. Undergo specific **energy audits**.
3. **Report energy consumption data** via **e-filing** of consumption returns.
4. **Comply** with prescribed energy consumption norms.

**Eight DC sectors:** Aluminum, Cement, Fertilizers, Iron and Steel, Pulp and Paper, Railways, Textile, Thermal Power Stations.

---

## 2.3 Role of the Central Government

The central government may:

- Specify **norms for processes** and energy consumption standards for equipment/appliances that consume, generate, transmit, or supply energy.
- **Prohibit manufacture, sale, purchase, or import** of equipment unless they conform to energy consumption standards.
- Direct **display of particulars on labels** on equipment.
- Specify any user or class of users as a Designated Consumer based on the intensity or quantity of energy consumed.
- Direct DCs to get energy audits conducted by **accredited energy auditors**.
- Direct DCs to appoint energy managers.
- Establish **energy conservation funds** (National and State).
- Constitute an **Energy Conservation Fund** and specify penalties.

## 2.4 Energy Audit Phases

### Preliminary (Walk-Through) Audit

- Quick assessment of major energy systems.
- Identifies obvious areas of waste.
- Produces a report with preliminary recommendations.
- Low cost, completed in 1–2 days.

### Detailed Audit

- Involves 8–10 steps:
  1. Preparation and top-management briefing; define scope and team.
  2. Plant data collection (plans, bills, production data).
  3. Preliminary walk-through survey.
  4. Baseline energy consumption analysis.
  5. Detailed monitoring, metering, and measurements.
  6. Material and energy balance for major utilities.
  7. Identification of energy-saving opportunities.
  8. Techno-economic evaluation (payback, ROI, NPV).
  9. Prioritisation and action plan / report.
  10. Implementation follow-up and monitoring & verification (M&V).

> **Exam note:** The detailed audit has ten steps. The preliminary audit produces quick, low-cost recommendations.

### Plant Energy Performance (PEP)

PEP = Actual specific energy consumption / Normative (best practice) specific energy consumption.

- PEP < 1 means the plant is performing better than the benchmark.
- PEP > 1 means there is room for improvement.

## 2.5 Energy Audit Instruments

### Electrical Measurements

| Parameter | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Voltage, Current, Power Factor | Power analyzer |
| kWh, frequency, harmonics | Energy meter, power quality analyzer |
| Illumination levels | Lux meter |
| Leakage detection | Ultrasonic instruments |

### Thermal Measurements

| Parameter | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Thermocouples, infrared thermometers, pyrometers |
| Heat flux | Heat flux sensors |
| Combustion analysis | Flue gas analyzers (O₂, CO₂, CO, NOₓ) |
| Flow (steam, water, gas) | Orifice plates, ultrasonic flow meters, rotameters |

## 2.6 Material and Energy Balance

### Why?

- Quantifies all energy inputs and outputs.
- Identifies where energy is lost.
- Provides the basis for identifying improvement opportunities.

### Components

A material and energy balance tracks:
- **Mass in = mass out** (conservation of mass).
- **Energy in = energy out + losses** (conservation of energy).

### Process Flowchart

1. Draw the process schematic.
2. Label all streams with mass flow rates, temperatures, and pressures.
3. Write balance equations for each unit operation.
4. Close the balance — verify that the total energy in equals total energy out plus losses.

### Sankey Diagrams

- Arrow width is **proportional to the size of the actual energy flow**.
- Useful for visualizing where energy enters, is converted, and is lost.

**Furnace Sankey example:** Flue gas losses (largest), wall losses, opening losses, heat storage to walls/fixtures, and useful output.

**Light bulb Sankey example:** Of 100 J electrical input, 10 J leaves as light and 90 J as heat — only 10% useful output.
