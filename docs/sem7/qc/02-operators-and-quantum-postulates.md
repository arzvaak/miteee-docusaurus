# Operators and Quantum Postulates

**Source coverage:** PDF pages 21-44.

## 1. Bras and dual vectors

If

$$
|v\rangle=\begin{bmatrix}v_1\\v_2\end{bmatrix},
$$

then its bra is its conjugate transpose:

$$
\langle v|=|v\rangle^\dagger=\begin{bmatrix}v_1^*&v_2^*\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The product $\langle v|w\rangle$ is a scalar. The product $|v\rangle\langle w|$ is an **outer product**, hence an operator/matrix.

## 2. Linear operators and matrices

An operator $A$ is linear when

$$
A(a|v\rangle+b|w\rangle)=aA|v\rangle+bA|w\rangle.
$$

Once the action of $A$ on every basis vector is known, its action on every vector is known. In an orthonormal basis, the matrix elements are

$$
A_{ij}=\langle i|A|j\rangle.
$$

The $j$-th matrix column is the coordinate vector of $A|j\rangle$.

For products, order matters:

$$
AB|\psi\rangle=A(B|\psi\rangle).
$$

The rightmost operator acts first.

## 3. Pauli matrices

The identity and Pauli operators are

$$
I=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad
X=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
Y=\begin{bmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
Z=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

They are Hermitian and unitary, and satisfy

$$
X^2=Y^2=Z^2=I.
$$

Their multiplication is non-commutative:

$$
XY=iZ,\quad YZ=iX,\quad ZX=iY,
$$

while reversing the order changes the sign.

## 4. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

If

$$
A|v\rangle=\lambda|v\rangle,
$$

then $|v\rangle$ is an eigenvector and $\lambda$ its eigenvalue. A measurement observable is associated with a Hermitian operator, whose eigenvalues are real and whose eigenvectors can be chosen as an orthonormal basis.

Examples:

$$
Z|0\rangle=+|0\rangle,\qquad Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle,
$$

so the computational basis is the eigenbasis of $Z$.

## 5. Adjoint, Hermitian, unitary, normal

The adjoint is

$$
A^\dagger=(A^*)^T.
$$

- **Hermitian:** $A^\dagger=A$. Observables are represented this way.
- **Unitary:** $U^\dagger U=UU^\dagger=I$. Closed-system state evolution and quantum gates use unitary operators.
- **Normal:** $AA^\dagger=A^\dagger A$. Normal operators admit an orthonormal eigenbasis.

Unitary operators preserve inner products:

$$
\langle Uv|Uw\rangle=\langle v|U^\dagger U|w\rangle=\langle v|w\rangle.
$$

Therefore they preserve norms and total probability. They are reversible, with $U^{-1}=U^\dagger$.

## 6. Quantum-mechanics postulates

The deck presents the standard ideas across five postulates. A compact exam version is:

### Postulate 1 - state space

A closed physical system is associated with a complex Hilbert space. Its pure state is represented by a unit vector $|\psi\rangle$. Vectors differing only by a global phase represent the same physical pure state.

### Postulate 2 - composite systems

The state space of a composite system is the tensor product of component spaces:

$$
\mathcal H_{AB}=\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B.
$$

### Postulates 3 and 4 - measurement and probability

For an observable $A$ with eigenstates $|a_i\rangle$ and eigenvalues $a_i$, expand

$$
|\psi\rangle=\sum_i c_i|a_i\rangle.
$$

Measurement returns $a_i$ with probability

$$
p_i=|c_i|^2=|\langle a_i|\psi\rangle|^2,
$$

and an ideal projective measurement leaves the system in the associated eigenspace.

### Postulate 5 - evolution

Between measurements, an isolated system evolves unitarily:

$$
|\psi(t_2)\rangle=U(t_2,t_1)|\psi(t_1)\rangle.
$$

In continuous time this is generated by the Hamiltonian through the Schrödinger equation,

$$
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle=H|\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

## 7. Measurement disturbs the state

Classically, an idealized measurement can reveal a pre-existing value without necessarily changing the system. Quantum measurement generally changes the state. If $|\psi\rangle$ is not already an eigenstate of the measured observable, the post-measurement state depends on the random outcome.

If the state is already an eigenstate, repeating the same ideal projective measurement immediately gives the same eigenvalue with probability one.

## Worked example: build an operator matrix

Suppose $A|0\rangle=|1\rangle$ and $A|1\rangle=|0\rangle$. The columns are the images of the basis vectors:

$$
[A]=\begin{bmatrix}|&|\\A|0\rangle&A|1\rangle\\|&|\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}=X.
$$

## Quick Quiz: Operators

**Question 1:** What does $U^\dagger U=I$ guarantee physically?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It preserves norms and inner products, so normalized states remain normalized; it also makes the operation reversible.</details>

**Question 2:** Are all Hermitian matrices unitary?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Hermitian means $A^\dagger=A$; unitary means $A^\dagger A=I$. Pauli matrices have both properties, but a general Hermitian matrix need not be unitary.</details>

**Question 3:** Why must observable eigenvalues be real?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Observable operators are Hermitian, and Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues.</details>
