## Lecture 02 — Energy Acts, Management, and Audit (Slides 1–10)

This chunk introduces the legal and institutional foundation of energy auditing in India: the **Energy Conservation (EC) Act, 2001**, its objectives and thrust areas, the roles of the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), State Designated Agencies (SDAs), Designated Consumers (DCs), and the central government.

Title slide for Lecture 02, covering the Energy Act together with Energy Management and Audit topics that follow in later chunks. [Source: slide 1]

Key framing points from the slide: [Source: slide 3]

- **Net Zero Energy Buildings** — introduced as a related concept.

*Trap:* The year is **2001** (EC Act – 2001); do not confuse it with other energy legislation.

### Slide 4 — Breakdown of Electricity Consumption in Commercial Buildings

Figures exactly as stated on the slide: [Source: slide 4]

- **Lighting (60%)** and **Air Conditioning (30%)** are the **largest energy users** in commercial buildings → together ≈ 90% of electricity consumption.
- Built-up area currently ~**1,396 million m²**; in 20 years, **2,211 million m² (estimated)**\*
- India will add more than **800,000 m²** of commercial buildings in the next **20 years**.
- Electricity consumption from commercial buildings grows at an annual rate of **9–10%** on average.

\* Asterisk appears on the slide; its footnote text is not legible in the source, so it is reproduced as-is rather than interpreted.

*Traps:* Don't swap the shares (lighting > air conditioning); the growth rate is a **range (9–10%)**, not a single value.

### Slide 5 — Objectives of the EC Act – 2001

The slide lists five objectives; the overarching goal is **"TO PROMOTE ENERGY EFFICIENCY IN THE COUNTRY."** [Source: slide 5]

1. Provide a **policy framework** and direction to **national energy conservation activities**.
2. Coordinate policies and programs on **efficient use of energy with stakeholders**.
3. Establish systems and procedures to **verify, measure and monitor Energy Efficiency improvements**.
4. **Leverage (Open to participate)** multilateral, bilateral and private sector support to implement the EC Act.
5. Demonstrate **Energy Efficiency delivery systems** through **public-private partnerships**.

*Mnemonic aid:* framework → coordination → verify/measure/monitor → leverage support → demonstrate via PPP.

### Slide 6 — Thrust Areas of EC Act (Institutional)

Four institutional thrust areas: [Source: slide 6]

- Establishment of **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)** — implementation of policies and programs related to energy.
- Establishment of **State Designated Agencies (SDA)** — coordinate, regulate and enforce the provisions of this Act **within the State**.
- Identification of **Designated Consumers**.
- Implementation of the **Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC)**.

*Trap:* SDAs act at the **state level** (enforcement within the State), whereas BEE implements policies and programs at the national level.

### Slide 7 — Thrust Areas of EC Act (Programmatic)

Continuing thrust areas: [Source: slide 7]

- **Energy Efficiency** in Govt. Buildings & Establishments.
- **Demand Side Management (DSM)** in Power Sector & Agriculture Sector.
- **Standards & Labeling** Program.
- Energy experts — **Energy Managers & Auditors** (certification of professionals).
- Establishment of an Energy Efficiency Delivery Service Mechanism (**ESCO** — Energy Service Company).

*Trap:* DSM is named for **two specific sectors**: power and agriculture.

### Slide 8 — Designated Consumers: Obligations

Designated Consumers are required to: [Source: slide 8]

- Appoint **certified energy managers**,
- Undergo specific **energy audits**,
- **Report energy consumption data**, and
- **Comply** with prescribed **energy consumption norms**.

Additionally, **e-filing of energy Consumption Returns** needs to be filed for designated consumers.

*Trap:* The requirement is a **certified** energy manager (not merely trained/experienced); reporting is via **e-filing** of returns.

### Slide 9 — Designated Consumers: Sectors Listed

The slide enumerates eight DC sectors: [Source: slide 9]

Aluminum • Cement • Fertilizers • Iron and Steel • Pulp and paper • Railways • Textile • Thermal Power Stations

*Trap:* Count/list carefully — the slide lists **eight** sectors; Railways is included among industrial-type entries, and Thermal Power Stations is distinct from other categories.

### Slide 10 — Role of Central Government

Powers/responsibilities under the Act, as stated: [Source: slide 10]

- Specify the **norms for processes** and **energy consumption standards** for any equipment/appliances which **consumes, generates, transmits or supplies energy**.
- **Specify** equipment or appliance (or class of equipment/appliances), as the case may be, for the purposes of the **proposed legislation**.
- **Prohibit manufacture, sale, purchase, or import** of equipment/appliances **unless they conform to energy consumption standards**.
- Direct **display of such particulars on labels** on equipment or appliances.

*Trap:* The standards apply across four energy roles — consume, generate, transmit, supply; the prohibition covers four actions — manufacture, sale, purchase, import. Label display of particulars is a separate directive tied to the Standards & Labeling theme of slide 7.

## Slides 11–20

### Slide 11 — Role of Central Government: Designated Consumers, Audits, and Energy Managers

The central government may **specify any user, or class of users, as a "designated consumer,"** having regard to the **intensity or quantity of energy consumed** [Source: slide 11]. Toward designated consumers it may then:

- Direct them to get an **energy audit** conducted by an **accredited energy auditor**.
- Direct them to **appoint an energy manager**, in charge of activities for conservation of energy.
- Prescribe **minimum qualifications** for appointment of **energy managers**.

> **Trap:** Designation hinges on *intensity or quantity* of energy consumed — either criterion suffices. Also remember the auditor must be *accredited*; the manager's qualifications are set by *prescribed minimums*.

[Source: slide 11]

### Slide 12 — Role of Central Government (continued): Reporting, Schemes, Building Codes

Three further powers over designated consumers [Source: slide 12]:

- Direct them to **furnish information** on energy consumed **and action taken** on the recommendations of the accredited energy auditor.
- Direct them to **prepare a scheme** for **efficient use of energy and its conservation**, and to **implement such schemes** as may be prescribed.
- **Prescribe Energy Conservation Building Codes** for efficient use of energy and its conservation in a **building or building complex**.

> **Exam note:** Reporting covers both *consumption data* and *follow-up action* on audit recommendations — omitting either half loses marks.

[Source: slide 12]

### Slide 13 — Penalties & Appellate Tribunal

- If any person **fails to comply**, he shall be liable to a **penalty not exceeding ten thousand rupees** (₹10,000).
- An **additional penalty of one thousand rupees** (₹1,000) **for every day during which such failure continues**.
- A **Member of the State Commission** shall be appointed as an **adjudicating officer** for holding an inquiry.
- **No civil court shall have jurisdiction** to entertain any matter related to the inquiry [Source: slide 13].

Penalty model (as given):

$$\text{Total liability} \le \text{₹}10{,}000 \;+\; \text{₹}1{,}000 \times (\text{days of continued failure})$$

> **Traps:** (1) The base penalty has a *cap* ("not exceeding") of ₹10,000; the ₹1,000/day charge is *additional and per day*. (2) Adjudication lies with the *State Commission member*, explicitly **not** a civil court.

[Source: slide 13]

### Slide 14 — Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)

- **Established on 1 March 2002** under the **EC Act**.
- Implements the **regulatory and promotional functions** of the EC Act.
- Objective: **reduce the energy intensity** of the country.
- Has **State Designated Agencies (SDAs)** in each state, responsible for **enforcement and awareness** [Source: slide 14].

> **Exam note:** BEE performs *both* regulatory and promotional roles; enforcement at state level runs through SDAs, not BEE directly.

[Source: slide 14]

### Slide 15 — Functions of BEE: Standards, Labelling, Designation

Statutory/notification functions [Source: slide 15]:

1. Specify the **process and energy consumption standards** required to be **notified**.
2. **Labeling** of certain equipment requiring some input of energy, along with prescription about the **display of standards upon such labels**.
3. To **notify users or class of users as 'designated consumers'** under this law.

> **Trap:** Under these slides, designating *designated consumers* appears as a central-government role (slide 11) and as a BEE function (slide 15) — the deck presents both; quote whichever slide is asked.

[Source: slide 15]

### Slide 16 — Functions of BEE: Awareness and Promotion

Promotional functions listed [Source: slide 16]:

- **Create awareness** and **disseminate information** for **efficient use of energy** and conservation.
- **Training of personnel**.
- **Strengthen consultancy services**.
- Promote **research and development** in the field of **energy efficiency**.
- Develop good **testing** and **certification** procedures.
- Promote use of **energy-efficient process, equipment, devices and systems**.
- Promote **innovative financing** of energy-efficiency projects; etc.

> **Exam note:** Contrast with slide 15 — notification functions (standards, labels, designated consumers) vs. promotional functions (awareness, training, R&D, financing).

[Source: slide 16]

### Slide 17 — Standard & Labelling: Standards and MEPS

- **Standard**: regulations to **limit energy consumption of manufactured products**.
- Purpose includes to **prohibit the sale of products less energy efficient than the Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS)** [Source: slide 17].

**Benefits** (as listed):

- Increased average efficiency or quality of products on the market.
- Reduced energy costs.
- Reduced greenhouse gas emissions.
- Protect consumers.
- Improved market efficacy & competition.

> **Trap:** MEPS is the *threshold below which sale can be prohibited* — standards regulate products; they are not voluntary guidelines.

[Source: slide 17]

### Slide 18 — Labelling: Definition, History, Benefits

- **Energy-efficiency labels** are information **affixed to manufactured products** that usually **communicate the product's energy performance**.
- **History and success**: labelling **started in Poland way back in 1962** [Source: slide 18].

**Benefits** (as listed):

- Recognize **best** products.
- Describe product **performance & quality**.
- Promote **competition & innovation**.
- Provide **visual information about EE standards** to consumers.

> **Trap:** The origin year/country often asked verbatim — **Poland, 1962**.

[Source: slide 18]

### Slide 19 — Types of Label

| Type | What it does |

| **Comparative label** | **Compares the energy consumption of similar products** |

> **Trap:** Endorsement = a *yes/no certification* within a category; comparative = *relative comparison across similar products*. Do not interchange.

[Source: slide 19]

### Slide 21 — ISEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Rating for Air Conditioners)

- **ISEER is a seasonal energy efficiency rating for air conditioners**, calculated as cooling load divided by energy consumed [Source: slide 21].

$$\text{ISEER} = \frac{\text{Cooling load (seasonal)}}{\text{Energy consumed}}$$

- Exam note: ISEER is a *ratio* — higher ISEER means more cooling per unit of electricity, i.e., a more efficient AC.
- Trap: do not invert the ratio (energy consumed is the **denominator**).

### Slide 25 — Star-label Upgradation

- The **star labelling program gets updated every year** as technology improves and more efficient products become available; **the labels will accordingly adjust** [Source: slide 25].
- Exam note: star thresholds are dynamic, not fixed — a 4-star appliance today may only meet a lower star level in future years.
- Trap: the revision cycle stated here is **annual** ("every year").

### Slide 26 — Star Rating of Buildings

- Buildings are rated on a **1–5 Star scale**, with **5 Star labelled buildings being the most efficient** [Source: slide 26].
- The **BEE Star Rating Scheme is based on actual performance** of the building in terms of **specific energy** usage, termed the **Energy Performance Indicator (EPI)** [Source: slide 26].
- **Average Annual hourly Energy Performance Index (AAhEPI)**, with units **Wh/hr/sq.m** [Source: slide 26].
- Energy consumption used for the calculation **excludes on-site renewable sources**, such as **solar photovoltaic** [Source: slide 26].
- Formula/symbol notes:

$$\text{EPI} = \frac{\text{Energy consumed in a building over a year}}{\text{Total built-up area}} \quad \text{(kWh/sq m/year)}$$

$$\text{AAhEPI} \; \left[\frac{\text{Wh}}{\text{hr}\cdot\text{sq.m}}\right]$$

- Traps: 5 stars = most efficient (not 1); on-site renewables (solar PV) are **excluded** from the consumed energy; do not confuse AAhEPI units (Wh/hr/sq.m) with EPI units (kWh/sq m/year).

### Slide 27 — Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC)

- **Energy Conservation Building Code Rules, 2018** — a part of the **Energy Conservation Act, 2001** [Source: slide 27].
- Applicable for **BUILDING COMPLEXES** [Source: slide 27].
- Key definitions [Source: slide 27]:
  - **"built-up area"** — area covered by walls **excluding PARKING**.
  - **"connected load"** — **total of the rated wattage of all equipment installed in the building**.
  - **"contract demand"** — the **maximum demand in kiloWatt agreed to be supplied by the electricity provider**.
- Traps: built-up area **excludes parking**; connected load (sum of rated wattages installed) ≠ contract demand (agreed maximum kW draw from the provider); ECBC Rules are 2018 but sit under the 2001 Act.

### Slide 28 — Energy Conservation Measures

- Energy conservation measures are **measures incorporated in the building design for saving energy**, including [Source: slide 28]:
  - **Managing or reducing peak electrical or thermal demand efficiently**.
  - **Reducing cooling or heating load**.
- Exam note: these are *design-stage* measures — they act on demand (peak electrical/thermal) and on loads (cooling/heating), not on supply-side generation.

### Slide 29 — ECBC Code (Impact)

- **ECBC-compliant buildings deliver 20–25% of energy savings in different climates, when compared with typical buildings** [Source: slide 29].
- **15 GW reduction in peak demand** [Source: slide 29].
- Trap: the savings figure (20–25%) is relative to *typical* buildings and holds across *different climates*; the peak-demand figure is **15 GW** (not 15 MW).

### Slide 30 — Energy Performance Index (EPI) and Climate Zones

- **EPI** = **total energy consumed in a building over a year divided by total built-up area**, in **kWh/sq m/year** [Source: slide 30].

$$\text{EPI} = \frac{\text{Total energy consumed in a building over a year}}{\text{Total built-up area}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kWh}}{\text{sq m}\cdot\text{year}}\right]$$

- **Climate zones** (five) [Source: slide 30]:
  1. HOT & DRY
  2. WARM HUMID
  3. COMPOSITE
  4. TEMPERATE
  5. COLD

Finer state-level boundaries are too small to read reliably, so only these zone-level observations are supported [Source: slide 30].

## Slides 31–40

### Slide 31 — Definition: "Energy Performance Index Ratio"

[Source: slide 31]

> "energy performance index ratio" means the ratio of the energy performance index of the **proposed building** to the energy performance index of the **standard baseline building**.

In symbols (as defined verbally in the source):

$$\text{EPI ratio} = \dfrac{\text{EPI}_{\text{proposed building}}}{\text{EPI}_{\text{standard baseline building}}}$$

This ratio is the metric used by the Whole Building Performance compliance route (Slide 33). **Trap:** the numerator is the *proposed* design, the denominator is the *standard baseline* building — reversing them in an exam answer changes the meaning.

### Slide 32 — ECBC: Applicability and Systems Covered

[Source: slide 32]

ECBC is **applicable to buildings with a connected load of 100 kiloWatt (kW) or above or a contract demand of 120 kilo-Volt Ampere (kVA)**. Note the **OR** condition — either threshold triggers applicability. **Common trap:** do not swap the numbers (100 ↔ kW, 120 ↔ kVA).

The code covers:

- building envelope;
- comfort systems and controls (heating, ventilation and air conditioning, service hot water system);
- lighting and controls;
- electrical and renewable energy systems;
- any other system, as may be specified from time to time.

The attached diagram (legible) depicts the **building envelope** as the sum of its components:

**roof + walls + windows, doors + foundation = BUILDING ENVELOPE**

i.e., the envelope is the collection of all physical separator elements between conditioned and unconditioned space, not just walls or the roof alone.

### Slide 33 — ECBC Compliance Mechanism

[Source: slide 33]

Two compliance routes:

1. **Prescriptive Method** — as per code (component-wise requirements).
2. **Whole Building Performance Method** — the "energy performance index ratio" must be **less than or equal to unity** (≤ 1).

Procedure for erection of a Code-compliant building: the **building owner** will **submit details as per ECBC certified by Empaneled Energy Auditors** to the **concerned authority**.

**Trap:** the WBP criterion is ≤ 1 (inclusive), not strictly < 1. Certification is by *Empaneled Energy Auditors*, not the owner themselves.

### Slide 34 — What Empaneled Energy Auditors Check

[Source: slide 34]

- (i) floor area; (ii) window area; (iii) wall area; (iv) roof area of the building; (v) built-up area of the proposed design of the building;
- solar heat gain coefficient, visible light transmittance;
- HVAC equipment details;
- equipment schedules;
- lighting power density + lighting controls;
- motors and their efficiencies.

The first five items are explicitly numbered (i)–(v) in the source — a likely exam list-recall item. Envelope glazing performance is captured by the pair *solar heat gain coefficient* (thermal) and *visible light transmittance* (daylight).

### Slide 35 — Levels of Energy Efficiency Performance

[Source: slide 35]

| Level | Energy savings vs. a conventional building |
|---|---|
| ECBC Buildings | 25% |
| ECBC+ Buildings | 35% |

### Slide 75 — Mass and Energy Balance (Figure 5.2) and the Balance Equations

Image-only slide. Figure 5.2 shows a **Unit Operation** block with stored quantities inside, surrounded by streams: [Source: slide 75]

| Direction | Stream | Notation in figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In | Raw Materials in | $m_{R1}, m_{R2}, m_{R3}$ |
| In | Energy in Heat, Work, Chemical, Electrical | $E_{R1}, E_{R2}, E_{R3}$ |
| Stored (inside block) | Stored Materials | $m_{S1}, m_{S2}, m_{S3}$ |
| Stored (inside block) | Stored Energy | $E_{S1}, E_{S2}, E_{S3}$ |
| Out | Products out | $m_{P1}, m_{P2}, m_{P3}$ |
| Out | Waste products | $m_{W1}, m_{W2}, m_{W3}$ |
| Out | Energy in products | $E_{P1}, E_{P2}, E_{P3}$ |
| Out | Energy in waste | $E_{W1}, E_{W2}, E_{W3}$ |
| Out | Energy losses to surroundings | $E_{L1}, E_{L2}, E_{L3}$ |

The two balance equations displayed are:

$$\text{Mass In} = \text{Mass Out} + \text{Mass Stored}$$

$$\text{Raw Materials} = \text{Products} + \text{Waste Products} + \text{Stored Products} + \text{Losses}$$

**Symbol key:** subscripts $R$ = raw/input, $S$ = stored, $P$ = products, $W$ = waste, $L$ = losses.

**Trap:** the mass balance is an *equality with a storage term* — it only reduces to "in = out" when nothing accumulates (steady state).

### Slide 77 — Energy Analysis using Sankey Diagram

- The Sankey diagram is a **VISUAL** tool to represent an entire input and output **energy flow** in any energy equipment or system.
- Flows are represented by **arrows**.
- **The width of the arrows is proportional to the size of the actual flow.** [Source: slide 77]

The slide's illustration (light bulb): **Electrical energy 100 J** input splits into **Light energy 10 J** (useful) and **Heat energy 90 J** (loss). [Source: slide 77, figure]

**Trap:** arrow *width* encodes flow magnitude — not velocity or direction. In the bulb example, only 10 J of the 100 J input leaves as light; 90 J is dissipated as heat.
