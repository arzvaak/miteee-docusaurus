## Question 1

### 1A — Global Warming Potential · (02 Marks)

**Source wording:** "Define Global Warming Potential (GWP). List any two examples of gases having a high GWP."

**Topic:** Climate/greenhouse-gas metrics.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
GWP is an index comparing the radiative forcing (heat trapped) of 1 unit mass of a gas over a chosen time horizon (commonly 100 years) with that of the same mass of CO₂ (GWP = 1 by definition). It combines a gas's infrared absorption capacity and atmospheric lifetime into one number.

High-GWP examples (any two): Methane (CH₄, ≈ 21–25× CO₂); Nitrous oxide (N₂O, ≈ 296–310× CO₂); CFCs/HFCs/SF₆ (thousands of times CO₂).

**Final answer:** GWP defined relative to CO₂ = 1 over a reference horizon; e.g., CH₄ and N₂O (or SF₆/CFCs) as high-GWP gases.

**Verification:** Both asked elements (definition + two named gases) covered; mark value (02) legible in source.

### 1B — Classification of energy · (04 Marks)

**Source wording:** "Explain the three classification of energy, with examples."

**Topic:** Basic energy concepts/forms.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:** The three standard classifications:

- **Primary vs secondary:** Primary is naturally available in raw form — coal, crude oil, natural gas, uranium, hydro, solar, wind. Secondary is converted/carrier form obtained from primary — electricity, steam, coke, compressed air, hydrogen.
- **Commercial vs non-commercial:** Commercial is bought/sold in markets with a price — coal, petroleum products, grid electricity, natural gas. Non-commercial is traditionally gathered free of market transaction — firewood, agro-waste, cattle dung.
- **Renewable vs non-renewable:** Renewable regenerates naturally — solar, wind, hydro, biomass, geothermal, tidal. Non-renewable is finite/depletable — coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear fuel.

**Final answer:** Primary/secondary, commercial/non-commercial, renewable/non-renewable, each with examples as above.

**Verification:** All three classifications with examples supplied, matching the four-mark demand; no official key in source.

### 1C — Energy-sector reforms · (04 Marks)

**Source wording:** "Elaborate on the energy reforms made in the electricity, oil sector and coal sector."

**Topic:** Indian energy-sector reform history.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

- **Electricity:** Reform of State Electricity Boards (unbundling into generation/transmission/distribution entities), independent regulatory commissions (CERC/SERCs), encouragement of private/captive/IPP participation, open access, and eventual consolidation through the Electricity Act, 2003.

**Final answer:** Electricity — unbundling/regulators/competition; Oil — APM dismantling/NELP/pricing freedom; Coal — captive mining/competition/price de-control.

## Question 2

### 2A — Electricity Act, 2003 · (06 Marks)

**Source wording:** "What is the aim of the Electricity Act, 2003? Explain reforms brought by the Electricity Act, 2003 in the areas of — Generation, Transmission & Distribution. — Reforms in Tariff principles & the Role of CEA."

**Topic:** Indian power-sector legislation.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
**Aim:** Consolidate the laws relating to generation, transmission, distribution, trading and use of electricity; promote competition; protect consumer interests; ensure supply of electricity to all areas; rationalise electricity tariffs; ensure transparent policies regarding subsidies; and provide for regulatory bodies.

**Generation, Transmission & Distribution reforms:**

- *Generation:* Delicensed — no licence needed to generate (thermal, gas, etc.); only hydro requires Techno-Economic Clearance/concurrence from CEA. Captive generation freely permitted.
- *Transmission:* Independent transmission utilities (CTU/STU) mandated; open access to transmission networks introduced to enable competitive trade.
- *Distribution:* Open access in distribution to be introduced progressively; distribution licensing with provision for multiple licences/franchisees to inject competition; standards of performance enforced.

**Tariff principles & Role of CEA:**

- *Tariff:* Determined by Appropriate Commissions guided by principles of cost of supply, reward of efficiency, safeguarding consumer interest and recovery of cost of electricity; progressive reduction of cross-subsidies; transparent subsidies only to be paid from budgets; tariff policy framed under Section 61–64; appeals via APTEL.
- *CEA:* Chief technical adviser to the Government; develops the National Electricity Plan; sets grid standards, safety and construction standards; concurrence for hydro schemes; advises regulators on technical matters.

**Final answer:** Act aims at consolidation, competition, universal supply and rational tariffs; generation delicensed, transmission/distribution opened via open access, tariffs made cost-based under independent regulation with CEA as apex technical body.

**Verification:** Every requested area (aim, G/T&D, tariff, CEA) addressed; mark value (06) legible.

### 2B — Salt-washing mass balance · (04 Marks)

**Source wording:** "Salt in crude oil must be removed before the oil can undergo processing in a refinery. The crude oil is fed to a washing unit where a freshwater feed to the unit mixes with the oil and dissolves a portion of the salt contained in the oil. The oil (containing some salt, but no water) is removed from the top of the washer, while the water (containing salt, but no oil) is removed from the bottom. If the spent wash water contains 15% salt and the crude oil contains 5% salt, determine the final concentration of the output products if the ratio of crude oil (with salt) to water is 4:1."

**Topic:** Material balance (leaching/extraction).

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
*Assumptions:* Percentages are by mass; all freshwater exits via the bottom stream; all oil exits via the top; steady state.

*Basis:* 400 kg crude oil (with salt) + 100 kg freshwater (ratio 4:1).

- Salt in crude = 0.05 × 400 = 20 kg → dry oil = 380 kg.
- Bottom stream: water = 100 kg = 85% of stream → total bottom = 100/0.85 = 117.65 kg → salt in bottom = 17.65 kg.
- Salt balance: salt to top oil = 20 − 17.65 = 2.35 kg.
- Top stream = 380 + 2.35 = 382.35 kg → salt concentration = 2.35/382.35 = 0.615 wt%.

**Final answer:** Washed oil leaves the top containing ≈ **0.62 wt% salt** (2.35 kg salt in 382.35 kg); spent wash water leaves the bottom at its stated **15 wt% salt** (17.65 kg salt in 117.65 kg).

**Verification:** Overall mass: in 500 kg = out 382.35 + 117.65 ✓; salt: in 20 kg = 2.35 + 17.65 ✓.

### 3B — Ten steps of detailed energy audit · (05 Marks)

**Source wording:** "With respect to detailed Energy Audit methodology, explain in details, the ten steps of this audit along with the plan of action and its purpose."

**Topic:** Detailed energy-audit methodology.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:** Step / plan of action / purpose:

1. **Plan and organise** — form audit team, define scope, schedule — ensures resources and commitment.
2. **Walk-through / informal interviews** — site familiarisation, discussion with managers/operators — identifies obvious wastes and priorities.
3. **Conduct preliminary survey and data collection** — collect utility bills, layout, process flow diagrams — establishes baseline structure of energy use.
4. **Conduct detailed survey/monitoring** — instrument measurements (power, flow, temperature, combustion analysis) — quantifies actual energy flows.
5. **Conduct more comprehensive evaluation / comparisons** — energy and mass balances against design/norms — locates inefficiencies.
6. **Analysis of energy use** — break down consumption by section/process, specific energy consumption trends — pinpoints major cost centres.
7. **Identify and develop energy-conservation opportunities (ECOs)** — brainstorm/list technical measures — generates improvement options.
8. **Cost-benefit analysis** — estimate savings, capital cost, payback/ROI/NPV for each ECO — ranks options economically.
9. **Reporting and presentation to management** — document findings, prioritised action plan with economics — secures approval and funding.
10. **Implementation and follow-up** — execute approved measures, monitor results (M&T/CUSUM) — verifies savings and sustains gains.

**Final answer:** The ten-step cycle above, from planning through follow-up.

**Verification:** All ten steps with action and purpose enumerated, matching the five-mark demand.

### 5A — Pipeline-insulation fuel saving · (05 Marks)

**Source wording:** "A power plant which operates for 8400 hours annually, has a boiler of 80% efficiency which uses fuel with a GCV of 10300 kCal/kg. Steam produced is sent to the turbine through steam pipeline of 100 mm diameter, which is not insulated for 100 metre length. Find out the fuel savings if it is properly insulated with 65 mm insulating material. Fuel Oil cost – Rs.15000/tonne; Surface temperature without insulation – 170 °C; Surface temperature after insulation – 65 °C; Ambient temperature – 25 °C."

**Topic:** Heat-loss reduction by insulation; fuel-saving economics.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
*Stated assumptions (paper permits assuming missing data):* horizontal pipe in calm indoor air; bare surface emissivity ε = 0.9 (oxidised/ painted steel); heat loss taken from the *given* surface temperatures; boiler efficiency and GCV convert heat saved to fuel saved.

Surface area: A = πDL = π × 0.1 × 100 = 31.42 m².

**Bare surface (Ts = 170 °C, ΔT = 145 °C):**
Natural convection (film ≈ 97.5 °C): h_c ≈ 7.9 W/m²·K; radiation: h_r ≈ εσ(T_s⁴ − T_a⁴)/ΔT ≈ 10.8 W/m²·K → h ≈ 18.7 W/m²·K.
q_bare ≈ 18.7 × 145 ≈ 2715 W/m² ≈ **2334 kCal/h·m²**.

**Insulated surface (Ts = 65 °C, ΔT = 40 °C):**
h_c ≈ 6.1 W/m²·K; h_r ≈ 6.6 W/m²·K → h ≈ 12.7 W/m²·K.
q_ins ≈ 12.7 × 40 ≈ 507 W/m² ≈ **436 kCal/h·m²**.

**Saving:**
Δq = 2334 − 436 ≈ 1898 kCal/h·m² → ΔQ = 1898 × 31.42 ≈ 59,640 kCal/h.
Annual heat saved = 59,640 × 8400 ≈ 5.01 × 10⁸ kCal/y.
Fuel saved = 5.01 × 10⁸ / (10,300 × 0.80) ≈ 60,800 kg ≈ **60.8 tonnes/y**.
Monetary saving = 60.8 × 15,000 ≈ **Rs 9.12 lakh per year**.

**Final answer:** ≈ 5.0 × 10⁸ kCal/yr heat saved → ≈ 60.8 t fuel oil/yr → ≈ Rs 9.1 lakh/yr. (Exact figure depends on the assumed emissivity/correlation; magnitude is robust.)

**Verification:** Back-check: 60,800 kg × 8240 kCal/kg ≈ 5.01 × 10⁸ kCal ✓; bare loss > insulated loss ✓; unit chain (W→kCal/h, kCal→kg fuel) consistent.

### 6A — Regression, correlation & CUSUM · (07 Marks)

**Source wording:** "Scooter Oil Mills, Brahmavar, during a monitoring program produced the following data. Derive the equation for the best fit straight line which describes the production to energy use. Calculate the Pearson's Correlation Coefficient. Perform a CUSUM analysis and comment on the energy consumption pattern."

| Month | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Production (Ton/Month) | 480 | 540 | 560 | 620 | 420 | 520 | 640 | 620 | 600 |
| Energy Use (TOE/Month) | 440 | 540 | 580 | 480 | 420 | 400 | 580 | 424 | 420 |

**Topic:** Energy modelling — least-squares regression, Pearson r, CUSUM control.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
Model: E = a + b·P (energy use TOE vs production T). n = 9; ΣP = 5000; ΣE = 4284; ΣPE = 2,395,680; ΣP² = 2,820,800; ΣE² = 2,080,976.

- S_PP = ΣP² − (ΣP)²/n = 2,820,800 − 25,000,000/9 = **43,022.2**
- S_PE = ΣPE − (ΣP)(ΣE)/n = 2,395,680 − 21,420,000/9 = **15,680**
- S_EE = ΣE² − (ΣE)²/n = 2,080,976 − 18,352,656/9 = **41,792**

Slope b = S_PE/S_PP = 15,680/43,022.2 = **0.3645 TOE/Ton**
Intercept a = Ē − b·P̄ = 476 − 0.3645 × 555.56 = **273.5 TOE**

**Best-fit line: E = 273.5 + 0.3645·P**

**Pearson r** = S_PE/√(S_PP·S_EE) = 15,680/√(43,022.2 × 41,792) = 15,680/42,403 = **0.370** (r² ≈ 0.14).

### 6B — Time Value of Money; Payback vs ROI vs NPV · (03 Marks)

**Source wording:** "What is the meaning of 'Time Value of Money'? Compare simple Payback period, ROI and NPV techniques."

**Topic:** Investment-appraisal fundamentals.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**
**Time Value of Money:** A rupee received today is worth more than the same rupee later because it can earn a return meanwhile (interest/opportunity cost), and future money carries inflation and risk. Hence cash flows must be discounted before comparison.

**Comparison:**

- **Simple Payback Period:** Time to recover initial investment = Investment ÷ Annual savings. Simple and quick screening tool; ignores time value of money entirely and ignores all cash flows after payback.
- **ROI (Return on Investment):** Average annual net return as % of investment = (Net annual savings/Investment) × 100. Shows profitability rate; still ignores the *timing* of cash flows and money's time value.
- **NPV (Net Present Value):** Sum of all project cash flows discounted at a chosen rate minus initial investment: NPV = −C₀ + Σ CFₜ/(1+i)ᵗ. Fully respects time value over project life; accepts projects with NPV > 0 and ranks alternatives correctly; needs a discount-rate choice and more data/effort than the other two.

**Final answer:** TVM = today's money outweighs future money; payback is quickest but crudest, ROI adds profitability percentage but ignores timing, NPV is the rigorous discounted criterion (accept if NPV > 0).

**Verification:** All three techniques contrasted on time-value treatment as asked; marks (03) legible.
