## Question 1

**1A** *(03)*
- **Source wording:** Explain the three classification of energy, with examples.
- **Topic:** Basics of energy — classification.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Primary energy (coal, crude oil, natural gas, solar, wind, biomass); secondary energy (electricity, steam, petrol, coke, hydrogen); final/useful energy delivered at point of use (light, heat, mechanical work).
- **Final answer:** Three classes with examples as above.
- **Verification:** Matches standard BEE/GCPC classification scheme; examples are conventional textbook ones.

**1B** *(04)*
- **Source wording:** With a neat flow chart, explain briefly the various steps of Energy Action Planning.
- **Topic:** Energy management systems — action planning.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Flow chart steps: top-management commitment → appoint energy manager/energy team → conduct preliminary walk-through audit → set baseline and targets → prepare action plan (no-cost/low-cost first) → implement measures → monitor & verify (M&V) → review, report and re-plan.
- **Final answer:** Closed-loop plan–do–check–act cycle as above with flow chart.
- **Verification:** Sequence consistent with ISO 50001-style PDCA and standard EA course notes.

**1C** *(03)*
- **Source wording:** With respect to Energy Conservation Act, 2001, define (i) Building, (ii) Energy Audit. List any four duties of a designated consumer.
- **Topic:** EC Act 2001 — definitions and designated consumers.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Building: a structure or erection used for commercial/industrial purposes with connected load/power exceeding thresholds notified by the Central Government. Energy Audit: verification, monitoring and analysis of use of energy including submission of technical report containing conclusions and recommendations for improving energy efficiency with cost benefit analysis. Four duties of a designated consumer: designate/appoint certified Energy Manager; get energy audit conducted by accredited auditor; maintain prescribed records; submit prescribed information to designated agency; comply with energy consumption norms/targets.
- **Final answer:** Definitions + any four duties as listed.
- **Verification:** Definitions paraphrase Sections 2 of the EC Act 2001; duties mirror Section 14 read with rules.

**2B** *(05)*

- **Topic:** Electricity Act 2003 — sector reforms and CEA role.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Generation: de-licensing (thermal/captive), open access. - **Verification:** Summarises provisions of Electricity Act 2003 (Sections 3, 7, 9, 61–64, roles under Section 73).

## Question 3

**3A** *(05)*
- **Source wording:** What are the laws of conservation? Skim milk is prepared by the removal of some of the fat from whole milk. This skim milk is found to contain 90.5% water, 3.5% protein, 5.1% carbohydrate, 0.1% fat and 0.8% ash. If the original milk contained 4.5% fat, calculate its composition assuming that only fat was removed to make the skim milk and that there are no losses in the process. (05)
- **Topic:** Material balance / law of conservation of mass.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Basis 100 kg skim milk → non-fat constituents = 99.9 kg pass unchanged from whole milk. Whole milk non-fat fraction = 95.5%, so whole-milk basis = 99.9/0.955 = 104.61 kg. Fat in whole milk = 4.5% × 104.61 = 4.71 kg (4.61 kg removed, 0.1 kg remains). Composition of whole milk: water 90.5/104.61 = 86.51%; protein 3.5/104.61 = 3.35%; carbohydrate 5.1/104.61 = 4.88%; fat 4.50%; ash 0.8/104.61 = 0.76%.
- **Final answer:** Whole (original) milk ≈ water 86.5%, protein 3.35%, carbohydrate 4.88%, fat 4.5%, ash 0.76%.
- **Verification:** Components sum to 100% (86.51+3.35+4.88+4.50+0.76 = 100.00); mass balance closes (104.61 − 4.61 removed = 100 kg skim).

**3B** *(03)*
- **Source wording:** Explain how Force Field Analysis helps in energy policy & planning. Briefly discuss its methodology.
- **Topic:** Management tools for policy/planning — Force Field Analysis.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** It identifies driving forces supporting a planned change (e.g., savings, incentives) versus restraining forces resisting it (costs, inertia, skills gap), enabling planners to strengthen drivers and weaken barriers. Methodology: define the change/goal → list driving forces → list restraining forces → score each for strength → act to reinforce drivers/mitigate restrainers → monitor equilibrium shift.
- **Final answer:** Lewin-type force field method applied to energy policy decisions as described.
- **Verification:** Standard Kurt Lewin force-field methodology as taught in EA courses.

## Question 4

**4A** *(06)*
- **Source wording:** A plant runs 8000 h/yr with an 80%-efficient boiler producing 15 kg/cm² steam. Pipeline diameter 100 mm, total length 300 m; 150 m uninsulated with surface temperature 195 °C. Proposal: insulate this length with 70 mm glass wool + aluminium cladding reducing surface temperature to 70 °C. Fuel Rs 15/kg, GCV 14250 kCal/kg, ambient 25 °C, insulation Rs 4000 per m². Find fuel and cost savings and payback period.
- **Topic:** Thermal insulation economics / heat loss estimation.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Assumption: bare-surface heat-loss correlation q = [10 + (Ts − Ta)/20] × (Ts − Ta) kcal/h·m²; pipe area π×0.1×150 = 47.12 m² (same area taken before/after). Uninsulated: ΔT=170 → q₁ = 18.5×170 = 3145 kcal/h·m² → Q₁ = 148,205 kcal/h. Insulated: ΔT=45 → q₂ = 12.25×45 = 551.25 → Q₂ = 25,977 kcal/h. Heat saved = 122,228 kcal/h. Fuel saved = 122,228/(14250×0.80) = 10.72 kg/h → 85,774 kg/yr (~85.8 t/yr). Cost saving = 85,774×15 ≈ Rs 12.87 lakh/yr. Investment = 47.12 m²×4000 ≈ Rs 1.88 lakh. Payback = 188,496/1,286,606 = 0.147 yr.
- **Final answer:** Fuel saving ≈ 10.7 kg/h (≈86 t/yr); saving ≈ Rs 12.9 lakh/yr; payback ≈ 0.15 yr ≈ 1.8 months.
- **Verification:** q₁≈3145 kcal/h·m² agrees with convection+radiation estimate for a 195 °C surface; payback < 2 months is typical for glass-wool pipe insulation retrofits.

**5B** *(03)*
- **Source wording:** During May-2013 a plant recorded maximum demand 900 kVA at average PF 0.8 lag; minimum required PF 0.95 lag; every dip of 0.5% attracts penalty of Rs 10,000/month. Calculate improvement in PF for June 2013 by installing 150 kVAR capacitors, and penalty payable for June 2013.
- **Topic:** Power factor correction and penalty computation.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** kW = 900×0.8 = 720 kW. Initial kVAR = 720×tan(cos⁻¹0.8) = 720×0.75 = 540 kVAR. After 150 kVAR compensation: net kVAR = 390 → tan φ′ = 390/720 = 0.5417 → cos φ′ ≈ 0.879 ≈ 0.88 lag. Improvement ≈ 0.08 (0.80→0.88). Penalty: shortfall vs 0.95 = 0.95 − 0.879 = 7.03 percentage points; slabs of 0.5% (or part thereof) require 15 slabs, because ceil(7.03/0.5) = 15 → penalty = 15×10,000 = Rs 1,50,000.
- **Final answer:** Improved PF ≈ 0.88 lag (improvement ≈ 0.08); June penalty ≈ Rs 1.5 lakh (Rs 1.4 lakh if only complete half-percent dips are counted).
- **Verification:** Vector triangle closes (720 kW, 390 kVAR → 818 kVA, PF 0.879); uncorrected case would give 30 slabs (Rs 3 lakh), confirming capacitors halve the penalty.

Its energy consumption and production data was collected for the duration April, 2012 to Sept, 2013.   Using Linear Regression Analysis, derive the equation for standard energy consumption. Compute the Pearson’s Correlation Co-efficient. Perform CUSUM analysis to calculate the energy savings due to installation of energy efficient motors & waste heat recovery systems. (08)
- **Topic:** Regression SEC model, correlation coefficient, CUSUM savings.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Add HSD, electricity, and coal to obtain monthly energy consumption $Y$ in toe/month. Use the eight pre-retrofit observations (April–November 2012) to establish the standard-consumption baseline, because the source states that the motors and waste-heat-recovery systems were installed in December 2012. For those eight observations, $\bar X = 437.5$ tonnes/month and $\bar Y = 370.5$ toe/month. Least-squares regression gives

  $$E = a+bP = 159.7914051 + 0.47590536P\quad\text{toe/month},$$

  where $P$ is monthly production in tonnes. The Pearson correlation for the eight baseline points is $r = 0.82111859$.

  For CUSUM, calculate $D=Y_\text{actual}-Y_\text{standard}$ for each post-retrofit month and accumulate from zero at December 2012. A negative cumulative value means that actual consumption is below the baseline; report savings as $-D$.

| Month | Production (t) | Actual $Y$ (toe) | Standard $E$ (toe) | $D=Y-E$ (toe) | Post-retrofit CUSUM (toe) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Jan 2013 | 560 | 400 | 426.298 | −26.298 | −51.633 |
| Feb 2013 | 440 | 360 | 369.190 | −9.190 | −60.823 |
| Mar 2013 | 360 | 330 | 331.117 | −1.117 | −61.940 |
| April 2013 | 420 | 340 | 359.672 | −19.672 | −81.612 |
| May 2013 | 420 | 340 | 359.672 | −19.672 | −101.283 |

The post-retrofit residual sum is $-185.07870594$ toe, so the source-derived cumulative saving is approximately $185.08$ toe over the ten post-retrofit months (about $18.51$ toe/month on this baseline).
- **Final answer:** Standard energy-consumption equation: $E(\text{toe/month}) = 159.7914051 + 0.47590536P(\text{tonnes/month})$. Pearson $r = 0.82111859$. CUSUM indicates approximately $185.08$ toe of cumulative post-December-2012 saving under the pre-retrofit baseline.
- **Verification:** The baseline regression uses only the eight April–November 2012 points, while the CUSUM table uses all ten December 2012–September 2013 points exactly once. Each table residual is actual minus predicted; the final CUSUM equals the sum of the ten residuals, $-185.07870594$ toe, so the positive saving is $185.07870594$ toe. This interpretation is a worked solution, not an official answer.

**6B** *(02)*
- **Source wording:** A shell & tube heat exchanger is cooled with a stream of demineralized water. Evaluate total heat rejected to cooling water (kCal/hr), if water flow rate is 200 m³/hr and temperature rise is 7 °C.
- **Topic:** Sensible heat duty calculation.
- **Worked solution - not an official answer:** Water mass flow = 200 m³/hr × 1000 kg/m³ = 200,000 kg/hr; cp ≈ 1 kCal/kg°C. Q = m·cp·ΔT = 200,000×1×7 = 1,400,000 kCal/hr.
- **Final answer:** Q ≈ 1.4 × 10⁶ kCal/hr (= 1400 Mcal/hr ≈ 1628 kW).
- **Verification:** Unit check kg/hr×kcal/kg·°C×°C = kcal/hr ✓; power equivalent 1.4e6/860 ≈ 1628 kW consistent.
