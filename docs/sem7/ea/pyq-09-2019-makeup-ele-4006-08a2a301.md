## Question 1

### 1A (05 marks)

**Source wording:** What is the aim of the Electricity Act, 2003? Explain, by clearly bringing out the significance of reforms brought by the Electricity Act, 2003 in the areas of
- Generation, Transmission & Distribution.
- Reforms in Tariff principles & the Role of CEA

**Topic:** Electricity legislation and sector reform.

Worked solution - not an official answer: Aim: to consolidate and amend the law relating to generation, transmission, distribution, trading and use of electricity; promote competition; protect consumer interests; rationalise tariffs; make subsidy policy transparent; ensure supply of electricity to all areas; and encourage efficiency and environmentally benign policies. Significance of reforms:
- *Generation*: de-licensed (no licence needed except hydro concurrence); free entry incl. captive generation — expands capacity via private investment.
- *Transmission*: independent transmission licensees; State/Central Transmission Utilities; mandatory open access — enables competitive bulk trade.
- *Distribution*: licensed distribution with progressive open access; possibility of multiple licensees; trading recognised as a distinct activity; SEB unbundling/corporatisation.
- *Tariff principles (Sec. 61)*: safeguard consumer interests; recovery of cost of electricity in a reasonable manner; reward efficiency in performance; subsidies must be explicit, budgeted and paid upfront; independent SERCs fix tariffs with Appellate Tribunal appeal.
- *CEA role*: apex technical adviser — prepares National Electricity Plan, prescribes grid/safety standards, advises Government, promotes measures for efficient, economical and reliable systems.

**Final answer:** As outlined above (descriptive; 05 marks).
**Verification:** Content consistent with the Act's objects and standard Energy-Auditing syllabus treatment; no numerical claim to verify.

## Question 2

### 2A (04 marks)

**Source wording:** Skim milk is prepared by the removal of some of the fat from whole milk. This skim milk is found to contain 90.5% water, 3.5% protein, 5.1% carbohydrate, 0.1% fat and 0.8% ash. If the original milk contained 4.5% fat, calculate its composition assuming that only fat was removed to make the skim milk and that there are no losses in the process. 04

**Topic:** Material balance (mass balance) — separation process.

Worked solution - not an official answer: Basis 100 kg whole milk. Only pure fat leaves, so all non-fat material passes to skim milk.
Skim milk non-fat fraction = 100 − 0.1 = 99.9%; whole-milk non-fat = 95.5 kg.
Mass balance: 0.999·S = 95.5 → S = 95.6 kg skim from 100 kg whole milk (fat removed ≈ 4.4 kg).
Each component in whole milk = skim % × 95.6/100:
- Water = 90.5 × 0.956 = 86.51%
- Protein = 3.5 × 0.956 = 3.35%
- Carbohydrate = 5.1 × 0.956 = 4.88%
- Ash = 0.8 × 0.956 = 0.76%
- Fat = 4.50% (given)

**Final answer:** Whole milk composition ≈ water 86.51%, protein 3.35%, carbohydrate 4.88%, fat 4.5%, ash 0.76% (skim yield 95.6 kg per 100 kg whole milk).
**Verification:** 86.51 + 3.35 + 4.88 + 4.5 + 0.76 = 100.00%; non-fat recovered = 0.999 × 95.6 = 95.5 kg = input non-fat. Balanced.

### 2C (04 marks)

**Source wording:** What is an energy efficient motor? Explain how the intrinsic losses are reduced in an energy efficiency induction motor. (04)

**Topic:** Energy-efficient motors — loss reduction techniques.

Worked solution - not an official answer: An energy-efficient motor is one designed and manufactured to deliver the same output at 2–7% higher efficiency than a standard motor by minimising internal losses. Loss-reduction measures:
- *Stator I²R*: increased copper cross-section, improved slot fill/design.
- *Rotor I²R*: larger, higher-conductivity rotor bars; optimised rotor slots.
- *Iron (core) losses*: low-loss high-grade silicon steel laminations, thinner laminations, longer core length.
- *Friction & windage*: premium bearings, aerodynamically improved fans.
- *Stray load losses*: optimised air gap, slot geometry and insulation.
Benefits: lower running temperature, longer insulation life, higher overload margin, better tolerance to voltage variation.

**Final answer:** Definition plus loss-wise measures as above (descriptive; 04 marks).
**Verification:** All five classical loss categories addressed; consistent with motor-loss theory (input = output + stator + rotor + iron + friction/windage + stray losses).

## Question 3

### 3A (04 marks)

**Source wording:** Explain the methodology followed for carrying out assessment of lighting systems employed in Industries. Also, explain how reduction in lighting feeder voltage is a tool for energy conservation? (04)

**Topic:** Lighting-system energy assessment and voltage-reduction savings.

Worked solution - not an official answer: Methodology: (1) compile inventory — luminaire type, lamp wattage, ballast, mounting, operating hours; (2) measure illuminance on a grid at the working plane with a calibrated lux meter; (3) compute average illuminance, uniformity ratio (min/avg) and connected load/LPD (W/m²); (4) benchmark against task requirements and norms (e.g., IS 3646 / ECBC); (5) identify savings — LED/T5 retrofits, electronic ballasts, daylight utilisation, occupancy/timer controls, switching segmentation — and quantify kWh savings.
Voltage reduction: reducing the lighting feeder voltage cuts lamp power draw (for incandescent/resistive lamps power falls roughly as V^1.5–1.6), lowers filament/gas-discharge losses and greatly extends lamp life; with modern regulator/stabiliser-based feeders, excess mains voltage can be trimmed to the optimum level while maintaining required lux levels, giving direct energy savings.

**Final answer:** Assessment procedure plus voltage-trimming mechanism as above (descriptive; 04 marks).
**Verification:** Both halves of the question addressed; power–voltage dependence quoted is the standard approximate exponent for filament lamps.

### 3B (04 marks)

**Source wording:** A centrifugal water pump operates at 36 m3/hr and at 1800 RPM. The pump operating efficiency is 65% and motor efficiency is 89%. The discharge pressure gauge shows 3 kg/cm2. The suction is 3 m below the pump centerline. If the speed of the pump is reduced by 20 %, estimate the following:
 a) pump flow,
 b) pump head
 c) motor power.
Assume motor and pump efficiency remains same at the reduced speed. (04)

**Topic:** Pump affinity laws and pump power calculations.

Worked solution - not an official answer: Initial head: 3 kg/cm² ≈ 30 m water column; suction 3 m below centreline adds 3 m static lift → H₁ = 30 + 3 = 33 m. Speed ratio k = 0.8.
a) Flow: Q₂ = k·Q₁ = 0.8 × 36 = **28.8 m³/hr**.
b) Head: H₂ = k²·H₁ = 0.64 × 33 = **21.12 m**.
c) Motor power (∝ N³): initial input power P₁ = ρgQ₁H₁/(η_p·η_m) = (1000 × 9.81 × (36/3600) × 33)/(0.65 × 0.89) = 3237/0.5785 ≈ 5596 W.
P₂ = 0.8³ × P₁ = 0.512 × 5596 ≈ **2.86 kW** (motor input; corresponding shaft power ≈ 2.55 kW).

**Final answer:** a) 28.8 m³/hr; b) 21.12 m; c) ≈ 2.86 kW motor input power.
**Verification:** Direct recomputation at new duty: ρgQ₂H₂/(η_pη_m) = (1000×9.81×0.008×21.12)/0.5785 = 1657/0.5785 ≈ 2864 W ✓; exponents 1, 2, 3 match affinity-law theory.

### 3C (02 marks)

**Source wording:** Explain any two energy conservation methods applicable to centrifugal pumps. (02)

**Topic:** Pump energy-saving options.

Worked solution - not an official answer (any two):
1. *Speed control (VFD) instead of throttling*: since power ∝ N³, matching speed to demand avoids throttle-valve head losses and gives large part-load savings.
2. *Impeller trimming / correct sizing*: machining the impeller to the actual duty point reduces head, flow and power permanently without valve losses.
(Other acceptable answers: restoring wear-ring/clearance maintenance to cut recirculation; operating near BEP; reducing system piping losses; proper multiple-pump sequencing.)

**Final answer:** Any two methods explained as above (descriptive; 02 marks).
**Verification:** Each cited method follows from affinity-law/system-curve reasoning.

## Question 4

### 4A (05 marks)

**Source wording:** A PV array of 500W has been installed to pump water from bore well of 2 meters deep, using a submersible motor & pump system to an overhead tank. The length of the pipe required to pump the water is 30 m. Following are the costs involved for sub systems and their life spans:
 - PV array- INR 400/watt, Life span = 15 yrs
 - Motor & Pump system- Rs 100/watt, Life span – 8 years
 - Pipe cost- Rs 400/m; Life span – 5 yrs
 - Cost of digging bore well – Rs 500/m
 - Maintenance cost – Rs 3000/year
 - Misc. cost: Rs 100/Watt
If interest rate is 10% and inflation is 6%, calculate Life cycle cost of the system if the project period is of 15 years. (05)

**Topic:** Life-cycle costing (LCC) of a PV pumping system.

Worked solution - not an official answer: Assumptions: costs at 500 W rating; bore-well digging = 500 × 2 = Rs 1000 (one-time); misc. = 100 × 500 = Rs 50,000; replacements at end of each subsystem life; real discount rate i′ = (1.10/1.06) − 1 = 3.774% p.a.; salvage nil.
Initial capital = 500×400 + 500×100 + 30×400 + 1000 + 50,000 = 200,000 + 50,000 + 12,000 + 1,000 + 50,000 = **Rs 313,000**.
Present worth of replacements (discounted at i′):
- Motor & pump at year 8: 50,000/(1.03774)⁸ = 50,000 × 0.7435 = Rs 37,176
- Pipe at years 5 & 10: 12,000 × [(1.03774)⁻⁵ + (1.03774)⁻¹⁰] = 12,000 × (0.8309 + 0.6905) = Rs 18,257
Maintenance: 3000 × [1 − (1.03774)⁻¹⁵]/0.03774 = 3000 × 11.296 = Rs 33,888.
LCC = 313,000 + 37,176 + 18,257 + 33,888 ≈ **Rs 402,321 ≈ Rs 4.02 lakh**.

**Final answer:** Life-cycle cost ≈ Rs 4.02 lakh (≈ Rs 402,000) over the 15-year project period.
**Verification:** Sum of components 313,000 + 37,176 + 18,257 + 33,888 = 402,321 ✓; inflating at 6% and discounting at 10% is mathematically identical to discounting real costs at 3.774%, confirming the single-rate treatment.

### 4B (05 marks)

**Source wording:** What is the 5S management tool? Explain in details, clearly bringing out the significance of each S, its implementation technique and potential benefits. (05)

**Topic:** 5S workplace management tool.

Worked solution - not an official answer: 5S is a Japanese lean-workplace organisation methodology creating orderly, safe, efficient workspaces — often the first step of energy/quality improvement programmes.
1. *Seiri (Sort)*: remove unneeded items; red-tag review. Significance: clears clutter; frees space.
2. *Seiton (Set in order)*: a place for everything, labelled and arranged for use frequency; shadow boards, floor marking. Significance: minimum search time, safer access.
3. *Seiso (Shine)*: clean equipment/workplace while inspecting; cleaning schedules. Significance: leaks, loose connections and abnormal wear are detected early — directly supports energy-loss spotting.
4. *Seiketsu (Standardise)*: written standards, checklists, colour codes sustaining first three S's. Significance: consistency across shifts/departments.
5. *Shitsuke (Sustain)*: discipline through training, audits, visual management, recognition. Significance: makes gains permanent.
Benefits: improved productivity and safety, less waste/idling time, early detection of energy and material losses, better morale and audit readiness.

**Final answer:** Definition plus five-S explanation as above (descriptive; 05 marks).
**Verification:** All five S's covered with significance, technique and benefits, as demanded by the question.

## Question 5

### 5A (07 marks)

**Source wording:** Scooter Oil Mills, Brahmavar, during a monitoring program produced the following data. Derive the equation for the best fit straight line which describes the production to energy use. Calculate the Pearson's Correlation Coefficient. Perform a CUSUM analysis and comment on the energy consumption pattern.

| Month | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Production (Ton/Month) | 480 | 540 | 560 | 620 | 420 | 520 | 640 | 620 | 600 |
| Energy Use (TOE/Month) | 440 | 540 | 580 | 480 | 420 | 400 | 580 | 424 | 420 |

(07)

**Topic:** Regression (production–energy line), Pearson correlation, CUSUM analysis.

Worked solution - not an official answer: With X = production, Y = energy use, n = 9: ΣX = 5000 (X̄ = 555.56), ΣY = 4284 (Ȳ = 476), ΣXY = 2,395,680, ΣX² = 2,820,800, ΣY² = 2,080,976.
Sxy = 2,395,680 − 9(555.56)(476) = 15,680; Sxx = 2,820,800 − 9(555.56)² = 43,022.2; Syy = 2,080,976 − 9(476)² = 41,792.
Slope b = Sxy/Sxx = 15,680/43,022.2 = 0.3645; intercept a = Ȳ − bX̄ = 476 − 0.3645×555.56 = 273.5.
Best-fit line: **E = 0.3645·P + 273.5 (TOE/month)**.
Pearson coefficient: r = Sxy/√(Sxx·Syy) = 15,680/√(43,022.2 × 41,792) = 15,680/42,403 = **0.370** (weak positive correlation).
CUSUM (deviation = actual − predicted; cumulative):

| Month | Prod. (t) | Actual E | Predicted E | Deviation | CUSUM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 480 | 440 | 448.5 | −8.5 | −8.5 |
| 2 | 540 | 540 | 470.3 | +69.7 | 61.2 |
| 3 | 560 | 580 | 477.6 | +102.4 | 163.5 |
| 4 | 620 | 480 | 499.5 | −19.5 | 144.0 |
| 5 | 420 | 420 | 426.6 | −6.6 | 137.4 |
| 6 | 520 | 400 | 463.1 | −63.1 | 74.4 |
| 7 | 640 | 580 | 506.8 | +73.2 | 147.6 |
| 8 | 620 | 424 | 499.5 | −75.5 | 72.0 |
| 9 | 600 | 420 | 492.2 | −72.2 | ≈ 0 |

Comment: r ≈ 0.37 is weak, so production alone poorly explains energy use (scattered operation/other drivers). CUSUM rises steeply in months 2–3 (consumption far above the baseline → poorer energy performance period), stays positive through month 8, then falls to ≈ 0 by month 9; the falling trend from month 4 onward indicates improvement relative to the fitted baseline. Changes in CUSUM slope mark shifts in plant energy efficiency.

**Final answer:** E = 0.3645·P + 273.5 TOE/month; r ≈ 0.37; CUSUM table and interpretation as above.
**Verification:** Σ deviations = 4284 − (9 × 476) ≈ 0, and closing CUSUM ≈ 0 confirms the regression passes through (X̄, Ȳ) ✓.

### 5B (03 marks)

**Source wording:** An energy auditor audits a 75 kW four pole 3 phase induction motor operating at 49.8 Hz and rated for 415 V, 100 A at 1440 RPM. The actual measured speed was 1470 RPM and the power analyzer recorded the applied voltage to be 428 V and drawing a current of 30 A.
 a) The auditor works out the percentage loading of the induction motor as a ratio of line current drawn to the rated current of the motor. Do you agree with the above methodology adopted by the auditor? Justify your answer with reasons.
 b) Determine the percentage loading of the motor for the given operating conditions. (03)

**Topic:** Induction motor loading estimation — current-ratio vs slip method.

Worked solution - not an official answer: a) No. At partial load a large share of stator current is magnetising current that produces no torque, current varies non-linearly with shaft load and the power factor drops markedly at light load; additionally the supply here deviates from nameplate conditions (428 V vs 415 V rated, 49.8 Hz vs 50 Hz), further invalidating a simple current ratio. The current-ratio result (30/100 = 30%) therefore misstates the true loading; the slip method is the appropriate technique.
b) Slip method: 4-pole machine → synchronous speed at 49.8 Hz = 120 × 49.8/4 = 1494 RPM. Measured slip = 1494 − 1470 = 24 RPM. Full-load slip = 1500 − 1440 = 60 RPM (at 50 Hz, 4%).
Percentage loading ≈ (operating slip)/(full-load slip) = 24/60 = **40%**.
(A voltage-corrected variant, loading ∝ slip × V², would give 40% × (428/415)² ≈ 43%.)

**Final answer:** a) Disagree — current ratio is unreliable at partial load (magnetising current, low PF, off-nominal voltage); b) loading ≈ 40% by the slip method (≈ 43% if voltage-corrected).
**Verification:** 1494 = 120×49.8/4 ✓; slip ratio 24/60 = 0.40 ✓; the slip-based value being higher than the 30% current-based figure is consistent with known behaviour of lightly loaded motors.
