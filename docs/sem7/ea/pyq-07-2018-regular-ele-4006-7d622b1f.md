## Question 1

### 1A — Fill in the blanks (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 1A. ___ is commercial energy
> ___ is a financial turnaround and revival package by Ministry of Power, Govt of India
> ___ in Rajasthan has a strategic oil reserve (05)
> ___ is an act of parliament of India, enacted to transform power sector
> National Action Plan on climate change has ___ missions

(The "(05)" token is printed within the block in the extraction; it applies to subpart 1A as a whole.)

**Topic:** Indian energy scene — commercial energy, power-sector policies.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

1. Commercial energy: **Electricity** (energy traded in markets; coal and petroleum products also qualify).
2. Financial turnaround and revival package by Ministry of Power: **UDAY (Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana)**.
3. Strategic oil reserve in Rajasthan: most plausibly intended **Barmer** (Rajasthan's crude-oil hub). Caveat: the formal ISPRL strategic reserves are at Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru and Padur — none in Rajasthan — so confirm this blank against class notes rather than treating it as settled.
4. Act of Parliament enacted to transform the power sector: **The Electricity Act, 2003**.
5. National Action Plan on Climate Change has **eight (8)** missions.

**Final answer:** Electricity; UDAY; Barmer (flagged ambiguous); Electricity Act 2003; 8 missions.

**Verification:** Items 1, 2, 4, 5 are standard, cross-checkable policy facts; item 3 is explicitly flagged above as the only uncertain blank.

### 1B — Energy content of natural gas in a spherical balloon (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 1B. A spherical balloon with a diameter of 5 mts is filled with natural gas. How much energy is contained in the quantity of natural gas (05)

**Topic:** Units, calorific value, volume–energy conversion.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

- Volume of sphere: V = (πD³)/6 = (π × 5³)/6 = 65.45 m³.
- Calorific value is not given. Per the paper's instruction "Missing data may be suitably assumed", take GCV of natural gas ≈ 36 MJ/m³.
- E = 65.45 m³ × 36 MJ/m³ ≈ **2,356 MJ ≈ 2.36 GJ ≈ 654 kWh**.

**Final answer:** ≈ 2.36 GJ (≈ 654 kWh) for 65.45 m³ of natural gas; scales linearly with whatever calorific value is assumed (e.g., ≈ 2,618 MJ at 40 MJ/m³).

**Verification:** V = (4/3)π(2.5)³ = 65.45 m³ ✓; MJ→kWh via 3.6 MJ/kWh ✓. Answer is assumption-dependent by design of the question.

## Question 2

### 2A — Technical & Economic feasibility of energy conservation opportunities (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 2A. Write a note on Technical & Economic feasibility of energy conservation opportunities (05)

**Topic:** Energy audit — screening and appraisal of ECMs.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

- **Technical feasibility:** whether proven technology/equipment exists and fits the plant — compatibility with existing process and utilities, space, reliability, safety, maintenance skills and spares, impact on product quality and output, interactions with other systems. Pilot trials or detailed engineering de-risk larger measures.
- **Economic feasibility:** quantified savings vs investment, appraised by simple payback, ROI, NPV, IRR and life-cycle costing; measures are ranked (short-payback items first) and checked for sensitivity to energy prices and load growth.
- An opportunity is implementable only when it clears **both** screens; a technically elegant but uneconomical measure (or vice versa) is rejected or deferred.

**Final answer:** Qualitative note covering both feasibility dimensions as above.

**Verification:** Standard energy-audit syllabus methodology; no numerical claims to verify.

### 2B — Energy Benchmarking (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 2B. Describe Energy Benchmarking and list out the steps in energy conservation benchmarking (05)

**Topic:** Performance comparison techniques.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

**Description:** Energy benchmarking compares the energy performance of a facility, process or equipment (via indicators such as specific energy consumption, kWh per kg of product) against its own past performance, peer plants, or best-available practice, to quantify gaps and set realistic improvement targets.

**Steps in energy conservation benchmarking:**

1. Select the performance indicator(s) (e.g., SEC, kWh/tonne).
2. Collect and normalise energy and production data.
3. Establish the benchmark (internal historic, industry average, or best-in-class).
4. Compare current performance against the benchmark and quantify the gap.
5. Investigate causes of the gap.
6. Set targets and draw up an action plan.
7. Implement, monitor, and review periodically (re-benchmark).

**Final answer:** As above (definition + seven steps).

**Verification:** Steps follow the accepted plan–do–check–act style benchmarking loop; internally consistent.

### 3B — Definitions (marks printed: 06)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 3B. Define (i) Capital Cost (ii) depreciation (iii) Salvage value (06)

**Topic:** Engineering-economics terminology.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

- **(i) Capital cost:** the total up-front expenditure required to acquire and commission an asset — purchase price plus transport, installation, civil/electrical works and commissioning.
- **(ii) Depreciation:** the systematic reduction in the book value of an asset over its useful life due to wear-and-tear and obsolescence, charged as an expense (e.g., straight-line or written-down-value method).
- **(iii) Salvage value:** the estimated residual (scrap/realisable) value of an asset at the end of its useful life.

**Final answer:** Three definitions as above.

**Verification:** Definitions mutually consistent (capital cost − total depreciation ≈ salvage value at end of life).

## Question 4

### 4A — Mixing / mass balance (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 4A. A solution containing 5 % solids is mixed with 20 % solid solution and a single output of 25 % is removed. If the 5 % solution enters at 10 Kg / sec, what are the other rates assuming that there is no accumulation (05)

**Topic:** Steady-state mass balance (no accumulation).

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

Let x = flow of the 20 % stream (kg/s), y = output flow (kg/s).

- Overall mass: 10 + x = y
- Solids: 0.05(10) + 0.20x = 0.25y

Substituting y: 0.5 + 0.2x = 2.5 + 0.25x ⟹ −2.0 = 0.05x ⟹ **x = −40 kg/s**, hence y = 10 − 40 = **−30 kg/s**.

Both results are negative, which is physically impossible. The reason: the printed output strength (25 %) exceeds **both** input strengths (5 % and 20 %); mixing two streams can never yield an output richer than the richest input. The data as printed are therefore internally inconsistent (an output strength below 20 %, e.g., 15 %, would give a feasible answer — no corrected value is invented here since the printed figure governs).

**Final answer:** Mathematically x = −40 kg/s and y = −30 kg/s; i.e., **no physically valid solution exists for the percentages exactly as printed**.

**Verification:** Bound check: any mixture of 5 % and 20 % feeds must lie in [5 %, 20 %]; 25 % lies outside ⟹ infeasible, confirming the algebra.

### 4B — Project selection by IRR (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 4B. Based on IRR, decide which project is to be preferred if the discount rate is 10 %

|                | Investment | 1     | 2     | 3     |
|----------------|-----------|-------|-------|-------|
| Project A      | 5,000     | 2,500 | 5,000 | 2,500 |
| Project B      | 8,000     | 5,000 | 2,500 | 5,000 |

(Marks "(05)" printed beside the table.)

**Topic:** Internal rate of return; accept/reject and ranking.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

Interpretation: Investment is the year-0 outflow; columns 1–3 are annual returns (same units).

- **Project A:** solve 2,500/(1+r) + 5,000/(1+r)² + 2,500/(1+r)³ = 5,000. Trials: NPV = +48 at 43 %, −15 at 44 % ⟹ IRR_A ≈ **43.8 %**.
- **Project B:** solve 5,000/(1+r) + 2,500/(1+r)² + 5,000/(1+r)³ = 8,000. Trials: NPV = +42 at 26 %, −72 at 27 % ⟹ IRR_B ≈ **26.4 %**.

Both IRRs far exceed the 10 % discount rate (both acceptable); rank by IRR ⟹ **prefer Project A**.

**Final answer:** Project A (IRR ≈ 43.8 % vs ≈ 26.4 %; hurdle 10 %).

**Verification:** Cross-check with NPV @ 10 %: A: −5,000 + 2,272.7 + 4,132.2 + 1,878.3 = **+3,283**; B: −8,000 + 4,545.5 + 2,066.1 + 3,756.6 = **+2,368**. NPV also favours A — decision consistent.

## Question 5

### 5A — Motor purchase: life-cycle cost comparison (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 5A. A motor replacement has the following strategy of purchase
> (a) Normal efficiency motor (Capital Cost = Rs 10,000, Annual Operating Cost = Rs 25,000)
> (b) Higher efficiency motor (Capital Cost = Rs 20,000, Annual Operating Cost = Rs 10,000) (05)
> For a 15 year motor life, 30 year investment horizon, inflation rate of 5 %, discount rate of 10 %, determine which purchase is beneficial

**Topic:** Life-cycle costing with inflation and discounting; replacement timing.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

Assumptions (permitted by the paper): operating costs escalate with the 5 % inflation rate, so costs are compared in constant (real) rupees at the real discount rate; each option buys a replacement motor at year 15 (15-year life, 30-year horizon).

- Real discount rate: r = 1.10/1.05 − 1 = 4.762 %
- P/A(r, 30 yr) = (1 − 1.04762⁻³⁰)/0.04762 = 15.799;  P/F(r, 15 yr) = 1/1.04762¹⁵ = 0.4977

**Option (a) Normal efficiency:**
LCC_a = 10,000 + 25,000 × 15.799 + 10,000 × 0.4977 = 10,000 + 394,964 + 4,977 ≈ **Rs 4,09,941**

**Option (b) Higher efficiency:**
LCC_b = 20,000 + 10,000 × 15.799 + 20,000 × 0.4977 = 20,000 + 157,985 + 9,954 ≈ **Rs 1,87,939**

**Final answer:** Buy the **higher-efficiency motor (b)**; present-worth saving ≈ Rs 2,22,002 (about 54 % lower life-cycle cost).

**Verification:** Robustness check — if inflation is ignored entirely and cash flows are discounted at 10 % nominal: LCC_a = 10,000 + 25,000×9.427 + 10,000×0.2394 ≈ Rs 2,48,067; LCC_b = 20,000 + 10,000×9.427 + 20,000×0.2394 ≈ Rs 1,19,057. Same decision, confirming (b) dominates under either convention.

### 5B — Present value of energy loss in a motor-pump system (marks printed: 05)

**Source wording (verbatim):**

> 5B. For the following motor-pump system, determine the present value of the energy loss: Fluid density = 1000 Kg / m3, Flow rate = 0.048 m3 / sec, Total Head = 60 mts, Motor input = 50 kW, Motor Efficiency = 85 %, Operating hours = 6500 hrs, System life = 10 years, Discount Rate = 10 %, Energy cost = Rs. 5 / kWh,

(The comma after "kWh" is the last character printed in the question block.)

**Topic:** Hydraulic power, loss quantification, present-worth of annual energy cost.

**Worked solution - not an official answer:**

- Ideal hydraulic power: P = ρgQH = 1000 × 9.81 × 0.048 × 60 = **28.25 kW**
- System energy loss (motor input − ideal hydraulic power; pump efficiency not given, so loss is taken against the ideal requirement): 50 − 28.25 = **21.75 kW**
- Annual loss energy: 21.75 × 6,500 = **1,41,375 kWh/yr** ⟹ annual cost = 1,41,375 × Rs 5 = **Rs 7,06,875/yr**
- P/A(10 %, 10 yr) = (1 − 1.1⁻¹⁰)/0.10 = 6.1446
- Present value of energy loss = 7,06,875 × 6.1446 ≈ **Rs 43,43,441 ≈ Rs 43.4 lakh**

**Final answer:** PV of the energy loss ≈ **Rs 43.4 lakh** over the 10-year life (annual loss 21.75 kW ≈ 1,41,375 kWh ≈ Rs 7.07 lakh/yr).

**Verification:** Decomposition check: PV(motor input energy) = 50 × 6,500 × 5 × 6.1446 ≈ Rs 99.85 lakh; PV(hydraulic energy) = 28.25 × 6,500 × 5 × 6.1446 ≈ Rs 56.41 lakh; difference = Rs 43.44 lakh ✓ matches. (Motor efficiency 85 % is consistent context data but does not alter the input-vs-hydraulic loss basis.)
