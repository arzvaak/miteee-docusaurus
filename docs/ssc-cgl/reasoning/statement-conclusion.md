## 1. Treat the Statement as the Whole Case

A conclusion follows only when it is supported by the statement without outside facts, personal belief, or a missing causal step.

![Inference map for statement-and-conclusion questions](/img/ssc-cgl/statement-conclusion-map.svg)
*Concept map: lock the statement's exact terms, test necessity, reject added assumptions, and decide each conclusion separately.*

| Conclusion wording | Strength | What must be shown | Common error |
|---|---|---|---|
| All / every | Universal | No exception is possible | Generalising from one or some |
| No / none | Universal negative | Complete exclusion | Treating absence of evidence as exclusion |
| Some / at least one | Particular | Existence is guaranteed | Assuming a class exists |
| Must / will | Necessary | Statement guarantees the outcome | Replacing likelihood with certainty |
| May / can | Possible | Statement does not rule it out | Treating possibility as fact |
| Only | Restrictive | Membership is limited exactly as stated | Reversing the condition |

**Worked example**

Statement: Every employee who enters the laboratory must wear an identity badge. Ravi is an employee and entered the laboratory.

Conclusion: Ravi must wear an identity badge.

The conclusion **follows** because the statement's rule applies directly to Ravi.

**Self-check**

Statement: Some roads in the district were flooded.

Conclusion: All roads in the district were closed.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The conclusion **does not follow**. “Some flooded” neither means “all flooded” nor proves that any road was closed.

</details>

## 2. Preserve Quantifiers and Conditions

Do not strengthen the statement while paraphrasing it.

**Worked example — conditional rule**

Statement: Applicants are admitted only if they present a valid identity document. Mira was admitted.

Conclusion: Mira presented a valid identity document.

“Admitted only if valid ID” makes the ID a necessary condition. The conclusion **follows**.

**Self-check — reversed condition**

Using the same statement, conclude: Arjun presented a valid identity document, so he was admitted.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The conclusion **does not follow**. A valid ID is necessary, but the statement does not say it is sufficient for admission.

</details>

**Self-check — particular to universal**

Statement: Some students in the class play chess.

Conclusion: Every student in the class plays a game.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The conclusion **does not follow**. The statement identifies only some students and says nothing about the rest.

</details>

## 3. Necessary Is Different from Plausible

Ask: **Could the statement be true while the conclusion is false?** If yes, the conclusion is not necessary.

| Statement supports | Safe conclusion | Unsafe addition |
|---|---|---|
| Exact rule applied to a named case | The case follows the rule | An unstated exception |
| A measured increase or decrease | That change occurred | Why it occurred |
| A scheduled closure | Access is unavailable then | What happens after reopening |
| A warning about risk | Risk exists | The harm will certainly occur |
| A request followed by action | Both events occurred | The request caused the action |

**Worked example**

Statement: The city library extended its closing time after receiving student requests.

Conclusion: Student requests were the sole cause of the extension.

The conclusion **does not follow**. The timing is stated, but “after” does not prove sole causation.

**Self-check**

Statement: The bridge will remain closed until structural repairs are complete.

Conclusion: Vehicles cannot use the bridge during the stated closure.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The conclusion **follows**. It is a direct consequence of the bridge being closed.

</details>

## 4. Cause, Prediction, and Recommendation

These conclusion types often add more than the statement provides:

- **Cause:** A sequence or correlation alone does not prove causation.
- **Prediction:** “Will certainly happen” needs an explicit guarantee, not a trend.
- **Recommendation:** “Should” introduces a policy judgment unless the statement itself establishes the requirement.
- **Value judgment:** Good, bad, fair, or wasteful needs a stated standard.

**Worked example — prediction**

Statement: Sales rose in each of the last three months.

Conclusion: Sales will definitely rise next month.

The conclusion **does not follow**. A past trend does not guarantee the next result.

**Self-check — recommendation**

Statement: The park recorded more visitors this month than last month.

Conclusion: The city should immediately double the park's budget.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The conclusion **does not follow**. Visitor growth is stated, but no budget need, cost, or policy rule is supplied.

</details>

## 5. Test Each Conclusion Independently

Never let one attractive conclusion pull a second one along.

| Step | Question | Reject when |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | What are the exact subjects and quantities? | A term or quantifier changes |
| 2 | Is the conclusion a restatement or necessary result? | It needs a new fact |
| 3 | Can the statement be true and conclusion false? | A counterexample is possible |
| 4 | Does cause, certainty, or advice appear? | The statement supplies only sequence or trend |
| 5 | Did outside knowledge enter? | The conclusion relies on real-world belief |
| 6 | Does each conclusion stand alone? | It depends on another conclusion |

A short single-statement item can fit a 36-second ceiling: one read for facts, one read for the conclusion, then the counterexample test.

**Self-check**

Conclusion I follows. Conclusion II sounds similar but requires an unstated fact. Should they be marked together?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. Mark **only Conclusion I**. Each conclusion must be derived independently from the statement.

</details>

## 6. Mixed Exam Practice

### Question 1

Statement: The city added 20 buses to its fleet.

Conclusion: Traffic congestion will definitely disappear.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Does not follow.** The statement gives one action but no guarantee about congestion.

</details>

### Question 2

Statement: All scholarship recipients submitted the required form. Neha received the scholarship.

Conclusion: Neha submitted the required form.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Follows.** Neha belongs to the group for which submission is stated.

</details>

### Question 3

Statement: The school advised students to carry umbrellas because rain was forecast.

Conclusion: It will certainly rain.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Does not follow.** A forecast and precaution do not guarantee the event.

</details>

### Question 4

Statement: The factory is closed for repairs from Monday through Wednesday.

Conclusion: The factory is not open on Tuesday.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Follows.** Tuesday lies within the stated closure period.

</details>

### Question 5

Statement: Some residents attended the meeting. Every attendee signed the register.

Conclusion: Some residents signed the register.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Follows.** The residents who attended belong to the group that signed.

</details>

## 7. Mastery Check

- Preserve every quantifier and condition.
- Separate necessary conclusions from plausible stories.
- Reject causal, predictive, and policy claims that need added facts.
- Use the counterexample test without outside knowledge.
- Decide multiple conclusions independently.
- Complete five fresh mixed questions and explain each rejection in one sentence.

Label each miss as **quantifier**, **condition reversal**, **outside fact**, **causation**, **certainty**, or **paired-conclusion error**.
