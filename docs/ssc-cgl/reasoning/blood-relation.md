## 1. Build the Family Tree

Blood-relation questions become reliable when you stop solving the sentence in your head. Fix a base person, move through one relation at a time, and answer from the direction asked.

![Decision map for solving blood-relation questions](/img/ssc-cgl/blood-relation-map.svg)
*Concept map: anchor the base person, decode each link, compare generations, and verify gender before naming the relation.*

Use a small notation system:

| Mark | Meaning | Example | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M` | Male | father, brother, son | Gender |
| `F` | Female | mother, sister, daughter | Gender |
| `+1` | One generation above | parent | Generation |
| `0` | Same generation | sibling or spouse | Generation |
| `−1` | One generation below | child | Generation |
| `=` | Married pair | husband and wife | Marriage link |
| Vertical line | Parent–child link | parent above child | Direction |

**Worked example**

P is the brother of Q. Q is the mother of R. How is P related to R?

P and Q are in the same generation. Q is one generation above R. Therefore P is R's mother's brother: **maternal uncle**.

**Self-check**

L is the sister of M. M is the father of N. How is L related to N?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Paternal aunt.** L is the sister of N's father, M.

</details>

## 2. Generation and Gender First

Find generation before choosing between uncle, brother, grandfather, or cousin. Then use explicit gender words.

| Relation word | Generation change | Gender fixed? | Safe translation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Father / mother | $+1$ | Yes | Male / female parent |
| Son / daughter | $-1$ | Yes | Male / female child |
| Brother / sister | $0$ | Yes | Male / female sibling |
| Husband / wife | $0$ | Yes | Male / female spouse |
| Parent / child | $+1$ / $-1$ | No | Do not infer gender |
| Sibling / spouse | $0$ | No | Do not infer gender |

Words such as “only” need exact reading:

- **Only son** means there is no other son; daughters may still exist.
- **Only child** means there is no sibling.
- **One of the daughters** proves that at least one other daughter may exist.

**Worked example**

A is the only child of B. B is the daughter of C. What is A's relation to C?

A is one generation below B, and B is one generation below C. A is therefore C's **grandchild**. A's gender is not stated, so “grandson” or “granddaughter” would be unjustified.

**Self-check**

R is the parent of S. S is the brother of T. What can be concluded about R's relation to T?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

R is **T's parent**. R's gender is unknown, so neither “father” nor “mother” is guaranteed.

</details>

## 3. Walk Relation Chains One Link at a Time

For a long sentence, underline each relation and draw immediately. Never jump from the first person to the final answer.

**Worked example**

K is the daughter of L. L is the son of M. M is the wife of N. How is N related to K?

1. K is L's daughter.
2. L is M's son, so M is K's grandmother.
3. M is N's wife, so N is K's **grandfather**.

**Self-check**

T is the mother of U. U is the brother of V. V is the mother of W. How is T related to W?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Maternal grandmother.** T is the mother of V, and V is the mother of W.

</details>

**Self-check — reverse direction**

J is the daughter of K. K is the brother of L. How is L related to J?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

L is J's **aunt or uncle**. L's gender is not stated, so a more specific answer is not valid.

</details>

## 4. Pointing and Introduction Questions

Replace pronouns with names as you read. The speaker's gender matters only when the wording makes the speaker part of the chain.

**Worked example**

Anita points to a boy and says, “He is the son of my mother's only son.” How is the boy related to Anita?

Anita is female, so her mother's only son is Anita's brother. The boy is that brother's son: Anita's **nephew**.

**Self-check**

Ravi points to a woman and says, “She is the mother of my father's only daughter.” Ravi has one sister. How is the woman related to Ravi?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Mother.** Ravi's father's only daughter is Ravi's sister. The woman's description as that sister's mother makes her Ravi's mother as well.

</details>

If a prompt permits two family trees, do not invent a missing gender or sibling count. A sound question must provide enough information for one option to be necessary.

## 5. Coded Relations

Decode every symbol into a sentence before drawing the tree. Symbols have no standard meaning across questions.

Suppose a question defines:

| Symbol | Meaning | Written as | Read as |
|---|---|---|---|
| `+` | Mother of | `A + B` | A is B's mother |
| `−` | Brother of | `A − B` | A is B's brother |
| `×` | Father of | `A × B` | A is B's father |
| `/` | Sister of | `A / B` | A is B's sister |

**Worked example**

Using those definitions, what is P's relation to R in `P − Q + R`?

P is Q's brother; Q is R's mother. P is therefore R's **maternal uncle**.

**Self-check**

Using the same definitions, what is X's relation to Z in `X × Y / Z`?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Father.** X is Y's father, and Y is Z's sister. Therefore X is also Z's father.

</details>

## 6. Speed and Error Control

A direct two-link chain can fit a 36-second ceiling. A longer coded family set should be solved as a diagram, not forced into the same time.

| Check | Question to ask | Error prevented |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Whose relation is being asked? | Answering the reverse relation |
| Chain | Did I draw every link? | Skipping a generation |
| Generation | Same, above, or below? | Uncle/brother confusion |
| Gender | Is it stated or merely assumed? | Aunt/uncle and son/child errors |
| “Only” | Only son, only daughter, or only child? | Excluding valid siblings |
| Final read | Relation of X to Y, or Y to X? | Direction reversal |

**Self-check**

The tree proves that D is one generation above E, but D's gender is unknown. Which answer is safest: father, mother, or parent?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Parent.** Generation alone does not determine gender.

</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

A is the sister of B. B is the father of C. How is A related to C?

Options: Mother, Paternal aunt, Maternal aunt, Sister

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Paternal aunt.** A is the sister of C's father.

</details>

### Question 2

P is the son of Q. Q is the daughter of R. How is P related to R?

Options: Son, Grandson, Nephew, Brother

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Grandson.** P is male and is two generations below R.

</details>

### Question 3

Meera points to a girl and says, “She is the daughter of my only brother.” How is the girl related to Meera?

Options: Sister, Daughter, Niece, Cousin

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Niece.** The girl is the daughter of Meera's brother.

</details>

### Question 4

If `A + B` means A is the mother of B and `B − C` means B is the brother of C, how is A related to C?

Options: Mother, Aunt, Sister, Grandmother

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Mother.** A is B's mother, and B and C are siblings.

</details>

### Question 5

H is the parent of J. J is the sister of K. What is H's gender?

Options: Male, Female, Cannot be determined

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Cannot be determined.** “Parent” fixes generation but not gender.

</details>

You have mastered the topic when you can draw each chain without rereading, preserve unknown gender, and answer five fresh questions without reversing the requested relation.
