## 1. Observe Before You Transform

Non-verbal questions become easier when you separate what may change from what must stay fixed. Check **count, position, direction, shading, and adjacency** in that order.

![Concept map for non-verbal reasoning transformations](/img/ssc-cgl/non-verbal-reasoning-map.svg)
*Concept map: identify the visual family first, then apply only the transformation that the question permits.*

| Question family | What changes | What stays fixed | First check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mirror image | Left and right | Top and bottom | Reflection axis |
| Water image | Top and bottom | Left and right | Horizontal axis |
| Rotation | Orientation | Shape and handedness | Angle and direction |
| Figure series | One or more attributes | Repeating cycle | Count and position |
| Paper folding | Symmetry after unfolding | Fold order | Reverse the folds |
| Embedded figure | Orientation may vary | Lines and intersections | Corners and crossings |
| Dice or cube | Visible faces | Opposite/adjacent relations | Shared faces |
| Shape counting | Scale | Original lines | Count by size |

**Worked example**

An upward arrow `↑` is rotated $90^\circ$ clockwise. It becomes **`→`**. Rotation changes orientation but does not reflect the shape.

**Self-check**

What does `→` become after a $180^\circ$ rotation?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**`←`.** A half-turn reverses the direction.

</details>

## 2. Mirror and Water Images

A vertical mirror swaps left and right. A horizontal water line swaps top and bottom.

| Original feature | Vertical mirror | Water image |
|---|---|---|
| Left side | Moves right | Stays left |
| Right side | Moves left | Stays right |
| Top | Stays top | Moves bottom |
| Bottom | Stays bottom | Moves top |
| `↗` | `↖` | `↘` |

Letters and digits may reverse under a mirror; do not replace them with a different normal character. Imagine the same strokes reflected across the axis.

**Worked example — mirror**

The vertical mirror image of `↗` is **`↖`**: the upward component remains, while right becomes left.

**Self-check — water image**

What is the water image of `↗`?

Options: `↖`, `↘`, `↙`, `↗`

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**`↘`.** Right stays right; up becomes down.

</details>

## 3. Rotation and Figure Series

For a figure series, record the change between consecutive frames. Two attributes may follow separate cycles.

| Attribute | Typical rule | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ rotation | Compare the same marker each frame |
| Position | Corner-to-corner movement | Number the positions |
| Count | Add or remove one element | Write totals below frames |
| Shading | Alternates or follows a cycle | Mark dark/light sequence |
| Shape | Repeats every $k$ frames | Find the shortest full cycle |

**Worked example**

Complete the sequence: `↑, →, ↓, ?`

Each arrow rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise. The answer is **`←`**.

**Self-check — two attributes**

A triangle moves clockwise through four corners while its shading alternates dark, light, dark, light. If frame 4 is light in the top-left corner, what must frame 5 show?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

A **dark triangle in the next clockwise corner**, which is the top-right if the cycle is top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left → top-left. Position and shading continue independently.

</details>

## 4. Paper Folding and Cutting

Unfold in the reverse order of the folds. At each unfold, reflect every existing cut across that fold line.

| Situation | Result on unfolding | Important exception |
|---|---|---|
| One fold, off-line hole | Two symmetric holes | None |
| One fold, hole on fold | One hole on the line | It does not duplicate |
| Two independent folds | Up to four copies | A hole on a fold duplicates fewer times |
| Corner cut | Symmetric edge/corner cuts | Track which folded edges are original |

**Worked example**

A square is folded once vertically. One hole is punched away from the fold. When opened, there are **two holes**, equally far from the fold on opposite sides.

**Self-check**

A sheet is folded once along a vertical line, and a hole is punched exactly on the fold. How many holes appear after unfolding?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**One.** A point on the reflection axis maps onto itself.

</details>

## 5. Embedded Figures, Dice, and Counting

For an embedded figure, trace one distinctive corner or crossing at a time. Ignore extra lines, but never ignore a missing required segment. Check whether rotation or reflection is allowed by the instruction.

For dice and cubes:

- Opposite faces never share an edge.
- Three faces visible at one corner are pairwise adjacent.
- Preserve adjacency while mentally rotating; do not swap opposite and adjacent faces.

For counting figures, count by size and mark completed shapes so none is counted twice.

**Worked example — squares**

A $2\times2$ square grid contains four small squares and one square covering the entire grid. Total squares:

$$
4+1=5
$$

**Self-check — cube**

A cube has opposite pairs $(1,6)$, $(2,5)$, and $(3,4)$. Which face is opposite 2?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**5.** The opposite pair is given directly; rotating the cube does not change it.

</details>

**Self-check — embedded figure**

An option contains every side of the target triangle but one required diagonal is missing. Is the target embedded?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**No.** Extra lines are allowed, but every required segment and intersection of the target must be present.

</details>

## 6. Mixed Practice and Speed Control

A direct reflection or rotation item can fit a 36-second ceiling. Complex shape counting or multi-fold paper cutting should be solved systematically rather than rushed.

### Question 1 — mirror

What is the vertical mirror image of `↘`?

Options: `↙`, `↗`, `↘`, `↖`

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**`↙`.** Down remains down; right becomes left.

</details>

### Question 2 — water image

What is the water image of `↙`?

Options: `↖`, `↘`, `↙`, `↗`

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**`↖`.** Left remains left; down becomes up.

</details>

### Question 3 — rotation

Complete the sequence: `→, ↓, ←, ?`

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**`↑`.** Each step is a $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation.

</details>

### Question 4 — paper fold

A sheet is folded once horizontally and punched away from the fold. How many symmetric holes appear after one unfold?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Two.** The original hole and its reflection lie on opposite sides of the fold.

</details>

### Question 5 — cube

If face A is opposite face D, can A and D appear together among the three faces meeting at one corner?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**No.** Opposite faces never share an edge or vertex.

</details>

### Question 6 — counting

How many squares are in a single square divided once vertically into two equal rectangles?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**One.** The outer boundary is a square; each inner region is a rectangle, not a square.

</details>

## 7. Mastery Check

- State the axis before reflecting.
- Distinguish rotation from reflection by handedness.
- Track position, direction, count, and shading separately in a series.
- Unfold paper in reverse order and respect holes on fold lines.
- Preserve opposite and adjacent face relations on cubes.
- Count shapes by size, marking each completed figure once.

For every miss, name the failed visual operation before trying a fresh question.

Continue with [Non-Verbal Reasoning focused practice](/exams/ssc-cgl/practice/non-verbal-reasoning) once you can name the transformation before comparing options.
