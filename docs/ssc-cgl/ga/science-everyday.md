General Science is not a random fact bank. Most questions reduce to a small set of units, transformations, organ functions, deficiency pairs, and everyday mechanisms. Prefer an explanation that predicts the result over a memorised slogan.

## 1. Start with Quantities, Units, and Motion

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Force | newton | N |
| Work or energy | joule | J |
| Power | watt | W |
| Pressure | pascal | Pa |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Electric charge | coulomb | C |
| Resistance | ohm | $\Omega$ |

Speed is distance per time and has magnitude only. Velocity includes direction. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Newton’s first law describes inertia; the second relates net force, mass, and acceleration; the third states that interaction forces occur in equal and opposite pairs acting on different bodies.

$$F=ma,\qquad W=Fs\cos\theta,\qquad P=\frac{W}{t}$$

**Worked example**

A net force of 12 N acts on a 3 kg body. Its acceleration is $a=F/m=4\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.

**Self-check**

A runner completes one lap and returns to the start. Is displacement equal to distance?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. Distance is the full path length, while displacement is zero because final and initial positions coincide.
</details>

## 2. Connect Heat, Sound, and Light to Daily Life

Heat flows spontaneously from higher to lower temperature. Conduction dominates through direct molecular interaction in solids; convection involves bulk motion in fluids; radiation needs no material medium.

A pressure cooker raises pressure, which raises water’s boiling temperature and cooks food faster. Sweating cools by evaporation. During daytime, land usually warms faster than sea, air rises over land, and cooler air moves inland as a sea breeze.

Sound is a mechanical wave and cannot travel through a vacuum. Frequency mainly determines pitch; amplitude is associated with loudness. Light can travel through a vacuum.

| Optical device | Behaviour | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Convex mirror | diverging; wide field; upright diminished image | rear-view mirror |
| Concave mirror | converging; image depends on object position | shaving/dentist mirror, reflector |
| Convex lens | converging | magnifier, camera systems |
| Concave lens | diverging | correction of myopia |

**Worked example**

A rear-view mirror must show a wide area while keeping the image upright. A **convex mirror** provides that combination.

**Self-check**

Why does food cook faster in a pressure cooker?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Higher pressure raises the boiling point of water, so the food cooks at a temperature above the ordinary boiling point at atmospheric pressure.
</details>

## 3. Use Circuit and Instrument Clues

Ohm’s law for an ohmic conductor at constant physical conditions is $V=IR$. In a series circuit, current is common and resistances add. In a parallel circuit, potential difference is common across branches and equivalent resistance is less than the smallest branch resistance.

Electrical power is $P=VI$; electrical energy is power multiplied by time. A fuse is placed in series and made from a material chosen to melt when current becomes excessive. Earthing provides a low-resistance safety path and reduces shock risk.

| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| Ammeter | electric current |
| Voltmeter | potential difference |
| Barometer | atmospheric pressure |
| Hygrometer | humidity |
| Seismograph | seismic motion/waves |
| Lactometer | relative density of milk |

**Self-check**

Should an ideal ammeter be connected in series or parallel?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

In series, so the circuit current passes through it. A voltmeter is connected in parallel across the component.
</details>

## 4. Classify Chemical Change, Acids, and Materials

An element contains one kind of atom; a compound contains elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion; a mixture has components physically combined in variable proportions. Alloys are treated as mixtures in standard school-level classification.

In aqueous solutions at ordinary reference conditions, pH below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, and above 7 is basic. Acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue. An acid reacting with a carbonate typically produces salt, water, and carbon dioxide.

Oxidation can be understood as loss of electrons or gain of oxygen; reduction as gain of electrons or loss of oxygen. They occur together in a redox reaction. Rusting needs iron, oxygen, and moisture; painting or galvanising limits contact with the environment.

**Worked example**

Adding dilute acid to a carbonate releases a gas that turns limewater milky. The gas is **carbon dioxide**.

**Self-check**

Is brass a pure element, compound, or alloy mixture?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

It is an alloy, principally of copper and zinc, and is classified as a mixture at this level.
</details>

## 5. Map Cells, Organs, and Hormones

The cell membrane regulates movement into and out of a cell. The nucleus contains most genetic material in a eukaryotic cell. Mitochondria are major sites of aerobic respiration; ribosomes synthesize proteins; chloroplasts perform photosynthesis in green plant cells.

| Structure or substance | Core function | Trap pair |
|---|---|---|
| Alveoli | gas exchange in lungs | not kidney filtration |
| Nephron | functional unit of kidney | not neuron |
| Red blood cell | oxygen transport through haemoglobin | not antibody production |
| White blood cell | immune defence | not primary clot formation |
| Platelet | blood clotting | not oxygen transport |
| Insulin | lowers blood glucose | glucagon raises it |
| Thyroxine | regulates metabolism | insulin is pancreatic |

The pancreas produces insulin and glucagon. The thyroid produces thyroxine and requires iodine for hormone synthesis. The pituitary is often called the master gland because its hormones regulate several other endocrine glands, though endocrine control is a feedback network.

**Self-check**

Which is the functional unit of the kidney: nephron or neuron?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Nephron. A neuron is a nerve cell.
</details>

## 6. Pair Nutrients, Deficiencies, and Disease Types

| Nutrient | Deficiency association | Main role cue |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A | night blindness | vision and epithelial health |
| Vitamin B1 | beriberi | energy metabolism and nerve function |
| Vitamin C | scurvy | collagen and wound healing |
| Vitamin D | rickets in children | calcium balance and bone mineralisation |
| Vitamin K | impaired clotting | synthesis of clotting factors |
| Iron | iron-deficiency anaemia | haemoglobin |
| Iodine | goitre from deficiency | thyroid hormone synthesis |

Communicable diseases are caused by infectious agents and can spread directly or indirectly. Deficiency diseases arise from inadequate nutrients and are not contagious. Antibiotics act against susceptible bacteria, not viruses; misuse encourages antimicrobial resistance.

Vaccination trains immune memory. It does not mean a vaccine contains enough active disease-causing organism to produce the full disease; vaccine platforms vary, and current public-health schedules should be checked from official sources.

**Self-check**

Which vitamin deficiency is classically associated with rickets in children?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Vitamin D deficiency, because vitamin D is important for calcium balance and bone mineralisation.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

Which SI unit measures pressure?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The pascal.
</details>

### Question 2

Which property of sound chiefly determines pitch?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Frequency. Amplitude is associated with loudness.
</details>

### Question 3

What gas forms when an acid reacts with a carbonate?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Carbon dioxide, along with water and a salt.
</details>

### Question 4

Which blood component is primarily associated with clotting?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Platelets.
</details>

### Question 5

Which instrument measures atmospheric pressure?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

A barometer.
</details>
