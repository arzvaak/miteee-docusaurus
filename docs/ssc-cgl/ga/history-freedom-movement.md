*Anchor the period first, then attach the ruler, Act, session, place, leader, or movement asked in the question.*

History becomes manageable when facts sit on a timeline. For each item, learn **date or period → event → person/place → consequence**. Avoid memorising an isolated year without the event on both sides of it.

## 1. Build the Long Timeline

Use five broad shelves before adding detail:

| Shelf | Anchor sequence | What SSC commonly tests |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient | Harappan → Vedic → Maurya → Gupta | sites, texts, rulers, inscriptions |
| Medieval | Delhi Sultanate → regional states → Mughals | dynasties, administration, monuments |
| Company rule | Plassey → Buxar → Diwani → expansion | battles, Governors-General, Acts |
| Crown and nationalism | 1857 → Congress → Swadeshi → Gandhian phase | leaders, sessions, movement aims |
| Transfer of power | Cripps → Cabinet Mission → Mountbatten Plan | proposals, dates, final outcome |

The Harappan civilisation was urban and is associated with planned streets, drainage, seals, and major sites such as Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, and Lothal. The Mauryan age is linked to Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka, and imperial inscriptions; the Gupta age is remembered for major developments in literature, science, art, and political history. These are broad anchors, not claims that every cultural change began or ended on one date.

**Worked example**

Prompt: “Which site is associated with a dockyard?” First classify it as **Harappan site-feature**. The standard exam association is **Lothal**.

**Self-check**

Why should Plassey and Buxar not be treated as interchangeable Company victories?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

They occurred in different years and had different political consequences. Plassey was fought in 1757; Buxar in 1764 strengthened Company power and preceded the 1765 grant of Diwani rights.
</details>

## 2. Place Dynasties and Rulers in Order

The Delhi Sultanate sequence is **Mamluk (Slave), Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi**. The Mughal line begins with Babur’s victory at the First Battle of Panipat in **1526**. Humayun was followed by Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, and Aurangzeb in the standard imperial sequence.

Link rulers to a limited set of secure associations:

- **Alauddin Khalji:** market-control measures and expansion under the Khaljis;
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq:** token currency and transfer-of-capital experiments;
- **Sher Shah Suri:** administrative and road reforms during the interruption of Mughal rule;
- **Akbar:** mansabdari, broad imperial consolidation, and major religious-policy questions;
- **Shah Jahan:** Mughal architectural patronage, including the Taj Mahal and Red Fort at Delhi.

**Self-check**

Which dynasty immediately preceded the Lodi dynasty in the Delhi Sultanate sequence?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Sayyid dynasty. The full order is Mamluk, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi.
</details>

## 3. Follow Company Rule Through Battles and Acts

The useful spine is:

- **1757, Battle of Plassey**;
- **1764, Battle of Buxar**;
- **1765, Diwani rights** in Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa granted to the Company;
- **1773, Regulating Act**, the first major parliamentary step to regulate Company administration;
- **1784, Pitt’s India Act**, creating a system of governmental control through the Board of Control;
- **1858, Government of India Act**, transferring rule from the East India Company to the British Crown after the Revolt of 1857.

Later constitutional reforms should be learned by the feature they introduced, not only by year.

| Measure | High-yield feature | Frequent trap |
|---|---|---|
| Indian Councils Act 1909 | separate electorates for Muslims | confused with dyarchy |
| Government of India Act 1919 | dyarchy in provinces | confused with provincial autonomy |
| Government of India Act 1935 | provincial autonomy; proposed federation | federation was not fully realised |
| Indian Independence Act 1947 | two Dominions and end of British rule | confused with the June plan preceding it |

**Worked example**

Prompt: “Which Act introduced dyarchy in the provinces?” The keyword **dyarchy** fixes the answer as the **Government of India Act 1919**, not the 1935 Act.

**Self-check**

Which Act is associated with provincial autonomy?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Government of India Act 1935. Dyarchy in provinces belongs to the 1919 Act.
</details>

## 4. Map the Revolt of 1857

The uprising began among Company soldiers at Meerut in May 1857 and spread through major parts of north and central India. Learn centres with leaders, while remembering that participation and local leadership were more complex than a single-pair table can show.

| Centre | Exam association |
|---|---|
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah II |
| Kanpur | Nana Sahib; Tantia Tope |
| Jhansi | Rani Lakshmibai |
| Lucknow | Begum Hazrat Mahal |
| Bihar/Jagdishpur | Kunwar Singh |
| Barrackpore | Mangal Pandey, in the immediate prelude |

Its causes included military grievances, annexation and political displacement, economic pressures, and social-religious fears. The revolt did not have uniform aims or reach across all of India. Its decisive constitutional consequence was the end of Company rule and transfer to the Crown in 1858.

**Self-check**

Which leader-centre pair is correct: Begum Hazrat Mahal—Lucknow or Begum Hazrat Mahal—Jhansi?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Begum Hazrat Mahal—Lucknow. Jhansi is associated with Rani Lakshmibai.
</details>

## 5. Track Congress and the Growth of Nationalism

The Indian National Congress first met at Bombay in **1885**, with W. C. Bonnerjee as its first president. The partition of Bengal in **1905** intensified the Swadeshi and boycott movement. The Surat split occurred in **1907**. The Lucknow Pact of **1916** was an agreement between the Congress and the Muslim League; the same year also saw reunion of Congress factions.

Three session anchors matter especially:

- **Lahore, 1929:** Jawaharlal Nehru presided; the Congress adopted the goal of Purna Swaraj, followed by observance of 26 January 1930 as Independence Day.
- **Karachi, 1931:** resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme.
- **Bombay, 1942:** the All India Congress Committee adopted the Quit India resolution.

**Worked example**

Prompt: “At which session was Purna Swaraj adopted as the Congress goal?” The correct pair is **Lahore session, 1929**, not Karachi 1931.

**Self-check**

Which came first: the partition of Bengal or the Surat split?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The partition of Bengal in 1905 came before the Surat split in 1907.
</details>

## 6. Distinguish Gandhian Movements and Final Proposals

Gandhi’s early Indian satyagrahas included **Champaran (1917)**, connected with indigo cultivators; **Ahmedabad mill strike (1918)**; and **Kheda (1918)**, connected with revenue relief. The Rowlatt Act agitation and Jallianwala Bagh massacre belong to **1919**.

| Movement or proposal | Date | Defining cue |
|---|---:|---|
| Non-Cooperation | 1920–22 | boycott of colonial institutions; withdrawn after Chauri Chaura |
| Civil Disobedience | from 1930 | Salt March; open breach of selected laws |
| Quit India | 1942 | immediate British withdrawal; “Do or Die” |
| Cripps Mission | 1942 | wartime constitutional proposal |
| Cabinet Mission | 1946 | Constituent Assembly plan; effort to preserve union |
| Mountbatten Plan | 3 June 1947 | partition and transfer-of-power plan |

**Self-check**

Why are Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience not the same movement?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Non-Cooperation centred on withdrawal from colonial institutions and honours. Civil Disobedience involved openly breaking selected colonial laws, symbolised by the Salt March.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

Which battle was followed by the Company’s acquisition of Diwani rights in 1765?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Battle of Buxar (1764). Plassey occurred earlier, in 1757.
</details>

### Question 2

Who is associated with the revolt at Jagdishpur in Bihar?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Kunwar Singh.
</details>

### Question 3

Which reform introduced separate electorates for Muslims?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Indian Councils Act 1909, often called the Morley–Minto Reforms.
</details>

### Question 4

Which Congress session is linked to the Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme resolution?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Karachi session of 1931.
</details>

### Question 5

Place these in order: Quit India, Civil Disobedience, Non-Cooperation.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Non-Cooperation (1920–22), Civil Disobedience (from 1930), then Quit India (1942).
</details>
