Current affairs changes; the method for studying it should not. This chapter therefore does **not** freeze a list of current officeholders, rankings, award winners, scheme figures, or budget numbers. Use the [live SSC CGL current-affairs brief](/exams/ssc-cgl/current-affairs) for dated facts. Use this lesson to decide what to verify, which source owns the fact, and what durable knowledge should be attached to it.

## 1. Separate the Live Fact from the Static Anchor

Every useful current-affairs item has two layers:

- the **live fact**: what happened, who currently holds a post, which edition was released, or what a new figure is;
- the **static anchor**: the institution, constitutional basis, headquarters, subject area, geography, or scientific idea behind it.

The live fact must carry an **as-of date**. The static anchor can enter long-term revision after it is checked once in an authoritative source.

| Prompt cue | Live fact to verify | Durable anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment | Name and effective date | Appointing authority; institution |
| Report or index | Latest edition and rank | Publisher; subject measured |
| Scheme update | Current rule or allocation | Ministry; target group; delivery body |
| Award | Current recipient and category | Awarding institution; field |
| Summit | Current host, theme, outcome | Organisation; members; headquarters |
| Disaster or mission | Current location and action | Map position; agency; scientific process |

**Worked example**

A live brief says that an institution released a new report. Do not learn only the headline rank. Record four fields: **edition date, report title, publisher, and what the indicator measures**. The first two may change next year; the last two often remain useful static anchors.

**Self-check**

Why is “the latest rank” unsafe in a timeless note?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Because a later edition can replace it. A rank is valid only with the report edition and reference date. The publisher and measurement theme are safer long-term anchors.
</details>

## 2. Use the Source That Owns the Claim

Choose a source by **institutional ownership**, not by whichever summary appears first. A coaching card can point you toward a topic, but it should not be the final authority for a volatile fact.

| Claim family | Preferred authority | What to capture |
|---|---|---|
| Union policy or ministry scheme | Ministry release or PIB | Date, ministry, scope, official name |
| Monetary policy or banking rule | RBI | Decision date, instrument, definition |
| Bill, Act, or Parliament status | Parliament/PRS summary plus official text | Stage, date, legal status |
| Recruitment or exam rule | SSC notice | Notice date, post, deadline, exact condition |
| National statistics | Releasing ministry or statutory agency | Period, unit, provisional/final status |
| International report | Publishing organisation | Edition, coverage, method, India entry |

For an exam question, also read the **reference date in the stem**. “At present,” “as of March,” and “in the 2025 edition” are three different instructions.

**Worked example**

Suppose two summaries disagree about a banking rule. The deciding source is the current RBI circular or official explanatory page, not the more recent-looking social post. Preserve the date of the RBI document with your note.

**Self-check**

Which source should decide the closing date of an SSC application: a newspaper report or the SSC notice?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The SSC notice and any official corrigendum decide it. A report may paraphrase or pre-date a later correction.
</details>

## 3. Convert News into Exam-Ready Static Links

The aim is not to collect every detail. Convert one verified event into a small fact unit:

1. **Entity** — person, institution, place, report, scheme, species, or mission.
2. **Attribute** — office, publisher, ministry, state, headquarters, field, or function.
3. **Static anchor** — one durable fact that explains the entity.
4. **Trap pair** — the nearest confusable entity or attribute.
5. **Recall prompt** — one question you can answer without rereading the article.

**Worked example**

A wetland appears in a dated news brief. Verify the current designation or event on the live route, then attach stable geography: its state or union territory, river basin if relevant, and whether the question asks about a Ramsar site, national park, sanctuary, or biosphere reserve. Do not copy a national total into this chapter because the total can change.

**Self-check**

A scheme is in the news. Which three anchors should be checked before making a flashcard?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Check the administering ministry, the intended beneficiary or objective, and the official delivery or implementing body. Add launch or revision dates only with a source and an as-of label.
</details>

## 4. Guard Against Volatile-Fact Traps

Treat these categories as **live-only** unless a question supplies a year or edition:

- current officeholders and appointments;
- winners, hosts, venues, themes, and “latest” awards;
- rankings, indices, record holders, and medal tables;
- scheme allocations, eligibility limits, targets, and beneficiary counts;
- budget, inflation, growth, policy-rate, reserve, and trade figures;
- counts of protected areas, Ramsar sites, GI tags, members, or languages in a changing list.

Use a four-part label: `fact — source owner — reference date — edition/period`. If one part is missing, the card is not ready for revision.

**Worked example**

Question: “Who is the current head of body X?” The word **current** triggers live verification. A timeless answer, even one that was correct when written, is not sufficient. Open the live brief or the body’s official site and use the question’s date.

**Self-check**

Can an old award-winner list be used to answer “Who won the latest edition?”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. It can teach the award’s field and awarding body, but the latest recipient must be verified for the stated edition or date.
</details>

## 5. Revise with Retrieval, Not Headlines

Keep each live item small. Read the verified brief once, close it, and retrieve:

- What happened?
- Who or which institution owns the action?
- Where and when did it occur?
- Which static subject does it connect to?
- What is the nearest trap?

Then schedule the item for short recall on the same day, after two or three days, and after a week. Retire or replace cards when a new edition makes the old live fact stale; retain the verified static anchor.

**Self-check**

Why is a headline-only card weak even when its fact is correct?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

It lacks ownership, date, and a static anchor. Those missing fields make it hard to resolve later updates and easy to confuse similar reports, schemes, or institutions.
</details>

## 6. Mixed Practice

### Question 1

A question asks for the publisher of an international index. What should be recalled first: India’s old rank or the publishing organisation?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Recall the publishing organisation. A rank belongs to a particular edition and may change; the publisher is the durable association the question is testing.
</details>

### Question 2

A ministry revises a scheme’s eligibility rule. Which part belongs only in the dated card?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The revised eligibility rule and its effective date belong in the dated card. The scheme’s administering ministry and core objective can be retained as static anchors after verification.
</details>

### Question 3

Two official-looking pages show different statistics. What is the first comparison to make?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Compare the reference period, release date, unit, and provisional/final status. Different periods or revisions can make both pages accurate in context.
</details>

### Question 4

An SSC notice and an unattributed screenshot disagree about an exam condition. Which controls the answer?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The dated SSC notice, including any official corrigendum, controls the answer. The screenshot has no reliable provenance.
</details>

### Question 5

A national-park story appears in today’s brief. Name two safe static anchors.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Its state or union territory and its characteristic ecosystem or flagship species are useful anchors when verified. A changing national count of parks is not a timeless anchor.
</details>

You are ready to practise when you can classify a prompt as **live**, **static**, or **live plus static**, select the source owner, attach an as-of date to volatile claims, and explain why an older rank or officeholder may no longer answer the question.
