## Concept Ladder

**Rung 1 - First Principle: What is Static GK?**  
Static GK means factual knowledge that does not change frequently: constitutional articles, historical dates, geographical features, scientific constants, award categories. Unlike current affairs, static GK is finite - about 300-400 high-probability facts. The exam tests recognition, not deep analysis.

**Rung 2 - Why a Map?**  
Your brain recalls location and association better than isolated numbers. A "map" means linking every fact to a bigger picture:  
- Polity: Article  Body  Function  
- History: Event  Year  Person  
- Geography: Place  Feature  Climate  
- Science: Invention  Scientist  Year  
When you build a network, one cue retrieves many facts.

**Rung 3 - Exam-Level Integration**  
SSC CGL Tier-I GA: 25 questions, 15 minutes. Speed = 36 seconds per question. You must know instantly:  
- Which article deals with President? (Art. 52)  
- Who founded the INA? (Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose)  
- What is the capital of Arunachal? (Itanagar)  
- Which vitamin is produced in sunlight? (Vitamin D)  
A map lets you jump across domains without hesitation.  
**Integration trick**: combine two subjects - "Article 370 is related to which state?" (Jammu & Kashmir) - this is polity+ geography.

**Rung 4 - Memory Hooks**  
Use mnemonics, visual images, and grouping:  
- **Polity Articles by theme**: "PEaCE" - President (52-62), Executive (63-75), Parliament (79-122), Courts (124-147), Emergency (352-360).  
- **Geography**: "DAM" - D for Dams, A for Atomic stations, M for Mountain passes - use alphabet grouping.  
- **History timelines**: "Voyage of Vasco da Gama (1498) - 30 years later Babur (1526) - 200 years later Plassey (1757)".  
- **Science**: "My Very Eager Mother Just Served Us Nine Pizzas" for planets (old) - adapt to "Mercury Venus Earth Mars Jupiter Saturn Uranus Neptune".

**Rung 5 - From Map to 200/200**  
A 200/200 attempt means zero guesses in GA. You eliminate three wrong options instantly. The map provides:  
- **Direct recall** for 18-20 questions (polity articles, capital cities, scientists).  
- **Elimination logic** for 5-7 ambiguous ones (e.g., "Which is not a fundamental right?" - use article list from map).  
- **Time buffer** - you finish GA in 10 minutes, leaving 5 for checking.  
Every rung strengthens speed and accuracy.

**Decision Rules for Ambiguous Questions**

1. **Rule of Conflicting Dates**: If two events have same year (e.g., 1857 = Revolt, 1885 = INC), read the question for person/place. If person = Mangal Pandey, answer is 1857. If place = Bombay, answer is 1885.  
2. **Rule of Nearest Article**: If you cannot recall exact article number, recall the Part. For example, "Which article deals with citizenship?" - Part II (Articles 5-11), so answer is within 5-11. Eliminate articles outside that range.  
3. **Rule of Geographical Pairing**: If question names a river and asks for state, think of the river's origin or major tributary. E.g., Ganga flows through Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal. If options include "Bihar", check if river passes its capital Patna.  
4. **Rule of Synonym Elimination**: For science, if two options seem correct (e.g., "Oxygen" and "Carbon dioxide" for photosynthesis), recall the process: plants take CO2 and water to make glucose and oxygen. Primary input is CO2. Choose accordingly.  
5. **Rule of Recent Update**: For awards, always assume the latest known winner before your exam date. For SSC CGL 2024, assume 2023 winners. If question says "2021", use that specific year context.

**Step-by-Step Algorithm for a Static GK Question**

1. **Read the question stem** - identify domain (polity, history, etc.).  
2. **Scan the options** - look for one obvious correct and three plausible distractors.  
3. **Apply domain-specific mnemonic** - if polity, recall the article range; if history, recall timeline.  
4. **Eliminate options that contradict known facts** - e.g., "First President of India" options: (a) Rajendra Prasad (b) Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan (c) Zakir Hussain (d) V. V. Giri. Prasad is first, so eliminate the rest.  
5. **If two remain, use association rule** - e.g., which dance is from Kerala? Kathakali vs Mohiniyattam. Both are from Kerala, but Mohiniyattam is a classical dance of Kerala as per Sangeet Natak Akademi. Use precise list.  
6. **Confirm and mark** - do not second-guess if you used a reliable mnemonic.

## Solved Examples

**Example 1**  
Q. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the appointment of the Chief Justice of India?  
Options: (a) Article 124 (b) Article 134 (c) Article 144 (d) Article 154  
Answer: (a) Article 124  
Explanation: Article 124 deals with establishment and constitution of Supreme Court including appointment of Chief Justice. Article 134 deals with appellate jurisdiction, 144 with civil judicial authorities, 154 with High Court. Using mnemonic: 124 = Supreme Court setup.

**Example 2**  
Q. The Chauri Chaura incident took place in which year?  
Options: (a) 1919 (b) 1920 (c) 1922 (d) 1925  
Answer: (c) 1922  
Explanation: Chauri Chaura (1922) led to withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement by Gandhi. 1919 -> Jallianwala, 1920 -> Non-Cooperation started, 1925 -> Kakori conspiracy. Timeline anchor: 1922.

**Example 3**  
Q. Which of the following is the longest river in India?  
Options: (a) Ganga (b) Yamuna (c) Brahmaputra (d) Godavari  
Answer: (a) Ganga (2525 km)  
Explanation: Ganga is longest (though Brahmaputra is longer in total course, but within India Ganga is longest). Godavari is longest in peninsular India (1465 km). Use known fact: Ganga is longest river in India.

**Example 4**  
Q. Deficiency of Vitamin D causes which disease?  
Options: (a) Scurvy (b) Rickets (c) Beri-Beri (d) Pellagra  
Answer: (b) Rickets  
Explanation: Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets in children (bone deformities). Scurvy = Vitamin C, Beri-Beri = B1, Pellagra = B3. Mnemonic: D = Day (sunlight) -> rickets.

**Example 5**  
Q. Who is known as the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?  
Options: (a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) B. R. Ambedkar (d) Sardar Patel  
Answer: (c) B. R. Ambedkar  
Explanation: Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee. Gandhi is Father of Nation, Nehru first PM, Patel first Home Minister. Association: "Ambedkar = Constitution".

**Example 6**  
Q. The headquarters of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) is located in:  
Options: (a) New York (b) Washington D.C. (c) Geneva (d) Paris  
Answer: (b) Washington D.C.  
Explanation: IMF and World Bank both in Washington D.C. New York has UN headquarters, Geneva has WHO, Paris has UNESCO. Use location pair: IMF = DC.

**Example 7**  
Q. Which of the following is a classical dance form of Tamil Nadu?  
Options: (a) Kathakali (b) Kuchipudi (c) Bharatanatyam (d) Odissi  
Answer: (c) Bharatanatyam  
Explanation: Kathakali = Kerala, Kuchipudi = Andhra Pradesh, Odissi = Odisha, Bharatanatyam = Tamil Nadu. State-dance mnemonic: T for Tamil -> Bharatanatyam (B starts with Bh).

**Example 8**  
Q. The term "SLR" in banking stands for:  
Options: (a) Statutory Liquidity Ratio (b) Simple Liquidity Rate (c) Statutory Lending Ratio (d) Savings Liquidity Rate  
Answer: (a) Statutory Liquidity Ratio  
Explanation: SLR is the percentage of deposits that banks must keep in liquid assets (gold, cash, etc.). CRR is cash reserve ratio. Use mnemonic: SLR = Statutory Liquidity Ratio (S-L-R matches exactly).

**Example 9**  
Q. Which gas is primarily responsible for the greenhouse effect?  
Options: (a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Hydrogen  
Answer: (b) Carbon dioxide  
Explanation: CO2 is main greenhouse gas from burning fossil fuels. Others like methane, water vapor also contribute, but CO2 is primary. Know: greenhouse effect = increased CO2.

**Example 10**  
Q. The "Dandi March" was led by which leader?  
Options: (a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Subhas Chandra Bose (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) Vallabhbhai Patel  
Answer: (c) Mahatma Gandhi  
Explanation: Dandi March (1930) was Gandhi's Salt Satyagraha. Nehru was involved later, Bose formed INA, Patel organized peasants. Direct association: Gandhi = Dandi.
