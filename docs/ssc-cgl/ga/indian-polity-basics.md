Polity questions are usually association tests: **provision → institution → power → limitation**. Learn the structure first. Names of current ministers, judges, governors, and other officeholders are live facts and belong on the [current-affairs route](/exams/ssc-cgl/current-affairs), not in this timeless chapter.

## 1. Read the Constitutional Map

The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**. Most provisions came into force on **26 January 1950**. The Preamble states the constitutional vision; Parts organise major subjects; Articles carry operative provisions; Schedules hold supporting lists and details.

| Area | Location | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | Part III, Articles 12–35 | Enforceable rights and remedies |
| Directive Principles | Part IV, Articles 36–51 | Governance goals, not directly enforceable |
| Fundamental Duties | Part IVA, Article 51A | Duties of citizens |
| Union executive | Part V | President, Vice-President, ministers, Attorney-General |
| State executive | Part VI | Governor, ministers, Advocate-General |
| Emergency provisions | Part XVIII | Articles 352, 356, and 360 |

**Worked example**

Prompt: “Which Part contains the Directive Principles of State Policy?” The category is a **Part-location** pair. The answer is **Part IV**, not Part III, which contains Fundamental Rights.

**Self-check**

Why is “adopted on 26 January 1950” incorrect?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. The question may deliberately swap adoption and commencement.
</details>

## 2. Rights, Principles, Duties, and Remedies

Fundamental Rights bind public power and are judicially enforceable, subject to constitutional restrictions. Directive Principles guide the State in law and policy. Fundamental Duties are constitutional duties of citizens but are not enforced in the same way as Fundamental Rights.

Article **32** guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. High Courts issue writs under Article **226**, for Fundamental Rights and also “for any other purpose,” so their writ jurisdiction is wider in subject matter.

| Writ | Basic function | Typical cue |
|---|---|---|
| Habeas corpus | Produce a detained person; test detention | unlawful custody |
| Mandamus | Command a public authority to perform a duty | failure to act |
| Prohibition | Stop a lower court/tribunal before excess action | proceeding pending |
| Certiorari | Quash an order of a lower court/tribunal | order already made |
| Quo warranto | Test authority to hold a public office | eligibility for office |

**Worked example**

A tribunal is about to hear a matter outside its jurisdiction. The preventive cue **about to proceed** points to **prohibition**. If an invalid order had already been made, **certiorari** would be the closer fit.

**Self-check**

Which Article is itself a Fundamental Right to constitutional remedies?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Article 32. It permits a person to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
</details>

## 3. Know the Union Executive and Constitutional Bodies

The President is the constitutional head of the Union. Under Article **74**, a Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister aids and advises the President. The distinction is between the office in whose name executive action is taken and the political executive that exercises day-to-day responsibility.

Do not learn current officeholders here. Learn the constitutional source and function:

| Institution or office | Article | Durable function |
|---|---:|---|
| Attorney-General for India | 76 | Highest law officer of the Union |
| Comptroller and Auditor-General | 148 | Audits public accounts as constitutionally provided |
| Finance Commission | 280 | Recommends fiscal distribution between Union and states |
| Election Commission | 324 | Superintendence, direction, and control of specified elections |
| Union Public Service Commission | 315 | Constitutional public-service commission |

**Self-check**

Which body is linked to Article 280: the Election Commission or the Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Finance Commission. The Election Commission is linked to Article 324.
</details>

## 4. Follow a Bill Through Parliament

Article **79** says Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and House of the People (Lok Sabha). Ordinary Bills can generally begin in either House. A deadlock over an ordinary Bill may lead to a joint sitting under Article **108**, subject to constitutional exclusions.

A Money Bill is defined by Article **110**. It can be introduced only in Lok Sabha. The Speaker’s decision on whether a Bill is a Money Bill is final under Article 110(3). Rajya Sabha may recommend changes but must return the Bill within **14 days**; there is no joint sitting for a Money Bill.

**Worked example**

Prompt: “Which House can amend a Money Bill and insist on its amendment?” Neither wording is correct. Rajya Sabha may only **recommend** amendments; Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.

**Self-check**

Does Parliament mean only Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President and the two Houses.
</details>

## 5. Distinguish Courts, Federal Lists, and Emergencies

The Supreme Court is the apex court; High Courts operate at the state or shared-state level. Judicial review tests state action against the Constitution. Do not confuse **original**, **appellate**, and **advisory** jurisdiction: the cue in the question decides which power is being tested.

The Seventh Schedule contains the **Union List, State List, and Concurrent List**. Parliament normally legislates on the Union List; state legislatures on the State List; both may legislate on the Concurrent List, with Article 254 governing repugnancy.

| Emergency | Article | Trigger family |
|---|---:|---|
| National Emergency | 352 | war, external aggression, or armed rebellion |
| President’s Rule in a state | 356 | failure of constitutional machinery in a state |
| Financial Emergency | 360 | threat to financial stability or credit |

**Self-check**

Which phrase replaced “internal disturbance” as a ground for National Emergency?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“Armed rebellion,” through the Forty-fourth Amendment. War and external aggression remain the other grounds in Article 352.
</details>

## 6. Attach Amendments to Their Exact Changes

Learn an amendment with one verified constitutional change, not with a vague slogan.

- **42nd Amendment (1976):** added “socialist,” “secular,” and “integrity” to the Preamble and inserted Fundamental Duties, among other changes.
- **44th Amendment (1978):** removed the right to property from Fundamental Rights; property is protected as a constitutional legal right under Article **300A**. It also strengthened emergency safeguards.
- **52nd Amendment (1985):** added the Tenth Schedule on defection.
- **73rd Amendment (1992):** constitutional framework for Panchayats; added Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule.
- **74th Amendment (1992):** constitutional framework for Municipalities; added Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule.

**Worked example**

Prompt: “The Tenth Schedule was added by which amendment?” Identify the subject first: **anti-defection**. The association is the **52nd Amendment**, not the 42nd.

**Self-check**

Is the right to property still a Fundamental Right?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. It is a constitutional legal right under Article 300A, not a Fundamental Right in Part III.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

Which Article assigns election superintendence, direction, and control to the Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Article 324. Article 280 concerns the Finance Commission and Article 315 concerns public-service commissions.
</details>

### Question 2

Which writ tests a person’s authority to occupy a public office?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Quo warranto. It asks by what authority the office is held.
</details>

### Question 3

How long may Rajya Sabha retain a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Fourteen days. It may recommend changes, but Lok Sabha decides whether to accept them.
</details>

### Question 4

Which Schedule distributes legislative subjects among three lists?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Seventh Schedule. The Tenth concerns defection; the Eleventh and Twelfth concern Panchayats and Municipalities.
</details>

### Question 5

What is the constitutional location of Fundamental Duties?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Part IVA, Article 51A.
</details>

Mastery means you can move both ways: Article → institution and institution → Article; writ → function and function → writ; amendment → change and change → amendment.
