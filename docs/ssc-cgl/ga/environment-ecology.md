## 1. Climb the Ecological Levels

An **organism** is one individual. A **population** contains one species in an area. A **community** contains interacting populations. An **ecosystem** adds the non-living environment. A **biome** is a broad climatic region; the **biosphere** includes all ecosystems.

Habitat means the place an organism lives. Niche means its ecological role and relationships—how it uses resources, tolerates conditions, and interacts with other organisms.

| Interaction | Effect | Standard example |
|---|---|---|
| Mutualism | both benefit | pollinator and flowering plant |
| Commensalism | one benefits; other largely unaffected | epiphyte using a tree for support |
| Parasitism | parasite benefits; host is harmed | tapeworm and host |
| Predation | predator kills and consumes prey | tiger and deer |
| Competition | both lose access to limited resources | plants competing for light |

**Worked example**

All deer of one species inside a reserve form a **population**. Deer, grasses, predators, and microbes together form a **community**; add soil, water, and climate to describe the ecosystem.

**Self-check**

Is a habitat the same as a niche?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. Habitat is where an organism lives; niche is its role and way of using resources within the ecological system.
</details>

## 2. Follow Energy and Matter Through Ecosystems

Producers capture energy, usually through photosynthesis. Primary consumers eat producers; higher consumers feed at later trophic levels. Decomposers break down organic matter and return nutrients to cycles.

Energy flow is one-way and diminishes at successive trophic levels because organisms use energy and lose much of it as heat. The “10% law” is an approximate teaching rule for trophic transfer, not an exact value for every ecosystem. Matter, unlike energy, is recycled through water, carbon, nitrogen, and other biogeochemical cycles.

Nitrogen-fixing microbes convert atmospheric nitrogen into biologically usable compounds. Nitrification and denitrification are different microbial steps; denitrification returns nitrogen gas to the atmosphere.

**Worked example**

In grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, grass is the producer and grasshopper the primary consumer. The snake occupies the highest listed trophic level, but the chain does not imply that snakes eat only frogs.

**Self-check**

Why are food webs more realistic than single food chains?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Most organisms have multiple food sources and predators. A food web represents those interconnected pathways.
</details>

## 3. Understand Biodiversity and Conservation

Biodiversity operates at **genetic, species, and ecosystem** levels. Endemic species occur naturally in a restricted region. A keystone species has a disproportionately large ecological effect relative to its abundance. An invasive alien species can spread outside its native range and disrupt local ecosystems.

| Conservation mode | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| In situ | protect species in natural habitat | national park, sanctuary, community reserve |
| Ex situ | protect outside natural habitat | zoo, botanical garden, seed/gene bank |
| Habitat restoration | repair ecological processes and native cover | wetland or grassland restoration |
| Corridor conservation | maintain movement between habitat patches | wildlife corridor |

National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, tiger reserves, wetlands, and reserved forests are not interchangeable labels. Read the exact designation. Counts and newly notified sites change, so verify them for the question date.

**Self-check**

Is a seed bank in situ or ex situ conservation?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Ex situ. Genetic material is conserved outside the species’ natural habitat.
</details>

## 4. Trace Pollution from Source to Effect

Primary pollutants are emitted directly; secondary pollutants form through atmospheric reactions. Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides contribute to acid deposition. Ground-level ozone is a secondary pollutant and component of photochemical smog; stratospheric ozone is protective.

| Term | Meaning | Exam clue |
|---|---|---|
| BOD | oxygen used by microbes to decompose biodegradable matter | high value often signals organic pollution |
| COD | oxygen equivalent needed to chemically oxidise pollutants | usually broader/faster lab measure |
| Eutrophication | nutrient enrichment and excessive algal growth | fertiliser/sewage runoff |
| Bioaccumulation | pollutant builds within one organism over time | persistent chemical in tissues |
| Biomagnification | concentration rises at higher trophic levels | top predator has greatest burden |

Noise is measured in decibels. Fine particulate matter can penetrate deep into the respiratory system. Thermal pollution changes water temperature and can reduce dissolved oxygen or disturb aquatic life.

**Worked example**

If a persistent pesticide becomes more concentrated from plankton to fish to a fish-eating bird, the pattern is **biomagnification**. Accumulation within one fish over time is bioaccumulation.

**Self-check**

What process links nutrient-rich runoff to oxygen depletion in a lake?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Eutrophication: nutrients stimulate algal growth; decomposition then consumes dissolved oxygen and can cause hypoxia.
</details>

## 5. Separate Climate Change from Ozone Depletion

The natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth habitable. Human activities increase concentrations of gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide, strengthening radiative forcing and driving climate change. Weather is short-term atmospheric condition; climate is the long-term statistical pattern.

Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation. Chlorofluorocarbons and related ozone-depleting substances release reactive halogens that destroy ozone. This is a different mechanism from greenhouse warming, even though some substances affect both.

Mitigation reduces emissions or increases sinks. Adaptation reduces harm from effects already occurring or expected—for example, drought-resistant crops, heat-action plans, and flood-resilient infrastructure.

**Self-check**

Is planting mangroves for coastal protection mitigation or adaptation?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

It can contribute to both: carbon storage supports mitigation, while protection from storm surge and erosion supports adaptation. The question’s stated purpose decides the answer.
</details>

## 6. Match Conventions to Their Subjects

| Agreement or body | Core subject | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Ramsar Convention | wetlands of international importance | not a climate treaty |
| CITES | international trade in listed wild species | not habitat designation by itself |
| Convention on Biological Diversity | conservation, sustainable use, benefit sharing | not ozone depletion |
| UNFCCC | framework for international climate action | umbrella framework |
| Kyoto Protocol | emission commitments, especially for developed parties | not ozone treaty |
| Paris Agreement | climate action through nationally determined contributions | current pledges are live facts |
| Montreal Protocol | ozone-depleting substances | not primarily carbon dioxide |

Treaty names are stable, but membership, conference decisions, targets, and national commitments can change. Use an official dated source for “latest COP,” “current target,” or “newest site” questions.

**Self-check**

Which protocol is directly associated with phasing out ozone-depleting substances?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Montreal Protocol. Kyoto and Paris concern climate change.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

Which level includes living communities and their physical environment?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

An ecosystem.
</details>

### Question 2

What happens to usable energy as it moves up trophic levels?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

It decreases because organisms use energy for life processes and lose much of it as heat.
</details>

### Question 3

Which conservation method keeps a species in its natural habitat?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

In situ conservation.
</details>

### Question 4

Which pollutant pattern increases concentration toward the top of a food chain?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Biomagnification.
</details>

### Question 5

Which convention is associated with wetlands of international importance?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The Ramsar Convention.
</details>
