*Preserve meaning first: lock the sentence type and tense, identify roles, then rebuild the required structure and check every reference.*

Transformation changes grammatical form, not the event, participants, time, or speaker’s intention. Direct items have a clear object or reporting pattern. Mark and return when a missing agent, ambiguous pronoun, or reporting context prevents a unique transformation.

## 1. Rebuild Active and Passive Around the Object

In the active voice, the subject performs the action. In the passive, the receiver becomes grammatical subject:

$$\text{active object}+\text{form of be}+\text{past participle}$$

The passive keeps the original tense through the auxiliary. Use a *by*-phrase only when the agent is important or needed. “Someone stole my bicycle” naturally becomes “My bicycle was stolen”; *by someone* adds little.

Only a verb with an object can normally form a personal passive. “The child slept” has no object to promote. Keep particles in phrasal or prepositional verbs: “They looked after the child” → “The child was looked after.”

**Worked example**

“The engineers are testing the bridge” becomes “The bridge **is being tested** by the engineers.” Present continuous becomes *is/am/are being + past participle*.

**Self-check**

Change to passive: “The storm damaged several houses.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“Several houses **were damaged by the storm**.” The plural new subject takes *were*, and *damaged* is the past participle.
</details>

## 2. Lock the Auxiliary for Tense, Modal, and Sentence Type

| Active form | Passive core | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simple present/past | is/are or was/were + V3 | writes → is written |
| continuous | is/are/was/were being + V3 | is writing → is being written |
| perfect | has/have/had been + V3 | has written → has been written |
| modal | modal + be + V3 | must write → must be written |

Keep questions as questions: “Did they approve the plan?” → “Was the plan approved?” With a *wh*-word, retain it: “Why did they cancel the match?” → “Why was the match cancelled?”

An imperative may become *Let + object + be + V3* in formal transformation exercises, or a more natural passive instruction: “Keep the door closed.” Meaning matters more than forcing one template.

**Worked example**

“You must submit the form by Friday” becomes “The form **must be submitted** by Friday.” The modal remains unchanged and is followed by *be + submitted*.

**Self-check**

Change to passive: “Has the clerk verified the documents?”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“**Have the documents been verified** by the clerk?” The plural subject takes *have*, followed by *been verified*.
</details>

## 3. Preserve Both Objects, Complements, and Agency

With two objects, either may sometimes become subject:

“The teacher gave **us** **a worksheet**.”

- “We were given a worksheet by the teacher.”
- “A worksheet was given to us by the teacher.”

Keep an object complement: “They elected Mira captain” → “Mira was elected captain.” Do not insert *as* unless the original construction and standard usage require it.

Passive voice can be useful when the agent is unknown, obvious, or less important: “The samples were stored at 4°C.” It is not automatically better or worse than active voice.

**Worked example**

“People believe that the painting is authentic” can become “It is believed that the painting is authentic” or “The painting is believed to be authentic.” Both preserve the general, unspecified agent.

**Self-check**

Can “The guests arrived at noon” be changed to a normal passive sentence?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

No. *Arrived* is intransitive here and has no object to promote. Do not create an artificial passive.
</details>

## 4. Report Statements with the Right Time Relationship

Direct speech gives the speaker’s words; indirect speech reports their content. Remove quotation marks, choose a suitable reporting verb, adjust pronouns, and use *that* where natural.

If the reporting verb is present or future, tense normally remains: “She says, ‘I am ready’” → “She says that she is ready.” With a past reporting verb, conventional examination backshift often applies:

| Direct speech | Conventional indirect form |
|---|---|
| present simple | past simple |
| present continuous | past continuous |
| present perfect | past perfect |
| will / can / may | would / could / might |

Do not backshift a continuing general truth merely to obey a table: “The teacher said, ‘Water boils at 100°C at standard pressure’” → “The teacher said that water boils at 100°C at standard pressure.” Context can also keep a past tense when the time remains explicit; choose the option that preserves the intended timeline.

**Worked example**

Ravi said, “I have completed the report.” → Ravi said that he **had completed** the report.

**Self-check**

Change to indirect speech: Meera said, “I will call you tomorrow.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

With a later reporting viewpoint: “Meera said that she **would call me the next day**.” The listener represented by *you* must be known; if it was not the reporter, replace *me* with the correct person.
</details>

## 5. Anchor Pronouns and Time Words to the Reporting Situation

Change pronouns by ownership, not by a mechanical person table:

- first person refers to the original speaker;
- second person refers to the original listener;
- third person usually keeps its referent.

Time and place words change only when the reporting viewpoint changes. Common shifts include *now → then, today → that day, yesterday → the previous day, tomorrow → the next day,* and *here → there*. If the report is made in the same place or on the same day, a shift may be unnecessary.

**Worked example**

Anita said to Kabir, “I will send you my notes.” → Anita told Kabir that **she** would send **him her** notes.

**Self-check**

Report from a later day: The guide said, “We are leaving this place today.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“The guide said that **they were leaving that place that day**.” *They* refers to the guide’s group; a singular *we* cannot simply become *he* without losing the group.
</details>

## 6. Convert Questions, Commands, Requests, and Exclamations

For a yes/no question, use *asked + if/whether + statement order*: “She asked, ‘Are you ready?’” → “She asked whether I was ready.” Do not retain question inversion in the reported clause.

For a *wh*-question, retain the *wh*-word and use statement order: “He asked, ‘Where do you live?’” → “He asked where I lived.”

For commands and requests, choose a reporting verb that preserves force, then use object + *(not) to*:

- “Sit down,” the officer said to us. → The officer **told** us to sit down.
- “Please wait,” she said to me. → She **asked** me to wait.
- “Do not touch it,” he said. → He **warned/told** us not to touch it, depending context.

Exclamations should become natural statements that retain emotion. “What a beautiful view!” she said → She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful view. For *Let us …*, a suggestion may become *suggested that we should …*.

**Self-check**

Change to indirect speech: The doctor said to Arun, “Do not skip the medicine.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“The doctor **advised Arun not to skip the medicine**.” *Advised* fits the professional recommendation; *not to skip* preserves the negative command.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice — Transformation Mastery

### Question 1

Change to passive: “They will announce the results tomorrow.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“The results **will be announced** tomorrow.” The unspecified agent can be omitted.
</details>

### Question 2

Change to active: “The final design was approved by the committee.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“The committee **approved the final design**.” The simple past is preserved.
</details>

### Question 3

Change to indirect speech: Priya said, “I am reading the instructions.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“Priya said that she **was reading** the instructions.” The past reporting verb triggers the conventional present-continuous backshift.
</details>

### Question 4

Change to indirect speech: He asked me, “Why did you leave early?”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“He asked me **why I had left early**.” Keep *why*, use statement order, and apply the conventional past-to-past-perfect shift.
</details>

### Question 5

Change to indirect speech: The chairperson said, “Let us postpone the vote.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

“The chairperson **suggested that they should postpone the vote**.” If the reporter belongs to the group, *we* may be retained by context.
</details>
