1. **Core Definition** - An idiom is a fixed group of words whose combined meaning differs from the literal meaning of its individual words. Example: "break the ice" = to initiate conversation, not to literally break ice.
2. **Why They Appear in SSC CGL** - Direct meaning recall (e.g., choose the correct meaning of the given idiom) and contextual usage (e.g., which phrase best fits the blank). Both test memory of figurative meanings.
3. **Recognition Cues** - If a phrase sounds unnatural when taken literally or has an unusual pairing (e.g., "spill the beans"), it is likely an idiom. Common patterns: body parts (head, hand, foot), animals (cat, dog, horse), colours (red, blue, green), numbers (one, two, seven), actions (bite, kick, pull).
4. **Memory Strategy** - Group idioms by the key word (e.g., all "head" idioms: head over heels, lose one's head, head start). Use short stories or mnemonics for each group.
5. **Literal Trap** - The most common wrong answer is a literal interpretation of the idiom. Example: "hit the sack" does not mean to hit a sack; it means to go to bed.
6. **Context Fit** - Some questions provide a sentence with a blank and four idiom options. You must pick the idiom whose figurative meaning matches the sentence tone and logic.
7. **Tone Sensitivity** - Idioms carry formal/informal or positive/negative shades. "Let the cat out of the bag" is informal negative (reveal a secret carelessly); "disclose" is neutral.
8. **Similar Pairs** - Two idioms may share a word but have opposite meanings: "cut corners" (do something poorly to save time) vs. "cut to the chase" (get to the point directly).
9. **Preposition-Fixed Phrases** - Some idioms depend on a specific preposition: "in the nick of time" (just in time), not "at the nick of time". These are non-negotiable.
10. **Speed Integration** - In the 15-minute English section, allocate about 1.5-2 minutes for the 4-5 idiom questions. Use recall tables and the flowchart to decide fast.

### Decision Rules for Direct Meaning Questions

1. **If the idiom is familiar** (you know the meaning) - answer immediately.
2. **If the idiom is unfamiliar** but has a keyword (e.g., "crocodile tears") - use keyword association (crocodile = fake) to guess meaning.
3. **If the idiom appears in two forms** (e.g., "take with a pinch of salt" vs. "take with a grain of salt") - SSC generally uses "pinch of salt" for Indian English. Both mean to be skeptical. Accept either.
4. **If there is a literal distractor** - cross it out first. The correct answer will be figurative.
5. **If two options are close** - look for the exact wording of the idiom in standard reference. "Beat around the bush" is correct; "beat about the bush" is also correct but less common in SSC. Choose the one given in the question.
6. **If you are confused between two idioms with similar words** - recall which one matches the situation. Example: "cut corners" vs. "cut to the chase" - one is negative (poor quality), other is positive (get to the point).
7. **Time-budget**: 15-20 seconds per idiom question. If stuck, mark for review and move on. Do not spend more than 30 seconds.

### Contextual Usage - Step-by-Step Algorithm

1. Read the sentence carefully. Identify the tone (positive/negative, formal/informal).
2. Mentally predict the required meaning. Example: The team __ and won the match. Required meaning: performed exceptionally well.
3. Scan the idiom options; reject any that are opposite in tone.
4. Among remaining, pick the one that fits the blank grammatically (tense, article placement).
5. Verify: does the idiom mean what you predicted? If yes, select.
6. Speed target: 25-30 seconds per contextual question.

## 200/200 Drill

For a 200/200 target, you must achieve 100% accuracy on the 4-5 idiom questions in SSC CGL Tier-I English. Use the following micro-drills.

### Timed Micro-Drills

1. **Speed Recall Flash (10 minutes)** - Write down all idioms you can recall from the 8 groups (body, animal, colour, number, action, preposition, formal, abstract). Aim for 60+ idioms in 2 minutes. Repeat daily.
2. **Literal Rejection (5 minutes)** - Take 10 idioms and write their literal meaning, then overwrite with figurative. Example: "Hit the sack" -> literal: hit a sack -> figurative: go to bed. Speed: 30 seconds per pair.
3. **Tone Sorting (5 minutes)** - Given 10 idioms, classify each as positive, negative, or neutral. Time: 3 minutes. Correct if wrong.
4. **Contextual Sprint (10 minutes)** - 10 sentences with blanks and 4 options each. Solve in 5 minutes (30 seconds per question). Aim for 10/10.
5. **Preposition Fix (5 minutes)** - 10 idioms with missing prepositions. Fill in the blank. Example: "___ the blink of an eye" -> "in". Time: 2 minutes.
6. **Final Mock Drill (15 minutes)** - Take 20 idiom questions (15 direct, 5 contextual) from your source book. Attempt in 10 minutes. Check answers and note errors.

### Repair Rules

- **If you chose a literal meaning**: Re-read the idiom and force yourself to say its figurative meaning aloud five times.
- **If you mixed up two similar idioms**: Write them side by side with a clear distinguishing story.
- **If you missed a tone mismatch**: Practice tone classification by reading a sentence and stating if it needs positive or negative idiom.
- **If you used a wrong preposition**: Drill the correct preposition with a chant: "In the nick, in the sack, in hot water, in a nutshell."
- **If you ran out of time**: In the real exam, if you are short on time, skip the idiom question and come back. Never guess wildly; the penalty makes blind guessing expensive. With these drills, idiom questions should be answered within the 15-minute English section without stealing time from cloze or comprehension.

Final standard: Idioms are pure memory plus context discipline. Revise the recall table every third day, retest every missed idiom, and keep the direct-recall speed under 20 seconds per question.
