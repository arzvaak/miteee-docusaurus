*Read for topic and tone, predict the blank’s job, test grammar and collocation, then re-read the completed passage.*

A cloze test is a connected passage, not a row of independent synonym questions. The correct word must fit the sentence grammatically **and** preserve the passage’s logic, tone, and natural phrasing. Direct blanks have a strong grammatical or collocational lock. Mark and return when two words remain plausible until a later sentence resolves the contrast.

## 1. Read the Passage Before Solving a Blank

First identify the subject, the writer’s attitude, and the movement of ideas. A passage may explain a process, contrast two positions, describe a problem and response, or argue for a conclusion. This global frame prevents a locally possible word from damaging the paragraph.

Read one sentence beyond the blank whenever possible. Pronouns, repeated keywords, and connectors often reveal whether the missing idea is positive, negative, causal, or contrasting.

**Worked example**

“The new irrigation system initially seemed expensive. Over time, however, it proved ___ because it reduced both water and electricity use.” The second sentence reverses the initial concern and supplies benefits, so **economical** fits better than *costly* or *ornamental*.

**Self-check**

Choose the best word: “The forecast warned of heavy rain. ___, the organisers moved the event indoors.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Therefore** fits because the move was a consequence of the warning. *Nevertheless* would signal contrast, which the context does not support.
</details>

## 2. Predict the Grammatical Slot

Before reading options, ask what form the blank requires.

| Signal around blank | Likely requirement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| article + blank + noun | adjective | an **effective** policy |
| modal + blank | base verb | can **improve** |
| preposition + blank | noun or -ing form | by **reducing** waste |
| linking verb + blank | adjective or noun complement | seems **reasonable** |

Check number, tense, agreement, and derivation. Words from the same family are not interchangeable: *economy* is a noun, *economic* often relates to the economy, and *economical* means avoiding waste or expense.

**Worked example**

“The committee reached a ___ decision after reviewing the evidence.” After *a* and before *decision*, an adjective is needed. **Unanimous** fits; *unanimously* does not.

**Self-check**

Fill the blank: “Regular maintenance can ___ the life of the machine.” Choose between *extension, extensive,* and *extend*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Use **extend**. A modal verb (*can*) takes the base form of a verb.
</details>

## 3. Respect Collocation and Fixed Complements

Collocation is the tendency of words to occur naturally together: we *draw a conclusion, pose a threat, meet a requirement,* and *take responsibility*. A grammatical combination may still be unidiomatic.

Learn a word with its common partners and prepositions. For example, one is *capable of* doing something, *responsible for* a task, and *committed to* a goal. After *committed to*, *to* is a preposition, so a noun or -ing form follows.

Do not choose by frequency alone. “Strong tea” and “powerful engine” are conventional; swapping the adjectives sounds unnatural even though both mean roughly “having force”.

**Worked example**

“The findings ___ doubt on the earlier claim.” The established collocation is **cast doubt on**, not *make doubt on*.

**Self-check**

Choose the natural completion: “The new rules will come ___ effect next month.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

The fixed expression is **come into effect**.
</details>

## 4. Follow Cohesion Across Sentences

Connectors state relationships:

| Relationship | Common signals | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| addition | moreover, also, in addition | Is another supporting point added? |
| contrast | however, whereas, although | Does the direction reverse? |
| cause/result | because, therefore, consequently | Which idea causes the other? |
| example/restatement | for example, in other words | Is the idea illustrated or clarified? |

Track reference words such as *this, such, they,* and *former*. Their antecedents can constrain a blank. Repetition through related words also builds a lexical chain: *rainfall → reservoir → water supply*.

**Worked example**

“Remote work reduces commuting. ___, it can make informal collaboration harder.” The second idea limits the first, so **however** is appropriate.

**Self-check**

Choose the connector: “The sample was small; ___, the result should be interpreted cautiously.”

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Therefore** or **consequently** expresses the result. A contrast connector would misstate the logic.
</details>

## 5. Match Meaning, Connotation, and Register

Near-synonyms differ in implication and setting. *Slim* is often approving, while *skinny* can be critical. *Childlike* can suggest innocence; *childish* usually suggests immaturity. The surrounding attitude determines which shade is intended.

Register is the level and type of language. A formal report may *request clarification*; a casual conversation may *ask what happened*. Avoid slang in a neutral institutional passage unless the narrator’s voice supports it.

Check semantic role as well as dictionary definition. A medicine may *alleviate* pain (make it less severe), while a policy may *prevent* harm (stop it from occurring). Those verbs are not interchangeable in every context.

**Worked example**

“The witness gave a ___ account, avoiding both exaggeration and emotional language.” **Factual** fits the neutral context; *dramatic* conflicts with the stated restraint.

**Self-check**

Choose the better word: “The minister ___ the proposal during the formal briefing”: *explained* or *chatted about*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Explained** matches the formal register. *Chatted about* is conversational and suggests an informal exchange.
</details>

## 6. Use Elimination, Then Re-read the Whole Passage

For each blank:

1. Predict meaning and part of speech.
2. Eliminate options that break grammar.
3. Test collocation and required preposition.
4. Test paragraph logic, tone, and reference.
5. Insert the choice and read through the next sentence.

Roots and affixes can narrow unfamiliar options but do not prove meaning. *In-* can mean “not” in *inaccurate*, but it can mean “into” in *infiltrate*. Confirm with context.

When two words survive, compare the complete phrases, not isolated definitions. A plausible word may create needless repetition or an unintended intensity. Do not change an earlier answer merely because a later option is unfamiliar; change it only when passage evidence conflicts.

**Self-check**

Why is *rapid* unsuitable in “The panel reached a ___ agreement after months of difficult negotiation” if the options include *tentative*?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

*Rapid* conflicts with “after months of difficult negotiation” and describes speed, not the agreement’s degree of certainty. **Tentative** can describe an agreement that remains provisional.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice — Cloze Mastery

Read the passage before answering:

“Urban trees do more than improve a street’s appearance. Their shade can (1) ___ surface temperatures, while their roots help soil (2) ___ rainwater. These benefits are significant; (3) ___, trees require suitable space and regular care. A planting programme that ignores local conditions may (4) ___ to deliver its promised gains. Cities should therefore select species (5) ___, considering climate, soil, and available space.”

### Question 1

Choose for blank 1: *reduce, decline, weaken,* or *descend*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Reduce** is transitive and takes *surface temperatures* as its object. *Decline* and *descend* are normally intransitive here.
</details>

### Question 2

Choose for blank 2: *absorb, assume, consume,* or *admit*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Absorb** fits the physical process of soil taking in rainwater.
</details>

### Question 3

Choose for blank 3: *however, similarly, therefore,* or *for example*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**However** introduces the qualification that benefits come with requirements.
</details>

### Question 4

Choose for blank 4: *fail, refuse, deny,* or *avoid*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Fail to deliver** is the natural construction and means not achieving the promised gains.
</details>

### Question 5

Choose for blank 5: *carefully, carelessly, scarcely,* or *loudly*.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Carefully** matches the following list of factors that require deliberate consideration.
</details>

Mastery means every choice can be defended by grammar plus passage evidence. Continue with [Vocabulary and Cloze focused practice](/exams/ssc-cgl/practice/vocabulary-cloze).
