*Equalise units, convert ratio parts to one common scale, and use products only after identifying direct or inverse variation.*

A ratio compares like quantities. It has no unit after both terms are expressed in the same unit. Most direct questions reduce to **parts × common factor**; mark and return if several ratios must be linked and the common term has not yet been equalised.

## 1. Convert a Ratio into Actual Parts

If $A:B=a:b$, write $A=ak$ and $B=bk$ for some common factor $k$.

If total $T$ is divided in ratio $a:b$, then

$$A=\frac{a}{a+b}T,\qquad B=\frac{b}{a+b}T.$$

Always make units identical before simplifying: $2\text{ m}:50\text{ cm}=200:50=4:1$.

**Worked example**

Divide ₹840 in the ratio $3:4$. Seven parts equal 840, so one part is 120. The shares are ₹360 and ₹480.

**Self-check**

The ratio of boys to girls is $5:3$ and the class has 64 students. How many girls are there?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Eight parts equal 64, so one part is 8. Girls $=3\times8=24$.
</details>

## 2. Use Cross Products for a True Proportion

If

$$a:b=c:d,$$

then

$$ad=bc,$$

provided the ratios are defined. The fourth proportional to $a,b,c$ is $d=bc/a$. The mean proportional between positive $a$ and $b$ is $\sqrt{ab}$.

| Form | Relation |
|---|---|
| $a:b=c:d$ | $ad=bc$ |
| fourth proportional $d$ | $d=bc/a$ |
| mean proportional $x$ | $x^2=ab$ |
| continued proportion | $a:b=b:c$ |

**Worked example**

Find $x$ if $6:15=14:x$. Cross multiplication gives $6x=210$, so $x=35$.

**Self-check**

Find the mean proportional between 9 and 16.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

$\sqrt{9\times16}=12$.
</details>

## 3. Join Ratios by Equalising the Common Term

To combine $A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$, make B equal. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12:

$$A:B=8:12,\qquad B:C=12:15,$$

so $A:B:C=8:12:15$.

A compound ratio multiplies corresponding terms. The compound ratio of $a:b$ and $c:d$ is $ac:bd$. Do not multiply when the question asks to join ratios sharing a named quantity; equalise the common term instead.

**Self-check**

If $P:Q=3:5$ and $Q:R=10:7$, find $P:Q:R$.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Scale the first ratio by 2: $P:Q=6:10$. Therefore $P:Q:R=6:10:7$.
</details>

## 4. Distinguish Direct and Inverse Variation

In direct variation, $y\propto x$, so $y/x$ is constant. In inverse variation, $y\propto1/x$, so $xy$ is constant.

| Situation | Usual relation | Required condition |
|---|---|---|
| cost and quantity | direct | same unit price |
| distance and time | direct | same speed |
| workers and days | inverse | same work and equal efficiency |
| speed and time | inverse | same distance |
| pipe rate and fill time | inverse | same tank volume |

**Worked example**

Eight equal workers finish a job in 15 days. Twelve equally efficient workers take $8\times15/12=10$ days, assuming the same work and daily hours.

**Self-check**

A car covers a fixed distance at speed ratio $3:4$. What is the time ratio?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Time is inversely proportional to speed, so the time ratio is $4:3$.
</details>

## 5. Weight Partnership by Capital and Time

Profit is divided in the ratio of capital-time products:

$$\text{share ratio}=C_1t_1:C_2t_2:\cdots$$

Use the same time unit. If capital changes, split that partner’s contribution into intervals and add $C\times t$ for each interval. Salary paid for managing the business, if stated, is handled before dividing the remaining profit.

**Worked example**

A invests ₹20,000 for 12 months and B invests ₹30,000 for 8 months. Their capital-time products are both 240,000, so profit is shared $1:1$.

**Self-check**

A invests ₹40,000 for a year; B invests ₹60,000 for six months. Find their profit ratio.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

$40{,}000\times12:60{,}000\times6=480:360=4:3$.
</details>

## 6. Turn Ages and Changed Ratios into Equations

Age differences remain constant; ratios do not. If present ages are $3k$ and $5k$, then after $n$ years they are $3k+n$ and $5k+n$.

For a quantity added to both terms,

$$\frac{A+x}{B+x}=\frac{m}{n}.$$

Do not “add $x$ to the ratio numbers” unless those numbers are the actual quantities. Direct items yield one linear equation. Mark and return when multiple past/future conditions create two unknowns that have not been reduced.

**Worked example**

Present ages are in ratio $3:5$. After 6 years the ratio is $2:3$. Then $(3k+6)/(5k+6)=2/3$, so $9k+18=10k+12$ and $k=6$. Ages are 18 and 30.

**Self-check**

Two numbers are in ratio $4:7$. Adding 6 to each makes the ratio $5:8$. Find the numbers.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Let them be $4k,7k$. Then $(4k+6)/(7k+6)=5/8$, so $32k+48=35k+30$, giving $k=6$. The numbers are 24 and 42.
</details>

## 7. Mixed Practice and Mastery

### Question 1

Simplify $1.5\text{ kg}:750\text{ g}$.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

$1500:750=2:1$.
</details>

### Question 2

If $x:y=7:9$ and $x+y=128$, find $x$.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Sixteen parts equal 128, so one part is 8 and $x=56$.
</details>

### Question 3

Find the fourth proportional to 4, 6, and 14.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

$d=6\times14/4=21$.
</details>

### Question 4

If 15 machines make a batch in 8 hours at equal rates, how long will 20 machines take?

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Machine-hours stay constant: $15\times8=20t$, so $t=6$ hours.
</details>

### Question 5

A and B share ₹9,100 in ratio $6:7$. Find B’s share.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Thirteen parts equal ₹9,100, so one part is ₹700. B’s share is $7\times700=4,900$, or ₹4,900.
</details>
