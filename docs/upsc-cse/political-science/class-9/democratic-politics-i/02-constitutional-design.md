## Topic Notes

### 1. What is a Constitution?

A constitution is the supreme law of a country that defines the composition and powers of the government, guarantees rights to citizens, and articulates the fundamental values of the nation. It acts as a framework for political and social life, ensuring rule of law, separation of powers, and protecting against tyranny. Constitutions are essential to prevent arbitrary exercise of power and to promote social and economic change.

### 2. The South African Constitution: A Case Study

South Africa’s journey to democracy involved the struggle against apartheid, a system of racial segregation and discrimination. Led by the African National Congress (ANC) under Nelson Mandela, the movement sought a democratic and open society. After decades of sacrifice, negotiations resulted in a new constitution in 1996 that emphasized human dignity, equality, and freedom. The constitution was drafted by an elected Constituent Assembly and included provisions for a bill of rights, an independent judiciary, and minority protection. It stands as a model of conflict resolution through constitutional means.

### 3. Making of the Indian Constitution

The Indian Constitution was drafted by the Constituent Assembly formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946). The Assembly had 299 members elected indirectly by provincial legislative assemblies, representing different communities and regions. After Partition, membership reduced to 284. Major committees like the Drafting Committee chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar prepared the final document. The Assembly deliberated for nearly three years, adopting the Constitution on 26 November 1949, with it coming into force on 26 January 1950. The experience of the Government of India Act 1935, colonial laws, and international models influenced the text.

### 4. Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly functioned as both a constitution-making body and a provisional parliament. It was composed of representatives from various political parties, including Congress, Muslim League (before Partition), and others. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the permanent President; Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha served as the provisional President initially. The Assembly conducted open sessions, incorporated over 2,000 amendments, and ensured extensive debate. Its inclusive nature gave the Constitution legitimacy as a 'national document' reflecting India’s diversity and aspirations.

### 5. Guiding Values of the Indian Constitution

The Preamble declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic, and seeks to secure justice (social, economic, political), liberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship), equality (status and opportunity), and fraternity (assuring individual dignity and national unity). These values form the 'philosophy' of the Constitution and guide judicial interpretation. They reflect a commitment to social transformation and the creation of a just society. The Constitution also provides for amendment procedures (Article 368) to adapt to changing needs, balancing rigidity and flexibility.

### Mains Prompt 1: Constituent Assembly, Government of India Act 1935

> It would have been difficult for the Constituent Assembly to complete its historic task of drafting the Constitution for Independent India in just three years but for the experience gained with the Government of India Act, 1935. Discuss.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2015 (GS Paper 1)  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** Introduction: Acknowledge the complexity and time constraint; the GoI Act 1935 served as a foundational template. Body: (1) Provisions borrowed: parliamentary system, federal structure, office of governor, emergency powers, judicial setup, central and state lists. (2) Modifications made: removal of imperial control, introduction of universal adult franchise, fundamental rights, directive principles. (3) Role in ensuring continuity and reducing delay: familiarity of legislators with the Act’s provisions allowed quicker consensus. (4) Criticisms: concerns about colonial legacy, but the Assembly consciously adapted provisions to democratic and egalitarian ideals. Conclusion: The GoI Act acted as a ‘working draft’ that streamlined the process, but the Assembly transformed it into a vibrant document reflecting India’s aspirations.
