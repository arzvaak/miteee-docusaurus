## Topic Notes

### Elections and Democracy

Elections are the visible symbol of democracy. In a large democracy, direct participation by all citizens is impractical, making representative democracy necessary. Elections are the method to choose representatives. However, not all elections are democratic; non-democratic regimes also hold elections. A democratic election must be free and fair, with basic rules often enshrined in the constitution. These rules cover: who can vote, who can contest, who supervises elections, how voters choose representatives, and how votes are counted.

### Election System in India

India follows the First Past the Post (FPTP) system for direct elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies. Under FPTP, the country is divided into single-member constituencies; the candidate with the most votes wins, regardless of whether they secure a majority. This can lead to a party winning a disproportionate share of seats compared to its vote share (e.g., Congress won 80% seats with 48% votes in 1984).

### Proportional Representation (PR)

In PR systems, seats are allocated to parties in proportion to their vote share. Israel treats the whole country as one constituency. A variant, the Single Transferable Vote (STV) system, is used in India for indirect elections to Rajya Sabha, Legislative Councils, and for President and Vice-President. In STV, voters rank candidates; a candidate must secure a quota of votes to win. While PR ensures better representation of smaller parties, it may lead to unstable coalitions. India chose FPTP for its simplicity, accountability of a specific representative to a locality, and ability to produce clear majority governments.

### Reservation of Constituencies

To ensure representation of SCs and STs, India adopted reserved constituencies instead of separate electorates. In reserved constituencies, all voters vote, but only candidates from the specified community can contest. The Delimitation Commission decides which constituencies are reserved based on proportion of SC/ST population. As of 2019, 84 Lok Sabha seats are reserved for SCs and 47 for STs. Recently, the Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) provides one-third reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies.

### Free and Fair Elections

The Constitution guarantees universal adult franchise: all citizens above 18 (earlier 21 before 1989) can vote. It also provides for an independent Election Commission to supervise, direct, and control elections. The Commission is independent of the executive and ensures free and fair polls. The Constitution also lays down the right to contest elections, subject to qualifications (age, citizenship, etc.) as prescribed by law.

### Electoral Reforms

The system has faced criticism: money power, muscle power, criminalization of politics, and lack of voter choices. Reforms suggested include state funding of elections, stronger anti-defection law, and ensuring inner-party democracy. The Election Commission has recommended reforms like making false declarations in affidavits a ground for disqualification. The concept of 'One Nation One Election' (simultaneous elections) is also discussed to reduce election-related disruptions and expenses, though it raises constitutional and federal concerns.

### Mains Prompt 1: Mains 2022

> Discuss the procedures to decide the disputes arising out of the election of a Member of the Parliament or State Legislature under The Representation of the People Act, 1951. What are the grounds on which the election of any returned candidate may be declared void? What remedy is available to the aggrieved party against the decision? Refer to the case laws.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2022, General Studies II, Question 11  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Briefly mention that the RP Act, 1951 provides a special machinery for adjudication of election disputes. 2. Procedure: - Election petition filed before High Court by any candidate or voter with leave. - Time limit: 45 days from date of election. - Trial by single judge; summary procedure. - HC may declare election void or uphold it. 3. Grounds for declaring election void (Section 100): - Improper rejection or acceptance of nomination. - Corrupt practices (bribery, undue influence, etc.) committed by returned candidate or with his consent. - Non-compliance with Constitution or RP Act provisions affecting result. - Candidate not qualified or disqualified. 4. Remedy: Appeal to Supreme Court under Section 116A of RP Act (order of HC is not final). SC's decision is binding. 5. Case laws: - Shiv Kirpal Singh vs. V.V. Giri (1970): Corrupt practices must be proved beyond reasonable doubt. - Mohinder Singh Gill vs. Chief Election Commissioner (1978): Election Commission's plenary powers. - K. Venkatachalam vs. A. Swamickan (1999): Disqualification under Article 191. 6. Conclusion: Summarize the robust mechanism ensuring purity of elections.

### Mains Prompt 2: Mains 2024

> Examine the need for electoral reforms as suggested by various committees with particular reference to “one nation – one election” principle.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2024, General Studies II, Question 1  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: State that electoral reforms are essential to strengthen democracy, reduce corruption, and ensure free and fair elections. 2. Need for reforms: - Criminalization of politics. - Money power and opaque funding. - Frequent elections causing policy paralysis and administrative burden. - Low voter turnout and urban apathy. - Misuse of official machinery. 3. Committee recommendations: - Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990): State funding, model code, EVMs. - N. N. 'One Nation One Election' (ONOE): - Concept: holding Lok Sabha and all State Assembly elections together. - Advantages: reduces election expenditure, minimizes disruption to governance, improves policy consistency. - Challenges: constitutional amendments needed (Articles 83, 85, 172, 174, 356), federal concerns, logistics, synchronization of terms, by-elections. - Ram Nath Kovind panel (2024) recommendations: two-phase approach, required amendments. 5. Conclusion: While ONOE can bring efficiency, its feasibility requires political consensus and careful structural changes; other reforms are equally critical.
