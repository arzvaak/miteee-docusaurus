## Topic Notes

### Definition and Types of Executive

The executive is the organ of government responsible for implementing laws and policies. It includes the political executive (elected representatives like Prime Minister and ministers) and the permanent executive (civil servants). Types: 1) Presidential (e.g., USA): President is both head of state and government, independent of legislature. 2) Parliamentary (e.g., India): Prime Minister is head of government, with a nominal head of state; executive is responsible to legislature. 3) Semi-presidential (e.g., France, Sri Lanka): both President and Prime Minister have significant powers, President directly elected.

### Parliamentary Executive in India: Constitutional Framework

India adopted the parliamentary system due to colonial experience and to ensure executive accountability. The President is the formal head, while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers constitute the real executive. Article 53(1) vests executive power in the President, exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers. Article 74(1) makes the advice binding; President may seek reconsideration but must accept reconsidered advice.

### The President of India

Indirectly elected by MPs and MLAs through proportional representation with single transferable vote. Term: 5 years. Impeachment for violation of Constitution (special majority). Powers: executive, legislative, judicial, emergency. Normally acts on aid and advice. Discretionary powers: a) send back advice for reconsideration (though bound by reconsidered advice), b) pocket veto on bills (no time limit for assent), c) appoint Prime Minister in hung parliament. 42nd and 44th Amendments reinforced binding nature of advice.

### Vice-President

Elected by MPs (both Houses) for 5 years. Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha. Acts as President during vacancy.

### Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

Prime Minister is head of government, appointed by President; must enjoy confidence of Lok Sabha. PM allocates portfolios; ranks: cabinet minister, minister of state (independent charge), minister of state, deputy minister. CoM does not exceed 15% of total Lok Sabha members (91st Amendment, 2003). Minimum educational qualification not required. Collective responsibility: entire CoM is collectively responsible to Lok Sabha; if confidence lost, ministry resigns. PM communicates all decisions to President.

### Collective Responsibility and Size of Council

Collective responsibility implies that all ministers are jointly responsible for government decisions. A vote of no confidence against one minister leads to resignation of the entire CoM. The 91st Amendment (2003) capped the size to prevent jumbo cabinets and excessive expenditure.

### Permanent Executive vs Political Executive

Political executive (ministers) are elected representatives who make policy decisions. Permanent executive (civil servants) implement policies and provide continuity. Ideal relationship: mutual respect, civil servants' accountability to ministers.

### Mains Prompt 1: Mains 2018 (Law Paper I)

> 'With the adoption of Parliamentary form of government, the vesting clause under Article 53(1) remains to a great extent meaningless, as real executive power lies in the Ministry.' Critically examine the above statement in the context of the status and position of the President of India under the Indian Constitution. Also answer, if the President of India does not accept the advice of the Prime Minister, what consequences would follow.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2018 Law Paper I, Q10  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Briefly explain Article 53(1) – executive power of the Union vests in the President. But under Article 74(1), President must act on aid and advice of Council of Ministers. 2. Parliamentary vs Presidential system: India chose parliamentary system for accountability; President is nominal head, real power with PM and Council of Ministers. 3. Evolution of President's position: 42nd and 44th amendments made advice binding; before, there was ambiguity. 4. Critical analysis: Statement is largely true as President acts on advice. However, President is not a mere rubber stamp – has discretionary powers: sending advice for reconsideration, pocket veto, appointing PM in hung parliament, seeking information under Article 78. 5. President's role during crises: asserts authority when governments are unstable (e.g., post-1989 coalition era). 6. Consequences if President refuses PM's advice: Constitutional crisis – President would violate Article 74(1) as interpreted by the Supreme Court. Could lead to impeachment for violation of Constitution. Government machinery may stall. In practice, President may try to dissuade PM, but if PM insists, President should comply. 7. Conclusion: While real power lies with the Ministry, the President is not 'meaningless' – remains the supreme commander, guardian of Constitution, with crucial situational discretion.

### Mains Prompt 2: Mains 2019 (GS Paper IV)

> What are the consequences of 'politicization of bureaucracy'? Discuss.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2019 General Studies Paper IV, Q10  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Define political executive (elected ministers) and permanent executive (bureaucracy). Ideal relationship: mutual respect, bureaucracy provides impartial advice, implements policies. 2. Politicization of bureaucracy: Political interference in transfers, postings, promotions; pressure to follow partisan agenda; erosion of neutrality and merit-based functioning. 3. Consequences: a) Weakening of institutional integrity – bureaucracy loses objectivity, becomes sycophantic. b) Policy implementation suffers – short-term political gains over long-term public interest. c) Decline in morale and efficiency – upright officers marginalized, demoralized. d) Corruption and nepotism – quid pro quo arrangements. e) Threat to rule of law and democratic accountability – bureaucrats become shields for politicians. f) Public trust erodes – governance seen as biased. 4. Examples: Instances where officers follow illegal orders or are penalized for honesty. 5. Measures: Need for fixed tenures, transparent transfer policies, code of ethics, stronger Whistleblower protection. 6. Conclusion: Politicization undermines the constitutional design of a neutral bureaucracy, harming good governance. Reforms essential to restore the line between political and permanent executive.
