## Topic Notes

### Why Do We Need a Constitution?

A constitution provides basic rules for coordination and assurance in a diverse society. It enables members to live together peacefully by establishing publicly known and enforceable rules, ensuring that others will also follow them.

### Specification of Decision-Making Powers

A constitution determines who has the authority to make laws and policies. In a democracy, the people elect representatives, and the constitution specifies the structure of the legislature and executive, e.g., Parliament in India.

### Limitations on the Powers of Government

Constitutions limit government power by granting fundamental rights to citizens, such as freedom of speech, protection from arbitrary arrest, etc. These rights are inviolable except under specific circumstances like emergency.

### Aspirations and Goals of a Society

Modern constitutions, like India's, enable the government to take positive measures for social welfare. The Directive Principles of State Policy embody the society's aspirations for justice, equality, and dignity.

### Fundamental Identity of a People

A constitution expresses the collective identity of a nation by defining citizenship, setting shared values, and providing a moral framework. India's constitution, for instance, does not base citizenship on ethnicity.

### The Authority of a Constitution

A constitution's effectiveness depends on its mode of promulgation (made by credible leaders), its substantive provisions (giving everyone reason to abide), and balanced institutional design (fragmentation of power, checks and balances, flexibility).

### Making of the Indian Constitution

The Constituent Assembly, elected indirectly under the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946), held its first sitting on 9 December 1946. Seats were allocated to provinces and princely states, with members chosen by provincial assemblies. The final Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949.

### Mains Prompt 1: 2015, Mains, GS I

> It would have been difficult for the Constituent Assembly to complete its historic task of drafting the Constitution for Independent India in just three years but for the experience gained with the Government of India Act, 1935. Discuss.

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2015, General Studies Paper 1, Question 5  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Acknowledge that the GOI Act 1935 provided a foundational framework. 
2. Borrowed features: Federal scheme, provincial autonomy, division of legislative powers, office of governor, emergency provisions, judiciary structure, civil services, etc. 
3. Familiarity and critique: Indian leaders were well-acquainted with the Act's strengths and weaknesses, allowing them to adapt and improve. 
4. Time-saving: Many administrative details were drawn from the 1935 Act, reducing the need for fresh deliberation. 
5. Conclusion: While the Constitution was an original document reflecting Indian aspirations, the 1935 Act significantly expedited the drafting process.
