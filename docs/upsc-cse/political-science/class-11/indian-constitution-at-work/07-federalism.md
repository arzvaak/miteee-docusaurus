## Topic Notes

### What is Federalism?

Federalism is an institutional mechanism to accommodate two sets of polities—regional and national—each autonomous in its sphere. Key ideas: dual government (Union and States), written constitution as supreme, division of powers (defence, currency for centre; police, agriculture for states), independent judiciary to resolve disputes. Identities and loyalties are dual (e.g., Gujarati and Indian). Actual working depends on political culture, trust, and party dynamics. Examples: break-up of USSR, Nigeria’s ethnic conflicts; West Indies federation dissolution illustrates need for balanced central authority.

### Federalism in the Indian Constitution

Article 1 describes India as a ‘Union of States’. Constitution creates Union and State governments with demarcated powers. Three lists: Union List (defence, banking, railways, currency), State List (police, agriculture, public health, land), Concurrent List (education, forests, trade unions). Residuary powers (subjects not in any list) vest with the Union. The division reflects a strong centre: revenue sources centralised, States dependent on Union grants.

### Federalism with a Strong Central Government

Constitution designed with a unitary bias to ensure unity and socio-economic development. Provisions: (1) Parliament can alter state boundaries, names, etc. (Article 3). (2) Emergency provisions centralise power; Parliament can legislate on State subjects. (3) Financial dominance: Union controls major revenue sources, States rely on grants; Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) supervised state resources. (4) Governor’s power to reserve bills for President’s assent, recommend President’s Rule (Article 356). (5) Executive power of Union supersedes States (Article 257). (6) All-India Services (IAS, IPS) controlled by Centre. (7) Articles 33-34 allow Parliament to protect armed forces during martial law.

### Conflicts in India’s Federal System

Tensions arise from centralised powers and State expectations. In 1950s-60s, Congress dominance smoothed relations. From mid-1960s, non-Congress State governments demanded greater autonomy. Post-1990s, coalition politics gave States more say, maturing federalism. Demands for autonomy focus on: (i) reallocation of powers to States, (ii) independent revenue sources (financial autonomy), (iii) control over administration, (iv) cultural/linguistic issues (e.g., anti-Hindi agitations). Governor’s role and President’s Rule (Article 356) remain contentious; Sarkaria Commission (1983) recommended non-partisan appointments. Supreme Court asserted judicial review over imposition of President’s Rule. Demands for new states on linguistic lines led to reorganisation (1956 onwards), creating states like Gujarat, Maharashtra, and northeastern states.

### Division of Powers – Detailed Lists

Union List (100 subjects): Defence, Atomic Energy, Foreign Affairs, Railways, Banking, Currency, etc. State List (61 subjects): Police, Agriculture, Prisons, Public Health, Land, Liquor, Livestock, etc. Concurrent List (52 subjects): Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Adoption, etc. Residuary powers: Cyber laws, etc., with Union. Note: Economic and financial powers are skewed toward Centre.

## Mains Answer Practice

### Mains Prompt 1: Mains 2014 | GS Paper 2

> Though the federal principle is dominant in our Constitution and that principle is one of its basic features, but it is equally true that federalism under the Indian Constitution leans in favour of a strong Centre, a feature that militates against the concept of strong federalism. Discuss. (12½ marks)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2014 General Studies Paper 2  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** Introduction: Recognise federalism as a basic feature yet with a unitary bias. Body: (1) Provisions creating a strong Centre: emergency powers, Article 3 (Parliamentary supremacy over state boundaries), financial centralisation, Governor’s role, Article 356, all-India services, residuary powers. (2) Rationale: need for national unity post-Partition, socio-economic planning, maintaining integrity. (3) How this militates against strong federalism: States’ fiscal dependence, limited legislative autonomy, misuse of President’s Rule. (4) Counterpoints: cooperative federalism mechanisms (Inter-State Council, GST Council), judicial safeguards (Bommai case), and evolving coalition politics. Conclusion: The Constitution strikes a balance; the challenge is to uphold cooperative federalism and prevent excessive centralisation.

### Mains Prompt 2: Mains 2018 | Political Science & IR Paper I

> Implementation of GST and NEET is a major challenge to Indian federalism. Comment. (10 marks)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2018 Political Science and IR Paper I  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** Introduction: GST and NEET as centralising measures. Body: (1) GST: subsumed state taxes, restricted fiscal autonomy; compensating cess, GST Council as a cooperative federal body; dependence on Union for compensation dents federal spirit. (2) NEET: uniform entrance test curtails state autonomy in medical education; violates federal diversity; some states sought exemption. (3) How these challenge federalism: erosion of state legislative competence, financial overreach. (4) Mitigating factors: GST Council provides a forum for consensus; NEET ensures merit and uniformity; they exemplify collaborative federalism if states are consulted. Conclusion: While posing challenges, these reforms can strengthen federalism if mechanisms like the GST Council are truly cooperative and states’ concerns are adequately addressed.

### Mains Prompt 3: Mains 2025 | GS Paper II

> Examine the evolving pattern of Centre-State financial relations in the context of planned development in India. How far have recent reforms impacted the fiscal federalism in India? (15 marks)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2025 General Studies Paper II  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** Introduction: Financial relations are the core of federalism. Planned development led to centralised fiscal control. Body: (1) Pre-1991 phase: Planning Commission, Finance Commission awards, dominant role of Union in resource allocation. (2) Evolving pattern: liberalisation reduced direct central control; 73rd/74th amendments added local bodies; later Finance Commissions increased devolution to states. (3) Recent reforms: NITI Aayog replaced Planning Commission, promoting cooperative federalism; GST subsumed state indirect taxes, affecting fiscal autonomy; 15th Finance Commission’s recommendations for vertical devolution (41%). (4) Impact: mixed — states have more untied funds but face GST compensation issues; increased conditional grants via centrally sponsored schemes; concerns about horizontal imbalances remain. Conclusion: Fiscal federalism has become more nuanced, but greater fiscal autonomy and institutional mechanisms like the GST Council are needed for balance.

### Mains Prompt 4: Mains 2024 | GS Paper II

> What changes has the Union Government recently introduced in the domain of Centre-State relations? Suggest measures to be adopted to build the trust between the Centre and the States and for strengthening federalism. (15 marks)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2024 General Studies Paper II  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** Introduction: Centre-State relations have been dynamic, with recent reforms reshaping federal balance. Body: (1) Recent changes: NITI Aayog replacing Planning Commission (2015); GST implementation (2017) and GST Council; increased use of digital governance and centrally sponsored schemes (e.g., PM-Kisan); use of Article 356 and controversies around Governor’s role; recommendations of 15th Finance Commission. (2) Impact: mixed — enhanced coordination but also perceptions of centralisation. (3) Trust-building measures: revive Inter-State Council as a regular dialogue platform; non-partisan gubernatorial appointments; greater financial devolution and autonomy; reduce conditional grants; implement Sarkaria/Punchhi Commission recommendations; involve states in policy formulation. Conclusion: Strengthening federalism requires genuine cooperative federalism, political will, and institutional mechanisms that respect state autonomy while maintaining national unity.
