## Master Summary

The chapter explores power sharing as a crucial element of democracy through case studies of Belgium and Sri Lanka. Belgium successfully accommodated ethnic differences through constitutional amendments ensuring equal representation and community government, while Sri Lanka's majoritarian policies led to civil war. Power sharing is desirable for prudential (avoiding conflict) and moral (democratic spirit) reasons. It takes various forms: horizontal (separation of powers), vertical (federalism), social group representation, and political party coalitions.

## Topic Notes

### Belgium and Sri Lanka: Divergent Paths

Belgium:
- Ethnic composition: Dutch-speaking (59%) in Flanders, French-speaking (40%) in Wallonia, 1% German-speaking. Brussels: 80% French, 20% Dutch.
- Tensions 1950s-60s due to French minority being richer, Dutch majority resented.
- Accommodation: 1970-1993, four constitutional amendments. Key features: equal number of Dutch and French ministers in central govt; special laws require majority from each linguistic group; state govts with many powers; Brussels separate govt with equal representation; community govt for language groups.
- Success: avoided civil strife, Brussels became EU headquarters.

Sri Lanka:
- Ethnic composition: Sinhala-speakers (74%), Tamil-speakers (18%): Sri Lankan Tamils (13%), Indian Tamils (5%). Sinhalese mostly Buddhist, Tamils Hindu/Muslim.
- Majoritarian measures post-1948: 1956 Sinhala Only Act, preferential university/job policies, state protection of Buddhism. Led to Tamil alienation, demands for autonomy denied, civil war erupted in 1980s, ended 2009.
- Consequences: civil war, loss of lives, economic setback.

Contrast: Belgium's power sharing prevented division; Sri Lanka's majoritarianism caused conflict.

### Why Power Sharing is Desirable

Two sets of reasons:
1. Prudential: Reduces conflict, ensures stability, prevents tyranny of majority which can backfire.
2. Moral: Inherent to democracy; people have a right to be consulted; legitimate government is where citizens have a stake.
Example: Panchayati Raj strengthens democracy and reduces corruption (prudential).

### Forms of Power Sharing

1. Horizontal: Among organs (legislature, executive, judiciary) at same level. Separation of powers, checks and balances.
2. Vertical: Among levels (federal/unitary). Central/Union and State/Provincial governments. Indian federalism as example.
3. Social Groups: Reserved constituencies, community govt (Belgium). Ensures minority representation.
4. Political Parties & Pressure Groups: Competition, coalition governments, interest group influence. Ensures power doesn't remain in one hand.
