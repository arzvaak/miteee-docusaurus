This chapter examines the role, functions, and challenges of political parties in a democracy, with a focus on India. It also discusses the need for reforms.

## Answer Skeletons

### 150-word: "Political parties are indispensable to representative democracy."

1. Define a political party as a group that contests elections and seeks power on agreed policies.
2. Explain aggregation: parties organise diverse social interests into programmes.
3. Explain governance: they select candidates, form governments, and coordinate law-making.
4. Explain accountability: opposition parties criticise government and offer alternatives.
5. Add limitation: money power, dynastic politics, and weak internal democracy reduce credibility.
6. Conclude: democracy needs parties, but parties themselves must become more democratic.

### 250-word: "Regional parties have strengthened Indian federalism."

1. Opening: link regional parties to India's social, linguistic, and territorial diversity.
2. Representation: bring state-specific demands into national politics.
3. Coalition effect: force consultation and bargaining in Union governance.
4. Policy effect: influence resource allocation, language, identity, and welfare priorities.
5. Democratic deepening: allow local leadership beyond national party structures.
6. Limitation: can promote narrow demands, unstable coalitions, or personality-led politics.
7. Conclusion: regional parties are a federal asset when channelled through constitutional negotiation.

### 10-marker: "Internal democracy is central to party reform."

Start with the contradiction: parties run democracy but may not be democratic internally. Mention concentration of power, absence of regular internal elections, poor membership transparency, dynastic succession, and opaque candidate selection. Then give reforms: verifiable membership registers, periodic organisational elections, transparent candidate criteria, audited finances, and public pressure. End by balancing regulation with autonomy: reform should democratise parties without making the state control political association.

## Topic Notes

### Meaning and Need for Political Parties

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power. They agree on policies for collective good and reflect fundamental political divisions. Parties are necessary to organise diverse opinions, form responsible government, and provide a link between citizens and government. Without parties, independent candidates cannot offer coherent policies, and governance becomes fragmented.

### Functions of Political Parties

1. Contest elections and select candidates.  2. Put forward different policies and programmes.  3. Make laws (party discipline in legislature).  4. Form and run governments.  5. Play the role of opposition and criticise government.  6. Shape public opinion and raise issues.  7. Provide access to government machinery and welfare schemes.

### Party Systems: One, Two, and Multi-party

One-party system: Only one party allowed (e.g., China). Not democratic.  Two-party system: Power alternates between two main parties (e.g., USA, UK). Multi-party system: Several parties compete, often forming coalition governments (e.g., India). India’s multi-party system reflects social and geographical diversity. Party system evolves from a country’s special circumstances.

### National and State Parties in India

Parties register with the Election Commission. Recognised parties get a unique election symbol. A national party must secure at least 6% of votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in four states, plus win at least four Lok Sabha seats. As of 2023, six national parties: Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), Communist Party of India-Marxist (CPI-M), Indian National Congress (INC), National People's Party (NPP). State parties (regional parties) have grown in number and strength, leading to coalition politics and strengthening federalism.

### Challenges to Political Parties

1. Lack of internal democracy: Power concentrated in a few leaders, no regular elections, no transparency.  2. Dynastic succession: Top positions controlled by family members, unfair to ordinary workers.  3. Money and muscle power: Rich candidates and criminals dominate, influencing policies.  4. Lack of meaningful choice: Ideological differences among parties have narrowed, reducing voter options.

### Reforming Political Parties

The chapter suggests measures such as: law to regulate internal affairs of parties; making it compulsory to maintain membership registers and hold internal elections; state funding of elections to reduce money power; and citizens' pressure for transparency. The Supreme Court has also issued directions on candidate disclosures.

## Prelims Traps

- Do not treat NCERT examples as exhaustive lists.
- Watch qualifiers such as only, always, never, directly, indirectly, constitutional, statutory, formal, and informal.
- A one-party system is not democratic merely because elections are held.
- A two-party system does not mean only two parties legally exist; it means two major parties dominate government formation.
- A multi-party system does not automatically mean instability; it can represent diversity and produce coalitions.
- Recognised party status is not the same as constitutional status; it is granted through election law and Election Commission rules.
- Regional parties can strengthen federalism; they are not automatically anti-national or separatist.

## Answer Framework Bank

- Necessity frame: parties aggregate interests, provide choices, form governments, and maintain accountability.
- Party-system frame: one-party, two-party, and multi-party systems differ by competition pattern, not just number of registered parties.
- Federalism frame: regional parties bring state-level diversity into national decision-making.
- Reform frame: match each challenge to a reform: internal elections, transparent membership, candidate disclosure, funding transparency, and citizen pressure.
- Balance frame: parties need regulation for fairness but autonomy for political freedom.

### Mains Prompt 1: National vs Regional Parties on Centralisation vs Autonomy

> "While the national political parties in India favour centralisation, the regional parties are in favour of State autonomy." Comment. (250 words)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2022 GS-II  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Briefly define national and regional parties and their contrasting stances.  2. National parties favouring centralisation: Examples – BJP’s uniform civil code, abrogation of Art 370; INC’s historical centralised planning; need for strong Centre for national integration, security, and uniform development.  3. Regional parties advocating state autonomy: Examples – DMK, TMC, TRS push for more financial devolution, control over state subjects, resistance to central legislations like farm laws; reflect linguistic and cultural identities.  4. Impact on federalism: Healthy tension strengthens federal balance; coalition era forces accommodation (e.g., GST Council, inter-state councils).  5. Conclusion: Both perspectives are essential – national parties ensure unity while regional parties protect diversity; the constitutional framework provides checks and balances.

Strengthening federalism: Greater voice for states in national decision-making; examples like Tamil Nadu’s influence on language policy, Telangana’s creation.  4. ### Mains Prompt 3: Political Personalities vs Political Parties

> Political personalities are more significant than political parties in India. Comment. (150 words)

**Source:** UPSC CSE Mains 2018 Political Science and IR Paper I  

**Outline hint (AI-generated study aid):** 1. Introduction: Context of personality cult in Indian politics; historical examples like Nehru, Indira Gandhi.  2. Arguments for significance of personalities: Charismatic leaders attract votes; many parties are built around individuals (e.g., BSP – Mayawati, SP – Mulayam Singh); voters often identify with leaders rather than party ideology.  3. Drawbacks: Weakens party institutions, promotes dynastic politics, reduces issue-based politics.  4. Counters: Strong ideological parties (BJP, Left) maintain cadre-based structure; rise of social media and policy debates is reducing personality dominance; institutional frameworks matter.  5. Conclusion: While personalities continue to hold sway, democratic maturity requires institutionalisation of parties for sustainability.
